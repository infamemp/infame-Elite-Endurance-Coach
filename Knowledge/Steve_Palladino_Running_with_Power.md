## 1. Philosophy & Core Principles

### The Power Paradigm

Palladino's foundational philosophy is the **Power Paradigm**: all training plans are based on tested CP/FTP, not arbitrary goal times. All intensities scale from the individual's current fitness. The cycle is: Test → Train at appropriate intensity → Retest → Adjust targets → Train → repeat (upward spiral). Goal-time plans risk the athlete getting in "over their head," leading to high injury and dropout rates. Palladino's plans eliminate that risk by anchoring every workout to measured fitness.

**Key phrase:** "Let the fitness come to you."

### Core Coaching Principles

1. **Anchor training to current fitness, not aspiration.** CP/FTP-based entry reduces injury and overtraining risk compared to goal-time plans. Training targets rise only when CP rises after retesting.

2. **Quality over quantity.** Training load is duration × relative intensity (RSS), not volume alone. A lower-volume plan with well-targeted intensity can produce equivalent or superior metabolic stress compared to a higher-mileage plan. "If all you do is run long and slow, it's going to teach you to run long and slow."

3. **Consistency is the growth engine.** Real improvement comes from staying healthy and moving plan-to-plan consistently. Short-term missed workouts are acceptable if they prevent the big setback (injury, illness) that costs weeks of fitness. "Consistency in the long view — not day-to-day. Avoid the big setback."

4. **Keep easy days easy.** Easy runs must stay at or below the 80% FTP ceiling. Going harder on easy days impairs recovery for the next hard workout and offers negligible fitness return. (See Section 2, Zone 1C.)

5. **If in doubt, leave it out.** No single workout makes or breaks a training plan or race. If on the verge of illness or a niggle, skip the workout. Never try to make it up — sandwiching two hard sessions creates problems. Do not rearrange the schedule.

6. **Do not chase CTL numbers.** "Your goal is to stay within a healthy ramp rate, not to hit a target CTL." The pattern and ramp rate matter more than any absolute number.

7. **Ramp rate is the guardrail.** Keep week-over-week CTL increase between 1–5 for most runners. Above 7 is unsustainable and invites injury/illness. Adjust for athlete age, injury history, and training composition. (See Section 4 for detailed ramp-rate guidelines.)

8. **Look at trends, not single data points.** One day dipping into cautionary RSB after a long run is not alarming. Examine the trend over days and weeks.

9. **Use multiple metrics, not one.** Combine extrinsic measures (RSS, CTL, RSB, ramp rate) with intrinsic measures (RPE, sleep quality, resting HR, general energy, aches/pains). "Don't be a one-metric person." If RPE and RSS diverge, investigate.

10. **Training zones are descriptive, not prescriptive** (quoting Dr. Andy Coggan) — except during tapers, where RSB and ramp rate become nearly prescriptive.

11. **Execute intervals bottom-to-top.** Start at the lower end of the prescribed range and progress upward only if power comes easily. Top-to-bottom fading is suboptimal. "I'd rather see bottom-to-top execution than fading from top to bottom."

12. **Avoid back-to-back higher-stress days.** Always keep at least one EZ day or off day between interval, tempo, and long-run stressors.

13. **Stay in the target range.** Going over usually worsens the fatigue-to-benefit ratio. Hit the target, check the box, move on. "Don't be a workout wonder."

14. **Treat weather as a CP problem first.** Heat, humidity, and altitude change CP, and load interpretation only stays valid if CP is updated or converted. (See Section 12.)

15. **Running overuse injuries are caused by training errors.** Biomechanical factors, anatomy, and shoes only change the threshold at which injuries occur — the cause is the training error (excessive ramp rate, insufficient recovery, overpowering easy days). "If someone wasn't training and they could have the same anatomic factors — they're not getting injured."

16. **Run training is primary.** Strength, mobility, core, and plyometrics are supplemental and should never detract from run training. (See Section 13.)

17. **Post-processing is the biggest benefit of running with power.** "The biggest benefit of running with power in my mind is not what you can do in real time. It's what you do in post-processing." The power-duration curve is the single most valuable output.

18. **Change the stimulus to continue progressing.** After a half-marathon plan, switch to 5K or 10K work. Changed stimulus + higher resulting CP → re-enter the next race plan at a higher level.

19. **Progress by duration OR power, not both simultaneously.** For endurance-limited runners: progress duration at a given power. For speed-limited runners: progress power at a given duration.

20. **Three questions every week:** (a) Was the workout executed as prescribed? (b) Is training load in the target area? (c) Did the athlete progress (power-duration curve, KPIs)?

### Zone 1 — Easy Running

**Coaching notes on Zone 1:**
- Most athletes conduct easy aerobic running in Zone 1C (75–80%).
- Palladino cautions athletes to avoid drifting into Zone 2 on easy days because recovery takes longer, yet the pace is rarely fast enough to improve FTP.
- He will allow "overly eager athletes" to drift up to 81–82% but reminds them he prefers ≤80%.
- Consistent overshoot of 80% indicates either an aggressive athlete or an underestimated FTP.
- Easy run prescription from training plans: "average power between 75–80% of FTP" with instruction "avoid overpowering this run."
- Long run easy portions: 80–83% of FTP.
- If an athlete goes 65–70% on an easy day because that is what easy felt like, no correction is needed.
- If the athlete consistently hits 83%, investigate whether FTP is set too low.

### Zone 2 — Endurance / Long Run

**Coaching notes on Zone 2:**
- Palladino generally discourages standard-duration easy aerobic running in Zone 2.
- Long runs / overdistance runs often average in Zone 2, especially if faster running segments or end-of-run pace cutdowns are employed.

### Zone 3 — Threshold Stimulus

**Coaching notes on Zone 3:**
- Zone 3 encompasses target powers used to stimulate FTP improvement.
- Zone 3A corresponds roughly to marathon power (88%) through approaching half-marathon power (94–95%).
- Zone 3B covers half-marathon power (~94–95%) up to FTP (100%), which for most runners corresponds to 10K–15K race power.
- In old pace terminology: marathon/half-marathon paced running forms the basement of Zone 3; 10K pace (FTP duration) forms the ceiling.
- Tempo prescription from plans: "1x 10:00 @ 94–96% of FTP" progressing to "1x 15:00 @ 94–96% of FTP."

### Zone 4 — Supra-Threshold

- Supra-FTP work executed in intervals of approximately 5–10 minutes.
- Shoulder zone between FTP and maximal aerobic (VO2max) power.
- If used properly, can provide stimulus for adaptations in both FTP and VO2max.
- Roughly equivalent to slower-than-5K to 10K race effort.

### Zone 5 — Maximal Aerobic Power Stimulus

- If executed properly, provides optimal stimulus for power at VO2max.
- Interval duration: 2–5 minutes.
- **Important note:** Palladino's Zone 5 range (106–116%) is significantly lower than Jim Vance's (116–128%). Palladino found through coaching that the Vance range is too high to sustain in a normal VO2max workout.

### Zone 6 — Anaerobic Power Stimulus

- Work is generally executed anaerobically to increasing degrees depending on target power and duration.
- Designed to provide stimulus for anaerobic capacity.
- Shorter intervals (:30–1:30) with long recoveries optimize the ability to hit target powers.

### Zone 7 — Sprint / Maximal Power

- Does not require a power meter to execute — go hard / maximal, duration measured in seconds.
- Directed toward developing top-end power.
- **Analysis target for hill sprints:** Palladino looks for >200% of FTP execution on 15-second max efforts and tracks peak power, not average power.

### Zone System Caveats (Palladino's Own Words)

1. "Training zones were intended to be descriptive, not prescriptive" (quoting Dr. Andy Coggan).
2. If FTP estimate is bad, zones are bad — GIGO (garbage in, garbage out).
3. Sustained running generally occurs in Zones 1–3A. Demarcations are most accurate relative to FTP in these zones. The most important demarcations for Palladino's prescriptions: upper Zone 1C and lower Zone 3A.
4. Above Zone 3, zones are NOT absolute and can vary between individuals based on their VO2max relative to FTP/CP, anaerobic capacity, and top-end power. Zones 4–7 are "fuzzy between athletes."
5. For Zones 4–6, power target is NOT the sole determinant of stimulus. Work interval duration, rest interval duration, rest interval power, and number of reps all matter.
6. For Zones 4–7, prescriptions should reference the individual athlete's power-duration curve, which is superior to zone labels alone.

### Zone Distribution Rules for General Prep

- **Early general prep:** Entirely easy aerobic running at ≤80% FTP/CP (Zone 1 only).
- **Middle general prep:** ~85% of total duration in Zone 1 + low Zone 2 (mostly Zone 1), ~15% in Zone 3 (mostly 94–101% FTP/CP), <1% in Zone 7 via short hill sprints. Zones 4–6 are intentionally avoided ("grey zone") — any exposure is spill-over only.
- **Late general prep:** Same backbone as middle general prep, with modest introductory HIIT exposure to Zones 4–5, typically 1 session per week.

## 3. Critical Power & W' (RWC) Model

### Functional Threshold Power (FTP)

- **Definition:** The highest power a runner can maintain in a quasi-steady state without fatiguing, where the sustainable duration typically falls in the ~30–70 minute range depending on the individual. For most sub-elite runners, TTE at FTP is often 35–50 minutes.
- FTP correlates with power at Maximal Lactate Steady State (MLSS) and lactate threshold (LT), representing similar physiologic states.
- The maximal duration one can hold FTP = Time to Exhaustion (TTE).
- FTP normalized to weight (W/kg) correlates to race speed from 5K to marathon.
- FTP is one of the most foundational metrics in running performance.
- Speed = Power × Running Effectiveness (RE).

### Critical Power (CP)

- CP is the threshold at which the dominant type of fatigue an athlete experiences changes. Analogous to anaerobic/lactate threshold measured through power output.
- CP distinguishes between the heavy and severe training domains.
- FTP and CP are "very similar, although it is not unusual for CP to be estimated slightly higher than FTP. For practical purposes, they are often used interchangeably."
- Better to consider CP as an approximate estimate of FTP based on the Monod model, rather than treating them as identical.
- Stryd Powercenter CP reflects a sustainable duration of approximately 50–60 minutes.
- Palladino uses 50 minutes as the reference duration for the Riegel formula.

### Common FTP/CP Errors

1. **FTP ≠ average power over 60 minutes.** TTE at FTP ranges 30–75 minutes; in most sub-elite runners it is 35–50 min.
2. **CP ≠ FTP exactly.** They are often close but not always equal.
3. **Running FTP ≠ cycling FTP.** Palladino explicitly warns against this assumption.
4. **95% of 20-minute power ≠ FTP.** One of the most common mis-applied assumptions. Population-based fixed percentages of any duration are unreliable.

### Reserve Work Capacity (RWC / W' / FRC)

- RWC is synonymous with W', FRC (Functional Reserve Capacity), and AWC (Anaerobic Work Capacity).
- It is the total amount of work (in kJ) one can do above FTP/CP during steady-state exercise before fatigue failure occurs.
- "Think of RWC like a battery — it has a certain capacity before it completely drains." When running above CP, the athlete digs into RWC; if it drops to zero, the athlete must slow dramatically or stop.
- FRC = WKO4 PDC metric, similar to anaerobic capacity: the extent (in kJ) one can exert above mFTP before fatiguing.
- Caution: if FRC value increases, mFTP estimation typically drops.
- Palladino uses RWC data from CP tests to override FRC settings in WKO for more accurate workout stimulus analysis. He analyzes how much RWC is depleted during interval sessions to assess whether the desired stimulus was achieved.
- If an athlete has higher-than-typical RWC, Palladino may prescribe higher interval targets than his standard 100–102% of FTP.

### How CP/W' Drive Training Prescription

- All training intensities are prescribed as percentages of CP/FTP.
- CP anchors the entire intensity system; RWC/FRC is used to evaluate whether high-end sessions produced the intended stimulus and to individualize super-threshold work.
- Progression: when CP rises after retesting, training targets adjust — same relative intensity, but higher absolute watts.
- Race power planning uses CP as an input to the Riegel formula. (See Section 8.)
- Some super-threshold targets are individualized from both CP and RWC.

### FTP/CP Estimation Frameworks

Palladino uses three main frameworks:

1. **WKO4 multi-parameter power-duration curve model (mFTP):** Preferred method. Samples multiple points along the runner's MMP curve from Pmax through at least the TTE region. Accuracy depends on having maximal or near-maximal efforts across many durations, not merely one favored range. Palladino recommends a 90-day MMP representation. Key metrics: Pmax, FRC, mFTP, TTE, SEE, CV.

2. **Two-parameter or three-parameter Critical Power models:** Stryd PowerCenter uses two parameters, intended to produce CP reflecting ~50–60 minutes of sustainable power. Greater duration separation between the short and long maximal tests improves reliability, provided both are truly maximal; the short test should be at least ~1 minute and the long can extend to ~20 minutes. When the longer test is under 12 minutes, overestimation risk rises.

3. **One-parameter modified Riegel model from a race or maximal effort:** Best input is a maximal race from 5K to half marathon. (See Section 9 for full details.)

### Practical CP Rules

- Use the same protocol and model consistently over time.
- Require maximal efforts each time.
- Keep taper/rest/venue/environment similar.
- Do not cherry-pick best efforts from separate dates.
- Re-estimate after roughly 6 weeks ± 3 weeks as fitness changes.
- Same footpod, same foot, same location each time.
- When multiple models agree (Stryd AutoCP, CP testing, Golden Cheetah, WKO mFTP), trust the result — estimates typically fall within ~1–2% of each other with properly tested data. The variance is on the left side (FRC/RWC/W' modeling), not the CP/FTP estimate.

## 4. Running Power Metrics & Variables

### Running Stress Score (RSS)

- **Definition:** Duration × relative intensity (average power / CP), with an exponential weighting applied to relative intensity. When running above CP, RSS accumulates disproportionately more weight.
- **Use:** Overall metabolic stress from a given run. Reflects the metabolic cost, not the fatigue or composition directly. Used for day-level stress comparison, weekly build patterns, taper design, and comparison against chronic training load.
- **Benchmarks:** A day around 200% of CTL is a significant day; ≤100% of CTL is usually a recovery day.
- RSS, rTSS, and TSS all produce strikingly similar models of training load.

### Chronic Training Load (CTL) — "42-Day Average"

- **Definition:** Exponentially weighted average with a 42-day time constant. Effectively analyzes 2–3 months of data, but the most recent 6 weeks are most influential.
- Palladino does NOT look at the absolute CTL number ("I don't look at the specific chronic training load"). He looks at patterns, ramp rates, and relative changes.
- Takes 2–3 months of data before CTL is truly valid for new users.
- Adaptations take ~6 weeks to accumulate deeply.

### Acute Training Load (ATL) — "7-Day Average"

- 7-day time constant exponentially weighted average.
- Not directly displayed in Stryd Power Center but computed in the background.

### Running Stress Balance (RSB)

- **Formula:** CTL − ATL
- **Interpretation:** Negative RSB = acute load > chronic load (building phase). Positive RSB = chronic load > acute load (fresh/tapered).
- RSB takes 2–3 months before it is accurate for new users.
- Palladino cautions: one day dipping into cautionary after a long run is not cause for alarm — look at the trend, ramp rate, and how you feel.

**Stryd Power Center RSB Zones:**

| RSB Zone | Range | Description |
|----------|-------|-------------|
| Not Applicable | > +25 | Insufficient acute load |
| Performance | +5 to +25 | Optimal freshness for race performance |
| Maintenance | +5 to −10 | Maintaining fitness |
| Productive | −10 to −25 | Productive building zone |
| Cautionary | −25 to −40 | Ramp rate probably > 7; consider backing off |
| Overreaching | < −40 to −45 | Overreaching; risk of injury/illness |

### ATL:CTL Ratio

- Productive range: 0.8 to 1.3.
- Functions similarly to RSB/TSB as a training load balance indicator.

### Ramp Rate (Week-over-Week CTL Change)

- **Calculation:** CTL this week minus CTL one week ago.

| Ramp Rate (CTL Δ/week) | Status | Notes |
|------------------------|--------|-------|
| < 0 | Negative ramp | Acceptable only in taper (short duration) |
| 0 | Flat | Maintenance |
| 1–2 | Conservative | Ideal for older/injury-prone runners |
| 3–5 | Moderate | Younger/elite athletes can sustain up to 5 |
| 5–7 | Cautionary | Not sustainable long-term; investigate |
| > 7 | Dangerous | Back off immediately; injury/illness risk |

- Ramp rate tolerance depends on runner age, injury history, and training composition. Easy aerobic blocks tolerate higher ramp rates (up to 5); blocks with VO2max intervals + tempo + long runs may only tolerate 2–3.
- Palladino recommends checking ramp rate weekly (Sunday or Monday).

### Running Effectiveness (RE)

- **Formula:** Speed ÷ Power (speed in m/s, power in W/kg). Higher RE = more effective conversion of external power into speed. Palladino uses RE rather than the inverse (ECOR = Power ÷ Speed) because "bigger is better."
- RE is NOT the same as running economy or efficiency.
- RE is a fatigue indicator: declining RE during a workout or race suggests fatigue.
- RE is impacted by grade and wind — must compare apples to apples.
- RE is sensitive to freshness/taper status: more positive TSB → better RE.
- RE is sensitive to hilliness: higher CVI (Course Variability Index) → lower RE.
- RE improves with plyometric training, general fitness building, improved LSS, and better HPR.
- **Race analysis use:** Predicted RE vs. actual RE helps explain time outcome vs. power produced.
- **Benchmarks at FTP on flat terrain in good conditions:** <0.99 below average, 0.99–1.01 near average, >1.01 good, >1.05 likely elite/world class.

### Horizontal Power Ratio (HPR)

- **Definition:** Percentage of gross external power directed horizontally to move the athlete forward. HPR = (Stryd Power − Form Power) ÷ Stryd Power.
- Palladino uses HPR instead of Form Power Ratio because "bigger is better."
- HPR increases with speed and declines with fatigue.
- Compare at a fixed intensity because HPR rises with speed.
- **Benchmarks at FTP on flat terrain:** <75–76% below average, 75–78% near average, >77–78% good, >80% likely elite/world class.

### Form Power Ratio (FPR)

- **Definition:** Form Power ÷ Stryd Power. FPR + HPR = 100%. It is the portion of total power used in up-and-down and side-to-side movement.
- Lower is better. Palladino prefers using HPR (the complement) instead.
- **Benchmarks at FTP on flat terrain:** >25% below average, 23–25% near average, <23% good, <20% likely elite/world class.

### Leg Spring Stiffness (LSS / LSS per kg)

- Palladino normalizes LSS by body mass (LSS/kg) for cross-athlete comparison.
- LSS is relatively stable across grade; may vary slightly by speed.
- When LSS/kg consistently drops to ~0.12–0.13, Palladino considers prescribing plyometrics.
- LSS improves with plyometrics, heavy weight training, hill work (uphill and downhill), and drills.
- LSS responds to taper: fresher legs → slightly higher LSS.
- Shoe cushioning affects LSS readings (race shoes vs. training shoes) — factor this into analysis.
- **Benchmarks (LSS/kg):** 0.113 (5th percentile), 0.128 below average, 0.143 average, 0.158 above average, 0.173 (95th percentile). Palladino likes to see about 0.16 or higher as "pretty nice."

### Ground Contact Time (GCT)

- Fatigue indicator: increasing GCT during a workout/race = fatiguing.
- GCT shortens at faster speeds — must interpret relative to pace.
- Under 200 ms is "very good."

### Power-to-Ground-Contact Ratio

- Because fatigue tends to lower power and increase GCT, the ratio amplifies fatigue effects.
- Falling ratio during a race → athlete is fatiguing. Improving → strong execution.

### Stride Rate / Cadence

- Palladino interprets cadence relative to athlete height, speed, and fatigue. Shorter athletes naturally have higher cadence.
- He generally avoids forcing cadence unless it drifts too low for the given speed or fatigue pattern.
- Practical cue: quicken cadence if it trends toward ~170 at a typical speed.
- If cadence drops too low, Palladino may prescribe cadence + GCT focused work using "multiple metrics rather than just one particular metric."

### Air Power (Pa), Pr, and Pt (Stryd Power Components)

| Symbol | Definition |
|--------|-----------|
| Pt | Total Stryd reported power (Pa + Pr) |
| Pa | Stryd "air power" — power to overcome air resistance |
| Pr (or Prc) | Power to overcome running resistance and climbing (Pt − Pa); equivalent to non-wind Stryd or treadmill power |

- Pa is not reported on: treadmill, some shoes (air port obstruction), or older non-wind Stryd pod.
- Palladino routinely checks air power in workout analysis; anomalous air power can explain missed targets or unexpected RPE mismatches.
- Important for treadmill conversion. (See Section 12.)

### Watts per Kilogram (W/kg)

- CP/FTP normalized to body mass. Palladino uses W/kg to suggest plan levels and to compare athletes more meaningfully than raw watts alone.
- Use W/kg when body weight is changing; raw watts alone can mislead. Example: 300 W at 70 kg = 4.29 W/kg versus 290 W at 67 kg = 4.33 W/kg — the lighter athlete is actually fitter per unit mass.

### Normalized Power (NP)

- Exponentially weighted rolling average of power.
- For session analysis: NP of an interval session gives the "training effect" vs. average power, which is diluted by recovery segments.

### Course Variability Index (CVI)

- **Formula:** Total climbing elevation (feet) ÷ total race distance (miles).
- Used to compare hilliness across races and match RE predictions.
- CVI of ~30 ft/mile = "starting to get a little bit hilly."
- Higher CVI → lower RE.

## 5. Training Methodologies & Systems

### The Power Paradigm (vs. Goal-Time Plans)

- **Core principle:** Training plans are based on tested CP/FTP, not arbitrary goal times. All intensities scale from the individual's current fitness.
- **Advantage:** Goal-time plans let runners get in "over their heads," leading to injury/dropout. CP-based entry reduces that risk.
- **Implementation:** Test → Train → Retest → Adjust → Train (upward spiral). Raise watt targets only when CP rises.

### Quality Over Quantity

- Traditional plans assess progression by mileage. Power-based plans use training load (RSS = duration × relative intensity).
- Compare load, not just hours/miles. Account for intensity distribution. Use peak workouts as evidence of race readiness.
- "If all you do is run long and slow, it's going to teach you to run long and slow."

### Training Intensity Distribution (TID)

- **General rule:** 70–90% of volume in Zone 1 (easy aerobic running).
- **Overall distribution:** Predominantly pyramidal across all training phases.
- **Exception:** During specific race prep for middle distance (1600m–5000m), distribution becomes more polarized (classical 3-zone definition).
- As race prep moves from marathon to shorter races, TID shifts upward while maintaining overall pyramidal shape.
- Base training is NOT "all easy, all the time": exposure up to CP is still "aerobic" development; short hill sprints at Zone 7 are maintained even in base; occasional Zone 3 maintenance work is included.
- Maintain the far left (short maximal efforts / hill sprints) AND far right (easy aerobic) of the power-duration curve.

### Polarized 5K Distribution

- A 5K runner is "far more polarized": much of the volume in Zone 1 (70–90%), with a smaller amount in Zones 3–5.

### General Prep System

Palladino replaces the term "base building" (as a finite phase) with **General Prep**. "Base" is career-long accumulation over months and years — not a short block. General Prep is the finite periodized phase.

- **Early General Prep:** Entirely easy aerobic only (≤80% FTP/CP, Zone 1).
- **Middle General Prep:** Pyramidal — ~85% Zone 1 + low Zone 2, ~15% Zone 3 (mostly 94–101% FTP/CP), <1% Zone 7 hill sprints. Zones 4–6 are the intentional "grey zone" — avoided.
- **Late General Prep:** Same backbone, with modest introductory HIIT in Zones 4–5, 1 session per week.

### PDC-Driven Individualization

- Training and testing should target apparent weak/underrepresented regions of the MMP curve when a model is tethered down.
- Over-focusing on one duration band can hide real fitness improvement because other underdeveloped durations suppress the modeled output.
- Above Zone 3, prescriptions should reference the individual athlete's power-duration curve, which is superior to zone labels alone.

### Descriptive Metrics, Prescriptive Taper

- RSS/RSB/CTL are mostly descriptive tools, except that tapering can be guided semi-prescriptively with them.
- Build with positive ramp rate and negative-but-sane RSB. A-race taper: positive RSB, ramp rate just below zero for only a few days. B-race taper: smaller freshness rise.
- Caution: do not hold negative ramp too long or positive RSB too long, or fitness starts to leak away.

### Single-Sport Load Management

- Running load should primarily be interpreted as running metabolic stress, not blended indiscriminately with cycling/swimming.
- Palladino acknowledges some triathlon coaches combine sports but prefers single-sport view for running-specific meaning.

### Interval Design System

- Twelve manipulable interval variables (Laursen and Buchheit): work intensity, work duration, recovery intensity, recovery duration, series duration, number of series, between-series recovery duration, between-series recovery intensity, total volume, modality/ground surface, environmental condition (heat/hypoxia), nutritional status.
- Use RWC depletion modeling and time at ≥95% Power@VO2max to optimize interval sessions.
- Prescribe in 2% power ranges for most intervals. Expand to 4% at higher intensities (Zone 5+).
- Neophytes may struggle with narrow ranges but improve with experience.

### Gen3 Training Plans — Individualized Interval Targets

- Gen3 plans allow individualized work interval targets via a calculator using FTP/CP + RWC.
- Override only work interval power targets; keep all other session elements as prescribed.
- Default plan interval intensity: typically 104–106% FTP/CP. Calculator targets may be harder — not recommended for interval-inexperienced athletes.
- Do NOT override: long run power targets, HMP/MP tempo segments, fartlek prescriptions.

### Micro-Interval Design

- For 30-second intervals: if the desired stimulus is VO2max, keep recoveries very short (<20 seconds) to maintain VO2 kinetics.
- If recoveries are 1–2 minutes, the stimulus shifts to anaerobic/RWC (left side of power-duration curve), NOT VO2max.
- "30 on / 30 off — the rest is too long and you actually break up those kinetics."
- Micro-interval physiology translates 100% from cycling to running.

## 6. Periodization & Planning

### Plan Types

1. **Testing Plan (2 weeks):** Establishes/validates all three key testing domains. Precursor to any other plan if testing is outdated.
2. **General Build Plan:** Builds fitness with gradual volume + intensity progressions. Precursor to race plan. May enable a shorter race (5K/10K) at the end. Example structure: starts at 4 runs/week, moves to 5 runs/week at week 6, starts around 3:05/week with 1:00 long run and peaks around 4:45/week with 1:30 long run over 10 weeks.
3. **Race Plan:** Builds fitness targeting a goal race (5K, 10K, Half Marathon, Marathon). Progresses gradually with injury avoidance as a prime factor. Includes built-in testing every ~4 weeks. 5K/10K plans: ~10–13 weeks. Half Marathon: ~13 weeks. Marathon: ~17 weeks.
4. **Maintenance Plan:** Bridge between race plans. Drops load off peak but maintains baseline for seamless handoff to next race plan. Tests mid-plan and end-of-plan.
5. **Post-Race Recovery Plan:** Safe resumption after A-race. Transitions back to race plan or maintenance plan.
6. **Intro Plan:** For new Stryd users to establish valid AutoCP.

### Plan Levels

- Based approximately on CP in watts/kg.
- Each level specifies: starting easy run duration, starting workout duration, starting long run duration, starting weekly volume, runs per week.

| CP (W/kg) | Suggested Level |
|-----------|----------------|
| < 2.9 | Level 1 |
| 2.9–3.5 | Level 2 |
| 3.5–4.2 | Level 3 |
| 4.2–4.9+ | Level 4–5 |

- Level 3 example starts: easy runs 25 min → 30 min, workouts 35 min, long run 50 min, weekly volume ~2:40 (at 5 runs/week).

### Transition Rules

- Do not step into a race plan above your actual level; use build plans if coming off injury/layoff.
- Maintenance plan bridges when the next race plan is not ready to start.
- "Catching the last six weeks of a marathon plan is not going to work."
- If truncating, verify: (a) the jump in load from current to entry week is not too large, and (b) important testing is not skipped.

### Sequencing

- **Typical sequence:** Test → Build → Race → Recovery/Maintenance → Race (repeat).
- **Consecutive races:** Race → Race is possible without maintenance phase if races are close together.
- **Cross-distance stimulus change:** Half-marathon plan → 5K/10K plan → return to half-marathon plan at higher CP.

### Weekly Microcycle Structure

- **3 runs/week:** EZ run, workout, long run.
- **4 runs/week:** EZ run, workout, EZ run, long run.
- **5 runs/week:** EZ run, workout, EZ run, workout, long run (+ EZ recovery day after long run).
- **6–7 runs/week:** Additional easy aerobic runs.
- **Supplemental training:** 2× per week after harder days.
- **Loading pattern:** 3 load weeks + 1 testing/recovery week (4-week mesocycle).
- **If more recovery needed:** Reduce all 3 load weeks slightly rather than dropping one load week entirely.

### General Prep Periodization (Detailed)

#### Early General Prep

- **Composition:** Entirely easy aerobic running ≤80% FTP/CP.
- **Duration:** 3–6 weeks (up to 2–3 months) for lower-fitness / returning runners; 1–3 weeks for stronger / recently-raced runners.
- **Goal:** Establish aerobic baseline without forcing volume or frequency.
- **Transition out:** 1–2 fartlek sessions: 4–6 × 1:00 @ 98–101% FTP/CP (2-min easy jog recoveries).
- **CTL Ramp Rate:** 1–3 TSS/day per week (≈2–5% per week).

#### Middle General Prep

- **Composition:** Pyramidal. ~85% Zone 1 + low Zone 2 (mostly Zone 1), ~15% Zone 3 (majority 94–101% FTP/CP), <1% Zone 7 hill sprints (8–15 seconds, 1×/week). Zones 4–6 intentionally avoided.
- **Duration:** 4–6 to 8–10 weeks.
- **Re-testing:** Consider FTP/CP and RWC retest at near midpoint if the block is long enough.
- **Double threshold:** More experienced runners may occasionally perform 1–2 double-session days per week in the upper Zone 3A through Zone 3B range.
- **Hill sprint rationale:** Builds far left of power-duration curve, eases transition to HIIT later, can benefit running economy.
- **CTL Ramp Rate:** 1–3 for most; 3–5 for younger/fitter/more experienced. Avoid extended periods >5 TSS/d/wk.

#### Late General Prep (Transition)

- **Key change:** Introductory/transitional exposure to HIIT in Zones 4–5. Modest loads, 1× per week.
- **Duration:** 1–3 weeks.
- **End-of-phase action:** Consider re-testing FTP/CP and RWC before specific race prep.

### Down-Week Rule

- Properly controlled CTL ramp rates can reduce the need for formal down weeks, but they may still be used, especially around re-testing.
- Down-week content: lower volume plus only maintenance exposure to higher intensity, e.g., 1–2 fartlek sessions of 4–6 × 1:00 @ 98–101% FTP/CP with 2-min easy recoveries.

### Mileage Progression — Year to Year

- Safe progression rate: +10 miles per year to peak/average miles per week.
- Can break into +5 mi/wk from summer highs to winter highs, then +5 mi/wk winter to next summer.
- Above 70 mi/wk, typically requires double training days.
- Example for college-bound runner (summer before each year): Freshman HS 30 → Sophomore HS 40 → Junior HS 50 → Senior HS 60 → Freshman College 70 → Sophomore College 80 → Junior College 90.

## 7. Workout Protocols & Prescriptions

### EZ Aerobic Run

- **Goal:** Aerobic stimulus + recovery bridge to next hard workout.
- **Power:** 75–80% of FTP (average power for the run). Some plans use 70–80% FTP/CP.
- **Duration:** 30–45 min (varies by plan level).
- **Rules:** Limit excursions over 80% of FTP; avoid exceeding 100% of FTP at any time; avoid segments longer than 10–30s if over 80% of FTP. Avoid rigorous hills.
- **Cue:** "Avoid overpowering this run."
- **Variant with accelerations:** Insert 4 × ~50m (or 4×8s) accelerations to near top-end speed, keeping average ≤80%.

### EZ Long Run

- **Goal:** Aerobic endurance development.
- **Power:** 80–83% of FTP (average).
- **Duration:** 60–120 min (progresses through plan).
- **Variant with accelerations:** Insert 4 × ~50m accelerations to near top-end speed.
- **Variant with fartlek segments:** Run at 80–83% for first half to 2/3 of the run, then roll into fartlek (4–10 × 1:00 "5K fartlek" @ 103–106% FTP/CP with 2-min easy recoveries), finish with 5–10 min @ <80%. Progression: 4 → 5 → 6 → 7 → 8 → 9 → 10 fast reps.
- **Variant with half-marathon power tempo:** 2 × 15:00 at ~94–96% FTP with wide separation between blocks, e.g., build 20 min @ 80–83%, then 15:00 HMP, 50 min @ 80–83%, second 15:00 HMP, finish easy ≤80%.
- **Variant with 5K power fartlek:** 2 sets × 6 × 1:00 at ~5K power (~108–113% FTP) within the long run.

### Tempo Run (Half-Marathon Power)

- **Goal:** Threshold stimulus (Zone 3A). Half-marathon specific.
- **Power:** 94–96% of FTP.
- **Structure:** Warm up 15–20 min @ 75–80% FTP with 4 × ~50m accelerations. Tempo block: single sustained effort, progressing from 10:00 → 12:30 → 15:00 min. 3-min easy recovery below 75% FTP after tempo block. Cool down EZ (<80% FTP) to complete planned total duration.
- **Effort equivalent (for runners without CP):** Approximate half-marathon effort (slower than 10K effort).
- **Total workout duration:** 50–55 min.
- **Progression:** Duration of tempo block increases. Both duration and number of reps progress: 1×10:00 → 1×12:30 → 1×15:00 → 2×10:00 → 2×12:30 → 2×15:00 → 1×20:00 + 1×15:00 → 2×20:00.

### Marathon Power Tempo Run

- **Goal:** Marathon-specific sustained power.
- **Power:** 89–91% FTP for the tempo segment.
- **Structure:** Warm up 10–20 min near 80% FTP + 4×50m accelerations, then 35–45 min @ 89–91% FTP (sustained, non-stop running), then cool down EZ.
- **Execution cues:** Not ideal on track; hilly route acceptable if uphill can drift >91% and downhill just below 88% as long as average stays 88–91%.
- **Progression:** 35 → 40 → 45 min.

### Long Run with Marathon-Power Insert

- **Goal:** Fatigue resistance + marathon specificity.
- **Structure:** Run 81–84% FTP until the final third, then 15–30 min @ 89–91% FTP, finishing with 5–10 min <80% FTP.
- **Progression:** 15 → 20 → 25 → 30 min late MP block.

### Near-Threshold Intervals

- **Goal:** Intensive threshold stimulus (Zone 3B).
- **Power:** 98–100% of FTP.
- **Structure:** Warm up 15–20 min EZ with accelerations. 3 × 7:00 to 3 × 10:00 @ 98–100% FTP/CP. 3-min EZ running/walking recoveries. Cool down EZ (70–75% FTP).
- **Total duration:** ~60 min.
- **Progression:** 7:00 → 7:30 → 8:00 → 8:30 → 9:00 → 9:30 → 10:00. Marathon plans include 3- and 4-rep variants.

### FTP/CP Fartlek Intervals (Threshold)

- **Goal:** Threshold stimulus / Zone 3B.
- **Power:** 99–101% of FTP.
- **Structure:** Fartlek format: 4–8 × 1:00 @ 99–101% FTP with 2-min easy running between reps. Warm up 15 min @ <80% FTP. Cool down EZ.
- **Cue:** "Do not overpower these. They are set so that the overall workout will not be challenging."
- **Progression:** 4 → 6 → 8 reps across weeks.

### Supra-Threshold Intervals

- **Goal:** Controlled work above threshold / 10K-adjacent intensity (Zone 4).
- **Power:** 100–102% FTP/CP.
- **Structure:** 3×5:00 to 7×5:00, or 4×6:00 to 6×6:00 @ 100–102% FTP/CP. 3-min easy recoveries.
- **Execution cues:** Start at lower end early; progress only if power comes easily.
- **Progression:** More reps and/or longer reps over time.

### Longer Threshold/Suprathreshold Intervals (Mixed "Cutdown")

- **Standard prescription:** 5–7 min intervals at 100–102% of FTP.
- **For athletes with higher RWC:** Expanded target range; may prescribe higher percentage (up to Zone 4).
- **Power progressive mixed intensity:** e.g., 2 × 7:00 (near threshold), 2 × 5:00 (suprathreshold), 1 × 3:00 (approaching power at VO2max). Targets increase as duration decreases.
- **Range width:** Usually a 2% power range; expanded to 4% for Zone 5+ intensities.

### VO2max / Maximal Aerobic Power Intervals

- **Goal:** Maximal aerobic power stimulus (Zone 4–5).
- **Power:** 103–106% of FTP/CP.
- **Structure:** Warm up 15–20 min @ 75–80% FTP with accelerations. 4×2:30, 4×3:00, 5×3:00, or 6×3:00. Equal-duration easy recoveries (3–4 min EZ running/walking). Cool down EZ (70–75% FTP).
- **Total duration:** ~60 min.
- **Execution cue:** "Stay fluid. Don't strain." Same bottom-to-top approach.
- **Progression:** Rep count increases across plans.

### Intensive MAP / RWC Intervals

- **Goal:** Higher-end aerobic power plus reserve-work-capacity recruitment.
- **Power:** 108–112% FTP/CP.
- **Structure:** 8×1:00 with 2-min EZ running/walking recoveries.
- **Execution cues:** Lower end early; progress only if power is coming easily.

### Hill Sprints (Short Top-End Speed)

- **Goal:** Develop far left of power-duration curve (maximal/neuromuscular power); maintain in base phase.
- **Power:** Near top effort / maximal (Zone 7, >150% FTP; target >200% FTP).
- **Structure:** Warm up 15–20 min @ 75–80% FTP with 4 × ~40–50m accelerations. 4–6 × 20-second efforts near top effort (or 4–8 × 8–15 s) — preferably on a hill. Walk down / passive recovery for 2 min between reps. Cool down EZ (75–80% FTP).
- **Total duration:** 45–50 min.
- **Progression:** 4 → 5 → 6 reps across weeks.
- **Analysis focus:** Track peak power (not average) over time. All reps should be >150% FTP (>500 watts target for a strong male athlete).
- **Notes:** Can be performed on track, trail, roads, or hill. If no hill available, use any safe flat terrain.

### Late General Prep Transition Sessions

**Example 1 — Mixed Intensity:**
2 × 8:00 @ 98–101% FTP/CP → 2 × 4:00 @ 102–104% → 1 × 2:00 @ 105–110%. Typically 1×/week.

**Example 2 — Replacement for hill sprints or near-threshold:**
2 × 5:00 @ 102–104% FTP/CP → 2 × 2:30 @ 105–110%.

**Example 3 — Fartlek:**
4–6 × 1:00 @ 105–110% FTP/CP with 1:00 easy-running recoveries.

### Structured Interval/Tempo Warm-Up Template

Used for more advanced workouts and testing sessions:
1. 10 min EZ running (75–80% FTP).
2. 1 min @ 95–100% FTP.
3. 2 min easy running (70–80% FTP).
4. 20s @ 104–108% FTP.
5. 2 min easy running (70–80% FTP).
6. Then into main workout set.

### Standard Warm-Up Protocol

- 15–20 min easy running (75–80% FTP).
- 4 × ~50m accelerations to near top-end speed.
- Optional: form drills / dynamic warm-ups and/or 1–3 min at 100% FTP for proficient runners.
- Warm-up is more important than cool-down.

### Cool-Down

- Sometimes relatively short (4 min) to keep overall daily load within weekly progression targets.
- Can be extended slightly if needed, but watch weekly load impact.
- Post-race cool-down: after recovering from race effort, run easy (<75% FTP) for 10–20 minutes.

## 8. Race Strategy, Power Planning & Peaking

### The Riegel Formula (Race Power Planning)

**Formula:** Estimated Power = Known Power × (Predicted Duration ÷ Known Duration)^exponent

- **Known Power source:** CP/FTP (use duration = 50 min) OR prior race average power.
- **Requirements:** Relatively recent (within ~6 weeks), reasonably accurate, similar environmental conditions.
- **Predicted duration:** Palladino recommends 50 minutes, often bracketing with 45 and 50 minutes.
- **Caveats:** Predicted duration must be honest/realistic; conditions (heat, altitude) must be similar; garbage in → garbage out.
- **Time prediction requires both:** target race power AND estimated running effectiveness.

**Exponent values:**

| Athlete Type | Exponent |
|-------------|----------|
| Elite runners | −0.05 |
| Faster/fitter runners | −0.06 |
| Most athletes (default) | −0.07 |
| Higher/slower runners, female, age >50, high anaerobic capacity | −0.08 |
| Very slow marathon runners | up to −0.11 |

**Validated examples (same female athlete, exponent = −0.05):**

| Race | Time | Race Power | 50-min FTP Est | 45-min FTP Est | WKO4 mFTP |
|------|------|-----------|-------------|-------------|-----------|
| 10,000m | 34:51 | 242W | 238W | 239W | 241W |
| 5,000m | 17:07 | 243W | 230W | 232W | 229W |
| 10 miles | 58:55 | 232W | 234W | 235W | 236W |

### Typical Race Power as % of CP

| Race Distance | Approximate % CP |
|--------------|-----------------|
| Marathon | 88–92% (amateur); ~95–96% (elite) |
| Half Marathon | 94–98% |
| 10K | 100–102% |
| 5K | 103–108% |
| Mile / 3K | 108–116% |

### Race-Power Planning System

- Shorter race duration = higher % of FTP/CP.
- 5K and 10K tables (see Section 16) are intended as rough guides.
- More detailed race-power planning can use the SuperPower Calculator functions and a power-duration / RE approach.
- Palladino publishes calculators in the Palladino Power Project Facebook group.

### A/B/C Race Classification

Palladino classifies races into three categories based on their role within the training and racing calendar. Classification determines the appropriate taper approach, TSB target, and CTL ramp rate management before and during the race period.

| Race Type | Effort Level          | TSB (at race)          | CTL Ramp Rate (at race) | Notes                                                                                                          |
|-----------|-----------------------|------------------------|-------------------------|----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| 'A' Race  | Full effort           | Approximately +5 to +25 | Negative for 1–5 days   | Full taper — **both** TSB and CTL ramp rate parameters should be met                                          |
| 'B' Race  | Full effort           | Approximately −5 to +5 | Positive to 0 (typically +1 to +2) | Racing full effort on an incomplete taper, with goal of keeping CTL ramp rate from going negative (i.e., not giving up CTL) |
| 'C' Race  | Controlled / less than full | Negative        | Positive                | The "race" is used with a training goal in mind and a specific training prescription. Little or no taper. Example: running a half marathon controlled at marathon power |

**Classification guidance:**
- An **A Race** is the primary goal event. Both TSB and CTL ramp rate criteria must be satisfied — the full taper is non-negotiable.
- A **B Race** is raced fully but without a complete taper. The priority is preserving CTL by avoiding a negative ramp rate. TSB will be lower than an A race. Best used when an important secondary race falls inside a build block.
- A **C Race** functions as a training stimulus with a race context. Effort is deliberately controlled. No taper is applied; CTL ramp rate stays positive. The course, crowd, and competitive environment serve the training prescription rather than a performance goal.

### Taper Targets

| Taper Type | RSB Target                    | CTL Ramp Rate Target                      | Effort Level                | Duration                    |
|------------|-------------------------------|-------------------------------------------|-----------------------------|-----------------------------|
| A-Race     | +5 to +25 (ideally ~+15)      | Negative for 1–5 days                    | Full effort                 | ~2 weeks                    |
| B-Race     | −5 to +5                      | Positive to 0 (typically +1 to +2)       | Full effort                 | Shorter; may stay in build  |
| C-Race     | Negative                      | Positive                                  | Controlled / less than full | None / minimal              |

- Keep intensity high while cutting volume during taper.
- Do not hold negative ramp too long or positive RSB too long — fitness starts to leak away.

### Taper-Week Fartlek Session

- 10 min warm-up @ <80% FTP.
- 4 × 1:00 @ 99–101% FTP (2-min EZ running recoveries).
- 3 × 15s gradual accelerations to faster than 5K effort (>103% FTP/CP), 1:45 easy recoveries.
- Cool down to 40 min total.

### Taper Week Schedule (5K/10K Race — 7-Day Example, Race on Sunday)

| Day | Session |
|-----|---------|
| Monday | EZ aerobic run, 30 min @ <80% FTP/CP |
| Tuesday | Taper fartlek (as above) |
| Wednesday | EZ aerobic run, 30 min @ <80% FTP/CP |
| Thursday | Taper fartlek (as above) |
| Friday | EZ aerobic run, 30 min @ <80% FTP/CP |
| Saturday | Pre-race day protocol (25 min total) |
| Sunday | Race warm-up + Race + Cool-down |

Variants: 5-day drops Monday and Friday; 6-day drops Friday. Final workout day before pre-race may use 2×15s accelerations instead of 3×15s.

### Pre-Race Day Priming Workout

- **Goal:** Non-taxing primer of various power systems.
- **Duration:** 25 min running (29 min total including walking).
- **Structure:**
  1. 10–15 min @ 75–80% FTP (easy running).
  2. 1 min @ 98–102% FTP.
  3. 2-min easy running recovery.
  4. 20s @ 103–110% FTP.
  5. 2-min walking recovery.
  6. 2 × 8s (~50m) accelerations to top speed, 1-min walking recoveries.
  7. Finish with EZ running for remainder.
- **Cue:** "Leave watch running — do not pause the watch."

### Race-Day Warm-Up Protocol

1. 10 min EZ running @ <80% FTP.
2. 1 min @ 98–100% FTP. Stop and recover.
3. 30s @ 105–110% FTP. Stop and recover.
4. 2 × 50m gradual accelerations to top speed. Recover.
5. Finish warm-up ~10–20 minutes before race start. Occasional ~40m strides are acceptable to stay warm.

### Post-Race Cool-Down

- After recovering from race effort, run easy (<75% FTP) for 10–20 minutes.

## 9. Testing & Assessment Protocols

### AutoCP / Power-Duration-Curve Validation

- **Requirement:** Collect maximal efforts in three key domains:
  1. **Short:** 2–3 minutes (maximal effort)
  2. **Medium:** 9–12 minutes (maximal effort)
  3. **Long:** 20–40 minutes (maximal effort or race)
- Keep each domain refreshed every 4–8 weeks. Older than 8 weeks may reduce accuracy.
- A maximum effort or race in any of these domains satisfies that domain.
- Palladino calls a power-duration curve with all three domains checked and recent data an "optimized power-duration curve."

### Before Starting a New Plan (Decision Tree)

1. Recent race + recent testing → no additional testing needed.
2. All three domains tested in last 6 weeks → ready to go ("optimized PDC").
3. No recent data → use 2-week testing plan.
4. Missing only 1–2 domains → test only the missing domains.

### Stryd CP Test — Track Option (3-Lap / 6-Lap)

1. Warm up 5 min. 2–3 × 100m strides at ~80% max effort.
2. 800m easy-pace run (2 laps, innermost lane; "comfortably maintain conversation").
3. Warm up another 5 min.
4. 1200m maximum-effort run (consistent pace, nearly exhausted at end).
5. 30-min recovery (walk or jog slowly).
6. 2400m maximum-effort run (consistent pace throughout).
7. Cool down.

### Stryd CP Test — 3-Min / 9-Min Option

1. Warm up 10 min. 5 × 100m strides at ~80% max effort.
2. 3-min maximum distance run (consistent pace, nearly exhausted at end).
3. 30-min recovery (walk or jog slowly).
4. 9-min maximum distance run (consistent pace).
5. Cool down.

### CP Test / Race Protocol (from Plan Documents)

1. Warm up 15 min EZ with 4×50m accelerations.
2. Run 3 min maximal.
3. Recover fully.
4. Run 5–10 min EZ.
5. Recover fully so total between test segments ≥30 min.
6. Run 10 min maximal.
7. Cool down EZ.
8. Use average powers from the 3-min and 10-min segments in the SuperPower Calculator.
- Alternative: run a 5K or 10K race as the CP estimation source.

### CP Test Protocol (Full Detail from Marathon Plan)

- **Test Week Structure:**
  - **Day 1 — 10-min max effort TT:** Warm up 20 min EZ with 4×8s/~50m accelerations. Stop/walk/recover. Run 10 min maximal (use Stryd 10-min model power as target for first 7–8 min). 45 min total running (~52 min elapsed with walking).
  - **Days 2–3 — EZ runs:** 70–80% FTP.
  - **Day 4 — 10s max + 2-min max:** Same warm-up. Run 10s maximal (hill preferred; if flat, use 15m flying start). Walk/recover. EZ 5–10 min. Walk/recover. At least 10 min between test segments. Run 2 min maximal (use 2-min model power for first minute). 45 min total running.
  - **Days 5–6 — EZ runs.**
  - **Day 7 — 20-min (or 5K) max effort TT:** Same warm-up. Run 20 min maximal. 60 min total running (~67 min elapsed).
- **Target setting for tests:** Use Stryd PDC model power at test duration as low end; model power +5–10% as high end. OR use prior test power −1% to +9%.
- **Post-test:** Enter 2-min, 10-min, and 20-min power-duration pairs into SuperPower Calculator. Update FTP/CP value in FinalSurge settings.
- **Key note:** "Testing is training, too" — each test effort provides a power stimulus for fitness growth.
- **Execution cue:** "Start off at your last test power. It might feel really easy — who cares. You're going to ramp until you're spent."

### Independent CP Testing

- Design own protocol with durations from 1 minute to 20 minutes.
- Greater separation between the short and long tests improves reliability.
- When the longer test is under 12 minutes, overestimation risk rises.
- Custom terrain acceptable if it matches the athlete's racing demands.

### WKO4 PDC / mFTP Assessment

- **Requirements:** 90-day run-power history with broad maximal or near-maximal efforts across wide range of durations.
- **Outputs:** mFTP, TTE, Pmax, FRC, SEE, CV.
- **Use:** Assess threshold, identify weak durations on the MMP curve, monitor change across time.
- Key rule: poor representation in one or more durations degrades model accuracy.
- Selectively test apparent areas where MMP and PDC are tethered down.
- Test the far left of PDC with flying start efforts (Stryd has a short time lag from standing starts).

### FTP Validation Checks (How Palladino Checks Whether FTP Is Reasonable)

1. RPE at various known intensities is the biggest tell.
2. Easy running consistently exceeding upper Zone 1C → FTP may be underestimated (or running too hard).
3. Long intervals near 100% FTP consistently too hard → FTP overestimated (or overtrained).
4. Long intervals near 100% FTP consistently too easy → FTP underestimated or improved.
5. Race %FTP against expected norms: a 5K runner typically at 105–107% FTP; if at 109% → FTP underestimated. 10K runner typically at 102–104%; if at 100% → FTP overestimated or sub-maximal race.
6. Very high CTL ramp rate feeling too easy in a power-based model → FTP possibly underestimated.
7. Cross-check estimates across methods with caution. Be suspicious of incongruous estimates; congruent estimates = useful cross-reference.
8. During weight change periods, use W/kg.

### Lactate-Threshold Testing

- Valid only with consistent protocol.
- If treadmill ramp testing changes grade, Stryd grade input must be updated during the test.
- Palladino flags cost and convenience as limitations for frequent use.

## 10. Form, Technique & Biomechanics

### Running Effectiveness as Form Feedback

- Track RE over time, filtered by intensity range (e.g., 88–102% FTP) and grade, to see if form is improving.
- RE improvements can result from improved LSS, better horizontal power ratio, and fitness gains.
- RE paper: improving RE can come through improving LSS and HPR; HPR interventions may include wall/fence drills, hip-extension work, and dynamic stride work.
- Running effectiveness can flag fatigue, but comparisons must control for grade and wind.

### Biomechanical Metrics Interpretation

- **HPR** is Palladino's preferred forward-motion metric; higher HPR means more gross external power directed horizontally.
- **Form power ratio** is the "non-forward" complement; lower is better.
- **LSS/kg** is preferable to raw LSS for comparison. One of the main actionable contributors to RE.
- **GCT** under 200 ms is very good; power/GCT decline suggests fatigue.
- **Cadence/stride rate** is interpreted relative to height, speed, and fatigue. Palladino describes cadence as stride rate rather than a rigid "180" target. Lower GCT at the same speed is favorable. Vertical oscillation is better interpreted relatively (vertical ratio / horizontal ratio) than as raw absolute.

### Technique Cues

- "Quick steps" and "low ceiling / don't bump your head" to reduce excessive vertical motion while preserving effective movement.
- Higher cadence tends to accompany lower vertical oscillation at the same speed.
- Fatigue commonly brings lower cadence, higher GCT, higher relative form power, and lower LSS.

### Treadmill vs. Overground

- Treadmill running is associated with different mechanics and lower running economy than overground running.
- Power targets cannot be assumed portable without correction. (See Section 12.)

## 11. Recovery, Injury Prevention & Longevity

### Recovery as a Primary Pillar

- Recovery is treated as a primary support pillar of all phases, not an afterthought.
- **Nutrition and sleep are foremost** — the most important complementary recovery measures in all training periods.
- Progression itself is an injury-prevention strategy: avoiding aggressive ramp rates, avoiding sustained periods >5 TSS/day per week except with close monitoring.

### Key Recovery & Injury-Prevention Rules

1. **Ramp rate as injury predictor:** >7 CTL change/week = injury/illness risk. Older runners and injury-prone runners: keep at 1–2.
2. **RSB as overtraining indicator:** Below −40 = overreaching territory.
3. **RPE-RSS mismatch investigation:** If RPE is high but RSS is low (or vice versa), investigate: sleep, stress, temperature, CP accuracy, residual fatigue from supplemental training.
4. **If in doubt, leave it out.** Skip workouts when on the verge of cold or when niggles appear. Do not make up missed workouts.
5. **Missing a full week:** Do not jump ahead in the plan. May need to repeat or back-step.
6. **Cross-training for injured runners:** Palladino will assign cycling with a reduced TSS value, acknowledging imperfect translation between cycling and running load.
7. **Avoid back-to-back higher-stress days.** Always keep at least one EZ day or off day between interval, tempo, and long-run stressors.
8. **Keep easy runs under the 80% ceiling** to recover for the next hard workout.
9. **"If you feel ill… it might be better to skip a workout rather than try to make it up later."**
10. **Shoe changes:** Different cushioning affects LSS readings — factor this into analysis.

### Plyometrics and Strength for Resilience

- Prescribed to improve injury resistance, not just performance.
- LSS monitoring: consistently low LSS/kg (0.12–0.13) triggers plyometric prescription.
- LSS can improve with plyometrics, heavy strength work, and hill work, which Palladino links to better mechanics and potentially improved RE.

### Environmental Management

- For indoor treadmill use, lower room temperature and use large fans to reduce heat load and prevent indoor sessions from becoming unintentionally harder than prescribed.

### Relative Freshness and RE

- Relative freshness can modestly improve RE. Palladino's authored RE paper notes a modest positive correlation between RE and less-negative / more-positive TSB.

## 12. Treadmill Running with Power

### FTP/CP Portability Rules

1. **Outdoor FTP/CP is NOT universally portable.** Invalid across altitude, temperature, humidity, and treadmill changes.
2. If FTP/CP is invalid for the treadmill, ALL relative training targets will be wrong.

### Heat/Humidity Correction

- Use the SuperPower Calculator to convert FTP from outdoor conditions to treadmill indoor conditions.
- Example: 300W FTP @ 10°C/50% humidity → 295.5W at 20°C/70% humidity (treadmill environment).
- Mitigation: lower room temperature, use large electric fans.

### Air Power (Pa) Correction — Stryd Wind Version

- Outdoor FTP/CP includes air power component → inflated relative to treadmill where Pa = 0.
- Must convert outdoor Pt-based CP to Pr-based CP for treadmill.
- Example: outdoor CP (Pt) = 337.7W, treadmill CP (Pr) = 325.7W — a substantial difference.
- Quick approximation: subtract ~4% from outdoor FTP/CP for treadmill.
- Non-wind Stryd outdoor FTP is more directly portable (after heat correction).

### Treadmill Process Flowcharts

**Non-Wind Stryd on Motorized Treadmill:**
1. Outdoor FTP/CP can be used.
2. Adjust FTP/CP to indoor conditions (heat/humidity).
3. Power targets based on adjusted FTP/CP — run at ~1% incline.

**Wind Stryd on Motorized Treadmill:**
1. Outdoor FTP/CP CANNOT be used directly.
2. Apply methods to convert to '0 air power' FTP/CP — or do CP test on treadmill.
3. Adjust FTP/CP to indoor conditions if needed.
4. Power targets based on adjusted FTP/CP — run at ~1% incline.

**Non-Motorized Curved Treadmill (NMCTM) — Both Stryd Versions:**
1. Outdoor FTP/CP CANNOT be used. Motorized TM FTP is also NOT portable.
2. Do CP test on the NMCTM — or use Stryd weight scaling factor (1.20–1.35×; lighter runners → closer to upper range).
3. Adjust FTP/CP to indoor conditions if scaling factor used.
4. Power targets based on adjusted FTP/CP.

**Wind Stryd on Motorized TM — Weight Setting Option (Palladino's Preferred Method):**
1. Use outdoor FTP/CP but adjust Stryd weight for TM use.
2. Determine % air power at CP/FTP outdoors.
3. Adjust Stryd weight by same percentage for treadmill use only.
4. TM power targets based on outdoor CP/FTP.

### Two Approaches to Reconcile Outdoor vs. Treadmill Power

**Option A — Two FTP/CP values (one outdoor, one indoor):** Weight setting stays fixed. Must use correct FTP/CP basis for each environment. Disadvantage: treadmill RSS slightly under-reported.

**Option B — Two Stryd weight settings (Palladino's preferred for coached athletes):** One FTP/CP value used for all environments. Increase Stryd weight setting for treadmill by same percentage as the power loss. Advantage: more accurate RSS and training load metrics. Disadvantage: must remember to switch weight settings.

### Incline Recommendations

- 1% treadmill incline best matches outdoor overground running (in terms of O₂ utilization).
- Applicable range: 0.5–1.5% across speeds from ~9:11/mi to ~5:21/mi.
- At speeds < 7:09/mi, difference between 0% and 1% is insignificant.
- Power reporting from pod to watch is accurate at 0–~1.5% incline. For ≥2% incline, must use Stryd phone app or Stryd Workout App on Garmin.
- **Palladino's preference:** Athletes should use only 1% incline. Increasing grade does not provide meaningful hill training effect.

### NMCTM Specifics

- ~37% higher O₂ uptake, ~22% higher HR than track or motorized TM at same velocity.
- Power on NMCTM appears more stochastic than motorized TM, closer to overground.

### Treadmill Distance Calibration

- Using RE: Calculate RE from 12-min test segment both outdoor and on TM.
- Distance calibration factor for TM = outdoor factor + ((outdoor 12' RE − TM 12' RE) × 100).

### Key Treadmill Rules

- Set watch to distance and speed from Stryd, turn off GPS.
- Do not test on TM unless all running/racing is on TM (test data confounds Stryd race planner).
- Running power is stochastic; TM power tracing is smoother than outdoor.

## 13. Supplemental Training

### Philosophy

- Run training is primary. Supplemental work must not detract from primary running training.
- Introduce supplemental work modestly, progress safely, sequence properly.
- **Frequency:** 2× per week, typically after harder sessions.
- **Priority if dropping components:** Keep priming drills (before runs) and glute/hamstring work (injury prevention).
- Supplemental training serves three purposes: (a) priming for the run, (b) injury resilience, (c) performance improvement (e.g., plyometrics → improved running economy/effectiveness).

### Components

**1. Pre-Run Priming:**
- Mobility drills: lunge matrix (Jay Johnson), hip swings.
- Form drills and spring drills (hops) as warm-up priming.
- Plyometrics before sprints can prime better sprint performance.

**2. Planks:**
- Front plank + side plank each side. Side planks = ½ duration of front plank.
- Start easy, progress 10s front / 5s sides every 2 weeks.

**3. Minimal Basic Strength:**
- Upper body: pushups, arm curls, military press, runner arm swings, bent-over rows (dumbbells); pull-ups and bar dips if available.
- Lower body: walking lunges with dumbbells; glute/hip thrusts.
- Start with easily accomplished weight for 8 reps, progress 1 rep every 1–2 weeks. Advance weight at 15 reps.
- Bodyweight movements: start easy, progress 1 rep every 2 weeks.

**4. Plyometrics:**
- Box jumps, frog jumps, single-legged hops (or single-legged stair hops).
- Start easy, progress 1 rep every 1–2 weeks. May add weight vest/belt.

**5. Mobility:**
- Hip flexor stretching, donkey kicks, wall drills (front and back side), lunge matrix, foam rolling.

| Ramp Rate | Week-over-week change in CTL | Primary safety governor |

| B Race | Secondary race run at full effort on incomplete taper; goal is preserving CTL | TSB ~−5 to +5; ramp rate positive trending to 0 |
| C Race | Training race; controlled/sub-maximal effort; no taper; used as a training stimulus with race context | TSB negative; ramp rate positive; eg HM at marathon power |

## 17. FTP Estimation Method Comparison

| Method | Parameters | Palladino's Assessment |
|--------|-----------|----------------------|
| WKO4 PDC model (mFTP) | >3, <10 | **Preferred.** Accurate if MMP has sufficient maximal data across durations. |
| Monod CP model (Stryd / independent) | 2–3 | Valid. Larger duration difference = more reliable. |
| Modified Riegel formula | 1 (race) | Useful. Often superior to 95%-of-20-min. Best with 5K–HM race. |
| Jim Vance 30-min TT (last 20 min) | 1 | **Not recommended.** Likely overestimates, especially faster runners. |
| Jim Vance 3+9 min (90% of average) | 2 | **Not recommended.** Larger margin of error. |
| Secret of Running 10-min (88%) | 1 | **Not recommended.** Assumes 60-min FTP; may err low. |
| Secret of Running 20-min (93%) | 1 | **Not recommended.** Same assumptions; may err low. |
| 95% of 20-min power | 1 | **Explicitly rejected.** Most common mis-applied assumption. |
| Fitzgerald 30-min TT = FTP | 1 | **Not recommended.** Will likely overestimate. |
| Stryd race-time conversion | 1 | **Problematic.** Assumes standard RE; error up to 5% at extremes. |
| Secret of Running race-time table | 1 | Uses default ECOR 0.98; error up to 5%. |

## 19. How to Use This Knowledge Base

### For AI Agent Retrieval & Reasoning

**Priority hierarchy when coaching a runner:**

1. **Safety first.** Always check ramp rate, RSB, RPE-RSS alignment, and injury/illness signals before prescribing or approving a workout. If anything is flagged, refer to Section 11 (Recovery & Injury Prevention) and invoke the "if in doubt, leave it out" principle.

2. **Anchor to tested fitness.** Before giving any intensity prescription, confirm the runner has a valid and recent CP/FTP. If not, direct them to Section 9 (Testing & Assessment) to establish one. Never prescribe from a goal time.

3. **Philosophy before methodology.** When explaining "why," draw from Section 1 (Philosophy & Core Principles). When explaining "how," draw from Section 5 (Methodologies) and Section 7 (Workout Protocols).

4. **Zone prescriptions require context.** Always pair a zone reference with the caveats from Section 2 — especially that Zones 4–7 are fuzzy and require PDC-driven individualization. Never use zones alone above Zone 3.

5. **Metrics are descriptive, not prescriptive** (except taper). When a runner asks about RSB, CTL, or ramp rate, remind them these are one piece of the picture. Cross-reference with RPE and subjective state per Section 4.

6. **Treadmill queries require special handling.** Always route through Section 12 before giving any treadmill power target. Outdoor FTP/CP is NOT portable without adjustment.

7. **Race planning requires both power AND RE.** When helping with race targets, use the Riegel formula and race power tables from Section 8 and Section 16, but always note that time prediction requires estimated RE as well.

8. **Use athlete-facing cues.** When communicating with runners, draw from the language in Section 14 for motivational framing and Section 1 for philosophical grounding. Palladino's voice is professional, reassuring, patient, and data-informed but never data-obsessed.

### Note on Conflicting Information

Minor discrepancies exist across source documents regarding exact power zone boundaries for plan-specific workouts (e.g., VO2max intervals prescribed as 103–105% in some plans and 104–106% in others). These reflect Palladino's individualization approach: he adjusts the precise target window based on athlete-specific CP, RWC, and the power-duration curve. When encountering such variation, the AI coach should default to the zone system boundaries in Table 1 as the structural framework, and use the specific workout prescriptions from Section 7 as the practical implementation guidance. If the runner has RWC data, use the Gen3 individualized calculator approach from Section 5 for the most precise targets.

Similarly, HPR benchmarks show minor variation (75–77% vs. 76–78% for "near average") across source documents. The AI coach should present the range as approximately 75–78% for near average, noting that exact cutoffs are approximate.
