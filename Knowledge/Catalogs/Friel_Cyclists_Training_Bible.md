### [CTB-L2-002] Flat Force Reps (workout MF1)

- Goal: build capacity to deliver force to the pedal on the bike (alternative to weight lifting; resembles heavy squats/step-ups/lunges).
- When used: prep period; Appendix B Muscular Force Workouts MF1. Prerequisite: warm up well; if knee concerns, caution below.
- Structure: after warm-up, on flat terrain shift to a high gear such as 53 × 14 (so hard that at full force you manage only about 50 rpm initially) and slow nearly to a stop. Seated (do NOT stand), accelerate with maximal effort for only 6 or 8 strokes total (3 or 4 per leg); first strokes ~50 rpm or lower, cadence rises by last strokes. Recover 3 to 4 minutes riding slowly in a low gear between sets.
- Intensity anchors: extremely high effort per stroke; power meter ideal (HR useless).
- First time: no more than 2 sets; if knee concern, only one set of 6 to 8 total strokes with conservative effort. Experiment with gears to find the right one.
- Progressions: per Table 12.5.
- Cues: combine with other workouts only if force reps are done first; never near end of a fatiguing ride (you cannot make a tired muscle stronger); alternatively follow a lone force reps session with a long cooldown.

### [CTB-L2-003] Hill Force Reps

- Goal: same as flat force reps with greater neuromuscular stress.
- Prerequisites: at least 4 flat force rep sessions and knees not complaining.
- Structure: after good warm-up, go to a short steep hill (about 8 percent grade) with little/no traffic and safe U-turns at top and bottom. Shift to a high gear, not as high as flat (e.g., 53 × 15). Come nearly to a stop at the bottom. Seated, alternate driving pedals down 3 to 6 times with each leg while otherwise pedaling normally (6 to 12 total strokes). Up to 3 sets with 3 to 4 minutes easy pedaling in low gear between.
- Total duration: force reps portion at most 15 minutes per set (3 sets about 45 minutes, excluding warm-up); may be part of a longer ride, combined with another Appendix B workout (force reps first).
- Notes: do a recovery ride the day after 2 or 3 sets of hill force reps (likely in base 1); soreness shows next day. See Table 12.5.

### [CTB-L2-004] Race-period HIT workout: 90-second intervals

- Goal: race readiness and freshness by the end of race week.
- When used (period/phase, prerequisites): race period (generally the last six or seven days before the race), 3 or 4 sessions depending on Saturday or Sunday race (Table 13.2).
- Structure: standard warm-up / several 90-second intervals with 3-minute recoveries (counts per day in Table 13.2) / short cooldown.
- Intensity anchors: the highest intensity expected in the race; power is the preferred measure.
- Total duration: quite short; decreases as the interval count decreases through the week.
- Progressions / variations: sprinters still do 90-second efforts in two parts: a sustained high effort (as following a leadout man or exiting a criterium corner), then an all-out sprint at the end. Time trialists rehearse pacing on the TT bike; climbers use a race-like hill likely to cause a break; include expected heat, wind, altitude if possible.
- Cues / notes: heart rate cannot gauge intensity because 90 seconds is too brief for HR to reach the target zone.

### [CTB-L2-005] AE1: Recovery

- Goal: speed recovery; integral to training throughout the season (not on the annual training plan).
- When used: any time; cross-training options in prep and early base.
- Structure: ride shorter than your average ride, on a flat course, primarily in the small chainring, with a comfortably high cadence.
- Intensity anchors: heart rate zone 1, or at or below 55 percent of FTP. Cross-training (e.g., cross-country skiing on a relatively flat course, health club machines): RPE 2 or 3 on a 10-high scale (Table 4.1). Heart rate zones for alternative activities such as skiing are unlikely to be the same as bike zones.
- Total duration: shorter than the average ride.
- Progressions / variations: indoor trainer or rollers any time of year, especially if flat roads are unavailable. May be combined with a speed skills workout.
- Cues / notes: light riding benefits advanced athletes who want to speed recovery; novices and intermediates may benefit more from taking the day off.

### [CTB-L2-006] AE2: Aerobic Threshold (AeT)

- Goal: boost aerobic fitness by improving delivery and use of oxygen to produce energy in the muscles.
- When used: year-round, first to build and later to maintain aerobic endurance. Once EF plateaus, do it about half as frequently as when initially building.
- Structure: warm-up; ride 1 to 4 hours at AeT heart rate plus or minus 2 bpm on a flat to gently rolling course or indoor trainer. The longer the A-priority race, the longer the AeT portion. If racing primarily criteriums or time trials, ride 1 hour to 90 minutes in the AeT heart rate zone.
- Intensity anchors: early in base use a heart rate monitor. If not recently lab-tested for AeT, assume AeT heart rate is approximately 30 bpm below anaerobic threshold heart rate (Chapter 4, "Setting Training Zones"). With a power meter: after the workout divide normalized power for the AeT segment by average heart rate for the same segment = efficiency factor (EF). Rising EF indicates improving AeT; it seldom rises linearly but ratchets up over several weeks. When EF plateaus for several consecutive sessions, switch from heart rate to power to gauge intensity: ride steadily at the NP plateau power plus or minus 5 watts.
- Total duration: 1 to 4 hours of AeT work (plus warm-up).
- Cues / notes: can also be used as the test of aerobic endurance (see T1).

### [CTB-L2-007] AE3: Intensive Endurance

- Goal: develop aerobic endurance while boosting muscular endurance; boost the body's capacity for processing oxygen to produce energy.
- Structure: warm-up; ride for an hour or more on a flat to gently rolling course, mostly in heart rate zone 2 or power zone 3. Remain seated on hills.
- Intensity anchors: intensive endurance RPE about 4 to 6 for most of the ride.
- Total duration: 1 hour or more.
- Variations: on an indoor trainer, shift gears frequently to increase the load and simulate hills.

### [CTB-L2-008] MF1: Flat Force Reps

- Goal: prepare for the more advanced MF2 (hill force reps).
- When used: most commonly in the prep period, but may be done any time in the season.
- Structure: after warm-up, shift to a high gear (e.g., 53 × 14) and slow to nearly a stop. Accelerate with only 6 or 8 strokes total (3 or 4 pedal strokes per leg, alternating legs normally). Extremely high effort on each downward stroke. Stay seated, do not stand. First strokes are slow (around 50 rpm or lower); cadence rises gradually with speed. Between sets, ride slowly in a low gear for 3 to 4 minutes.
- Intensity anchors: extremely high effort per downward stroke (see Table 12.5 for details).
- Progressions / variations: no more than 2 sets the first time. Experiment with gearing the first couple of times; if you err, err on the low-gear (easy) side.
- Cues / notes: if there is concern about the knees, do only 1 set of 6 to 8 total pedal strokes and be conservative with gear and effort.

### [CTB-L2-009] MF2: Hill Force Reps

- Goal: same as MF1 but on a hill, to develop greater neuromuscular fitness.
- When used / prerequisites: have previously done at least 4 flat force reps sessions and know your knees do not complain about the high load.
- Structure: warm up well. Use a short (30 to 50 yards or meters), steep (8 percent grade is perfect) hill with little or no traffic, where you can safely U-turn at top and bottom. Coast to the base, shift to a high gear (not quite as high as flat; e.g., 53 × 16 instead of 53 × 14), come to a very brief balanced stop without unclipping. Seated, alternately drive the pedals down 3 to 6 times with each leg, otherwise pedaling normally = 6 to 12 total strokes. Never stand. Up to 3 sets per workout. After each set shift to a low gear and pedal gently 3 to 5 minutes. Do not shorten recovery (reduces neuromuscular benefit); be sure legs are recovered.
- Intensity anchors: power, not heart rate, is the only gauge; very high wattage on each stroke (see Table 12.5).
- Frequency: no more than twice per week, at least 48 hours between workouts.
- Cues / notes: high-risk, high-reward. At the first sign of any tenderness (even slight), stop the workout.

### [CTB-L2-010] MF3: Hill Repeats

- Goal: muscular force.
- Structure: steep hill of about 6 to 8 percent grade taking 20 to 30 seconds to climb; 3 to 8 repeats with 2 to 4 minutes recovery between. Climb in the saddle with minimal upper body movement, do not stand. Select a gear allowing a cadence of only 70 rpm or lower. Recover by coasting down before the next rep.
- Intensity anchors: power zone 5 each climb. Heart rate is not a good indicator (reps are short).
- Frequency: no more than twice per week, at least 48 hours between workouts.
- Cues / notes: stop if knees become sensitive. Do not do this workout if prone to knee injury.

### [CTB-L2-011] SS1: Spin-Ups

- Goal: improve pedaling efficiency, shown by an increasingly higher maximum cadence without bouncing on the saddle.
- Structure: on a flat or slightly downhill road, or an indoor trainer on light resistance, in a low (easy) gear, gradually increase cadence over about 1 minute to maximum spin rate (maintainable without bouncing). Relax lower legs and feet, especially toes. Hold maximum cadence as long as possible (probably only a few seconds). Recover at least 1 minute. Repeat several times.
- Intensity anchors: heart rate and power are not meaningful.
- Cues / notes: best with a handlebar device displaying cadence.

### [CTB-L2-012] SS2: Isolated Leg

- Goal: eliminate dead spots at the top and bottom of the stroke.
- Structure: on a flat or slightly downhill road, do 90 percent of the work with one leg while the other rests. Indoors with light resistance, rest the other foot on a chair or stool. Spin at the highest cadence you can. Change legs when fatigue begins.
- Intensity anchors: heart rate and power have no significance.

### [CTB-L2-013] SS3: 9-to-3

- Goal: master the most challenging part of the pedal stroke, at the top.
- Structure: while pedaling, imagine driving the pedal forward from the 9 o'clock position (back side) to 3 o'clock (front side) without going through 12 o'clock. Use a low (easy) gear for low force at a comfortably high cadence.

### [CTB-L2-014] SS4: Fixed-Gear

- Requires a fixed-gear bike: one chainring, one cog, no derailleurs, no freewheel (local shop can set one up). You are always pedaling and can't coast; learn to relax and let the bike do the work.
- First few rides: flat area with no traffic or stop signs (e.g., large empty parking lot); keep workouts short.
- Caution: can be dangerous until you master the bike; avoid busy streets.

### [CTB-L2-015] SS5: Cornering

- Structure: on a curbed street with clean surface and 90-degree turns, practice: lean both bike and body into the turn; lean body while keeping bike upright; keep body upright while leaning the bike. Practice several speeds and different approach angles. Include a few with sprint efforts into and out of corners.
- Cautions: avoid streets with heavy traffic. Heart rate and power are not important.

### [CTB-L2-017] ME1: Tempo Intervals

- Goal: muscular endurance.
- Structure: mostly flat road, slight uphill (1 to 3 percent grade), or indoor trainer. After warm-up, 3 to 5 work intervals of 12 to 20 minutes with recoveries about one-fourth as long (3 to 5 minutes; e.g., 4 minutes after a 16-minute interval). Stay seated. Recover pedaling easily in zone 1. Avoid heavy traffic and frequent stop signs.
- Intensity anchors: heart rate zone 3 or power zone 4. Power preferred. If using heart rate only, the interval starts as soon as you begin pedaling hard, not when zone 3 is reached; during the lag use RPE 5 to 6 on a 10-high scale (Table 4.1).

### [CTB-L2-018] ME2: Cruise Intervals

- Goal: muscular endurance at about anaerobic threshold; especially key for time trialists (intensity very similar to a 40 km time trial).
- Structure: relatively flat course, slight uphill (1 to 3 percent) or indoor trainer; 3 to 5 work intervals of 6 to 12 minutes. Recover in zone 1 easy pedaling for one-fourth of the preceding interval (e.g., 90 seconds after 6 minutes). Stay relaxed; cadence similar to that used at a race of that distance.
- Intensity anchors: about anaerobic threshold, RPE 7 or 8. Power preferred: at or slightly below FTP. Heart rate: gradually raise to near FTHR (Table 4.2); timed interval starts when the hard effort begins, not when the goal HR is reached; estimate by RPE 7 or 8 (Table 4.1).
- Progression: first workout of new season totals about 12 minutes or less of work (e.g., 2 × 6 minutes). Over a few sessions increase total work to about 30 to 50 minutes (e.g., 5 × 6 minutes or 5 × 10 minutes).
- Variation (optional, harder): shift every 20 to 60 seconds between normal gear for this intensity and a higher (harder) gear.

### [CTB-L2-019] ME3: Hill Cruise Intervals

- Goal: same as ME2, with somewhat increased intensity and slightly greater benefit for muscular force.
- Structure: same as ME2 but on a long low-gradient hill (2 to 4 percent grade; light traffic, no stop signs) or into a strong headwind. Stay seated; smooth pedal stroke, minimal upper body motion. Recover by turning around and returning to the bottom in zone 1; the coasting descent makes recoveries longer than in ME2.
- Intensity anchors: power near FTP preferred; heart rate gauges FTHR (Tables 4.2, 4.3). With heart rate only, interval starts when you begin pedaling hard. FTHR may not be reached in the first or even second interval (heart rate lag and warm-up effect); maintain RPE 7 or 8 on a 10-high scale.
- Variation: shift between normal gear and a higher (harder) gear every 30 seconds or so.

### [CTB-L2-020] ME4: Sweet Spot Intervals

- Goal: muscular endurance.
- When used: start of the base period.
- Structure: after warm-up, 2 work intervals on a flat or slightly uphill (2 to 3 percent grade) course; ride steadily, in the saddle. Intervals 12 to 20 minutes, with recovery about one-fourth the interval duration (e.g., 4 minutes between 16-minute intervals).

- Progression: at the start of base use intervals about 12 minutes; as adaptation occurs, gradually lengthen them.

### [CTB-L2-021] AnE1: Group Ride

- Goal: simulate racing; anaerobic endurance. Unstructured ride with a group; could also be a B- or C-priority race.
- Structure: frequent maximal efforts. Achieve zone 5 (power) or zone 5b (heart rate) and higher for several seconds to a few minutes, several times in the ride. These surges are "matches" that closely simulate race efforts.
- Intensity anchors: power preferred; heart rate is not indicative of performance.
- Cues / notes: afterward note total time in the goal zone. Be cautious about intensity and road safety; watch traffic and riders unskilled in group riding.

### [CTB-L2-022] AnE2: VO2max Intervals

- Goal: anaerobic endurance / VO2max.
- Structure: flat to rolling course (frequent small hills), no stop signs, light traffic. After a long warm-up, several work intervals of 30 seconds to 4 minutes. Recover with easy zone 1 pedaling for as long as the preceding work interval. As fitness improves, gradually reduce recovery to half the interval duration. Cadence at the high end of comfort range.
- Progression: start with about 5 minutes total work (e.g., 10 × 30 seconds); over several sessions build to 15 to 20 minutes (e.g., 5 × 3 minutes and 5 × 4 minutes).
- Intensity anchors: power zone 5 (power meter preferred). Heart rate lag makes HR ineffective. Without power meter: RPE 9 on a 10-high scale (Table 4.1).

### [CTB-L2-023] AnE3: Pyramid Intervals

- Goal: same as AnE2.
- Structure: same as AnE2 but work interval progression 1-2-3-4-4-3-2-1 minutes in power zone 5. Recovery after each interval equals the preceding work interval duration. Cadence at the high end of comfort range.
- Intensity anchors: power zone 5; without power meter RPE 9 on a 10-high scale. Heart rate ineffective (lag, short intervals).
- Progression: after a few of these or AnE2 workouts, reduce recoveries by as much as half (e.g., 1 minute zone 1 after a 2-minute interval).

### [CTB-L2-024] AnE4: Hill Intervals

- Goal: anaerobic endurance.
- Structure: relatively steep hill, 6 to 8 percent gradient, taking 2 to 4 minutes to climb, light traffic, no stop signs. After a thorough warm-up, 5 to 7 climbs in power zone 5, total 10 to 20 minutes of climbing (e.g., 5 × 2 minutes or 5 × 4 minutes). Steady zone 5 intensity, comfortably high cadence. Recover by coasting down and starting a new interval every 2 to 4 minutes (e.g., 2 minutes recovery after a 2-minute climb).
- Intensity anchors: power zone 5; without power meter RPE 9 on a 10-high scale.
- Variation: include several surges to zone 6 within each interval.
- Cues / notes: very hard; usually best done only once a week and followed by at least 48 hours of recovery.

### [CTB-L2-025] SP1: Form Sprints

- Goal: sprint form and technique.
- Structure: early in a ride after warm-up, 6 to 10 sprints on varying terrain (flat, uphill, downhill). Each 5 to 10 seconds, with 3- to 5-minute recovery. Alternate standing and sitting; focus on posture and technique.
- Intensity anchors: power zone 6, RPE about 9 on a 10-high scale; hold back slightly on intensity.
- Cues / notes: best done alone to avoid competing while refining technique.

src: Appendix B, SP1 | p. 265
evidence: author-experience

### [CTB-L2-026] SP2: Jumps

- Goal: improve explosive power.
- Structure: warm up well. Early in the workout, on indoor trainer or road, 2 to 4 sets of 5 jumps each (10 to 20 jumps total). Each jump is 8 to 12 revolutions of the cranks (each leg) at high cadence. Recover 1 minute after each jump and 5 minutes between sets. On the road vary terrain: flat, uphill, downhill; also include cornering.
- Intensity anchors: power and effort maximal for each jump.

### [CTB-L2-027] SP3: Group Sprints

- Goal: race-like sprinting and tactics.
- Structure: within a ride, several 5- to 15-second race-like sprints, with another rider or a group. Designate sprint zones (e.g., segments marked by road signs). Use all techniques of good form sprints at much higher intensity. Recover several minutes between sprints.
- Intensity anchors: power and effort maximal.

## L3 Exercises & Technique

### [CTB-L3-001] Warm-up and cooldown for strength sessions

- Warm-up step 1: ride a stationary bike 10 to 15 minutes, gradually increasing workload, to raise blood flow to the legs.
- Step 2: before each exercise, do a few reps with light load (e.g., body weight squats) emphasizing technique.
- Cooldown: spin easily at a comfortably high cadence, about 5 minutes, to restore the proper neuromuscular firing sequence after heavy lifting ("pedaling in squares").
tags: type:exercise sport:cycling subtopic:warm-up subtopic:cooldown level:all
evidence: author-experience
src: Ch 12 | p. 202

### [CTB-L3-002] Double-Leg Squat

- Choose with: barbell on shoulders just above scapulae, or dumbbell in each hand, or loaded backpack. Consider weight belt, especially in MS.
- Feet about pedal width apart: 7 to 8 inches (18 to 20 cm) between insteps, pointing straight ahead.
- Back straight, head up, eyes forward (not at feet).
- Squat until thighs almost parallel to floor (knee bend like pedal at 2 o'clock). A low stool/bench can gauge depth; when butt touches, stand.
- Knees point straight ahead over feet.
- Return to start with back straight and head up; do not look down.
tags: type:exercise sport:cycling subtopic:squat system:neuromuscular level:all
evidence: author-experience
src: Ch 12 Fig 12.1 | p. 206

### [CTB-L3-003] Single-Leg Squat

- Advantages: load considerably lighter than double-leg (less injury risk); mimics one leg at a time. Downsides: needs balance; takes more time (twice the sets).
- Barbell across shoulders above scapulae or dumbbell in each hand; stand about 2 feet (60 cm) from a bench/knee-high platform, facing away.
- Reach back with one leg, top of that foot on the bench.
- Back straight, head up; squat on forward leg until thigh almost parallel (knee bend like 2 o'clock pedal). Adjust distance from bench/stance so the forward knee stays over the foot.
- Return to start with eyes forward; repeat with other leg.
tags: type:exercise sport:cycling subtopic:single-leg-squat system:neuromuscular level:all
evidence: author-experience
src: Ch 12 Fig 12.2 | p. 207

### [CTB-L3-004] Dead Lift

- Excellent for leg extension strength but riskier than squats (novices use back). Get expert feedback. With light early-season loads, small plates force low reach and overemphasize back: use dead lift floor racks or adaptor plates to start higher; dumbbells may be better for novices.
- Stand facing barbell, feet about pedal width: 7 to 8 inches (18 to 20 cm).
- Start in full squat, thighs about parallel to floor.
- Back straight, head up, eyes forward (never down); grasp bar with hands slightly wider than feet.
- Stand up using legs only, not back; back straight not rounded.
- Return to start using legs, back straight, head up, eyes forward.
tags: type:exercise sport:cycling subtopic:dead-lift level:all
evidence: author-experience
src: Ch 12 Fig 12.3 | p. 208

### [CTB-L3-005] Step-Up

- Combined hip-knee-ankle extension simulating pedaling; dumbbells somewhat safer (lower center of gravity) than barbell.
- Platform height critical: about 13 to 15 inches (33 to 38 cm); taller athletes need higher bench. Replicates leg position with one pedal at 6 o'clock and other at 12.
- Barbell across shoulders above scapulae or dumbbell in each hand.
- Place one foot on bench; raised knee slightly below hip, thigh about parallel to floor. Do not use higher platform (knee above hip).
- Back straight, head up, eyes forward; step up so both feet are on bench.
- Step back down with the same foot; repeat. Complete all sets for one leg, then the other.
tags: type:exercise sport:cycling subtopic:step-up level:all
evidence: author-experience
src: Ch 12 Fig 12.4 | p. 209

### [CTB-L3-006] Lunge

- Needs floor space. Step distance must be right: too short, can't get low; too long, strains back leg hip flexor.
- Stand tall, feet together, dumbbell in each hand (or barbell across shoulders).
- Step forward, foot flat, toes straight ahead; knee aligned with ankle, weight mostly on that leg; rear leg balances.
- Lower until forward thigh almost parallel to floor; rear knee close to floor.
- Stand and step the back foot forward to return to start.
- Repeat with other leg, alternating until suggested reps are completed with each leg.
- Back straight, head up throughout.
tags: type:exercise sport:cycling subtopic:lunge level:all
evidence: author-experience
src: Ch 12 Fig 12.5 | p. 210

### [CTB-L3-007] Leg Press

- Safest leg extension exercise (machine, no balance, not lifting body weight), so loads can be much heavier.
- Feet on platform pedal-width apart, 7 to 8 inches (18 to 20 cm), pointing straight up (not flared).
- Lowered position: hips, knees, ankles at about the 2 o'clock pedaling bend; knee angle slightly greater than 90 degrees. Use machine locking stop to gauge depth.
- Press up until knees almost straight, just short of lockout.
- Lower to start. Knees in line with feet throughout, never flared out or angled in.
tags: type:exercise sport:cycling subtopic:leg-press level:all
evidence: author-experience
src: Ch 12 Fig 12.6 | p. 211

### [CTB-L3-008] Leg Curl (hamstring balance)

- Purpose: balance hamstrings with quadriceps and help prevent hamstring strains; not a primary power exercise. Balance does not mean 1:1; quads will always be stronger, perhaps twice as much. Women may need it more (greater quad-hamstring imbalance per some research).
- Machine: stand on platform, bring one leg back so ankle contacts lever; curl to about a right angle at the knee; return.
- Home alternative: anchor elastic to a post or sturdy table leg, loop around one ankle; lying prone, curl to about a right angle; return.
- Use smaller loads and more reps than extension exercises in MS (hamstrings fragile): see Table 12.4 (70-80% 1RM, 8-12 reps).
tags: type:exercise sport:cycling subtopic:leg-curl subtopic:hamstrings level:all
evidence: author-experience
src: Ch 12 Figs 12.7-12.8 | p. 212

### [CTB-L3-009] Core exercises: rationale, Front Plank, Side Plank, Front Plank with Rows

Core = muscles from armpits to groin; stabilize spine, support shoulders and hips, transfer force between arms and legs (like a car transmission). Climbing out of the saddle, the arm on the same side pulls up on the bars as the leg drives down; a weak core loses power. A weak core shows as hips rocking side to side (and spine/shoulders swaying) when seated at low cadence near max power. Also reduces lower back problems. "Powerful legs but a weak core is like shooting a cannon from a canoe." A physical therapist assessment (see Ch 5) can show whether core needs work, but it's cheaper to assume it does. Core exercises take few minutes; can be done stand-alone, before/after rides, with any strength approach.

Front Plank: straight line head to toes, no sagging or raised hips; have someone check posture first times. Elbows shoulder-width apart directly below shoulders; arms forward or slightly pointed in. Hold about 30 seconds = 1 rep; rest about a minute; 3 reps per session.

Side Plank: engages different core muscles. Lie on side, one hip on floor, feet stacked, upper body on bent elbow; raise hips; balance on side of lower foot and elbow. Legs, hips, shoulders, head in straight line (assistant or mirror helps). Free hand raised or on hip. Hold 30 seconds, rest about a minute, repeat 2 more times; switch sides and repeat.

Front Plank with Rows (advanced; master front plank first): start as front plank but arms straight (push-up position), weight on hands holding lightweight dumbbells; bike gloves may help palms. Feet shoulder-width apart. Keeping head-to-toe line, slowly lift one weight to shoulder, set it slowly down, then other arm = 1 rep. 5 reps per set, rest about a minute, 3 sets per session. More advanced: widen feet and alternately lift weights to the sides until arm is parallel with floor.
tags: type:exercise sport:cycling subtopic:core-strength level:all
evidence: author-experience
src: Ch 12 Figs 12.9-12.11 | pp. 213-217

### [CTB-L3-010] Power Clean (ballistic lift)

Ballistic lifting builds neuromuscular force and power (force applied rapidly). Any traditional hip-knee-ankle extension exercise may be done explosively (lift fast, lower slowly). Loads 50% of 1RM or less; 3 to 6 reps per set; warm up with very light load (body weight or empty bar). The explosive nature matters more than load, initially. The power clean is the recommended ballistic exercise for explosive cycling power; advanced, requires mastering a complex movement.
Steps:
- Bar near floor, about halfway up shins; knees bent, head up, eyes forward; butt about knee height (position like dead lift).
- Grasp bar with hands slightly wider than thighs, palms down, knuckles forward of the bar.
- One continuous explosive movement of legs, torso, arms: bar to thigh height (heels should come off floor if truly explosive), continue to shoulder height with elbows flaring out; without pausing rotate arms so bar rests on hands with elbows below bar. Nonstop. Use legs, not back.
- Set weight down slowly using legs, not back. Rest a few seconds and repeat. Full recovery between sets is necessary for building power.
- Progress load: broomstick, bar only, then plates; stay under 50% 1RM.
Not for those new to weight lifting.
tags: type:exercise sport:cycling system:neuromuscular subtopic:power-clean subtopic:ballistic level:advanced
evidence: author-experience
src: Ch 12 Fig 12.12 | pp. 218-219
