### [TRPM-L1-002] Bob 16-week plan: block commentary (Weeks 1-16)

- Weeks 1–4: Introduce speed changes and threshold work; threshold remains a primary focus throughout the 16 weeks and on weekends. Big-gear work to address muscular power starts Friday of Week 2 (TEMP-W8). Weekends provide long rides for aerobic fitness; 4-week block finishes with a rest week (Week 4) to adapt.
- Weeks 5–8: Anaerobic capacity focus and longer threshold intervals. Week 6 introduces sprint work and a mock time-trial workout (VO₂-W1) targeting snap and VO2max pacing. Thursdays vary dynamically: rest when recovering from two hard days, or strong workouts during 3-day blocks. Weekend rides progressively lengthen. Week 7 features back-to-back FTP sessions (Tue/Wed); Week 8 is a rest week.
- Weeks 9–12: Begins with comprehensive testing (TEST) while fresh from rest week. Weekend racing and race-specific efforts begin; threshold work continues in races and long rides. This block is critical for muscular and aerobic endurance. Training load builds significantly; Week 12 is a rest week.
- Weeks 13–16: Week 13 opens with three consecutive hard days and ends with a practice 20 km time trial to dial in TT position and equipment (Bob had not previously done threshold work >20 min or in aero position). Week 14 includes hard Anaerobic Capacity work (Wed) and two race/hard training days on the weekend. Week 15 contains threshold and AC sessions plus weekend racing. Week 16 is the final pre-event taper/rest week (Mon rest, Tue–Thu AR-W1 1.25 hr) to restore muscle glycogen and eliminate soreness without losing sharpness. Monday of Week 16 is designated for bicycle maintenance (never make mechanical changes two days before a race). Friday of Week 16 is the race tune-up ride (RACE-W2) before Saturday's criterium/TT and Sunday's road race.

### [TRPM-L1-005] Joe 12-week plan: block commentary

Weeks 1–4: Week 1 Sweet Spot (tempo) workouts x2 plus brick (bike-run) on weekend; rides short to fit swim/run. Week 2 testing: swim (Tue), bike (Wed), run (Sat). Bike test: 5-min VO2max power and 20-min effort; FTP = 95% of 20-min average (5% off) used for rest of plan. The 5-min test exhausts FRC making the 20-min test more accurate; also measures VO2max power, which may exceed Coggan Classic 106–120% of FTP; if he does 150% of FTP for 5 min, use iLevels as basis for training. Pace: hold back in first 2 min of 20-min test. Week 3: AC workout Wed, FTP Fri, 4.5 h Tempo ride Sun, "hour of power" run Sat; easier week. Week 4: reduced intensity and time.
Weeks 5–8: Week 5 VO2max workout Wed combining 3-min and 2-min efforts, AC work at end (unusual; normally most intense work first) to build fatigue resistance for hills. 3-min intervals pace ~115–120% FTP; 2-min intervals target 135% FTP; even if below 135% complete all four intervals. Sunday first "kitchen sink" workout (everything thrown in), 4.5 h; finish tired, muscles twitching/threatening cramp = pushed to max; exhausting muscular endurance without damaging muscle gives great adaptations. Week 6: key FTP workout Wed; Sunday 4.5 h Endurance. Week 7 easier (freshness for final 4 weeks). Week 8: AC workout of 18 intervals in 3 sets of 6 (mental breakthrough, confidence builder; builds fatigue resistance); Friday FTP work in aerobars pushing each interval to limit; Sunday Tempo and Sweet Spot 4.5 h.
Weeks 9–12: Week 9 Wed FTP work also a test; if intervals completed easily at prescribed wattages, raise FTP and adjust zones. Friday solid Tempo; Sunday brick with tough kitchen sink progression then run. Week 10 intensity in all three sports; Friday VO2max workout teaching pacing: two sets of 5 × 3 min at 106% of FTP; Saturday three-sport short triathlon simulation. Week 11: Wednesday full hour at Sweet Spot; Friday classic 2 × 20 min at FTP to test baseline with VO2max intervals mixed in (FTP can be raised with VO2max work; lower VO2max levels raise time near FTP); weekend not overly tough. Week 12 taper: rest Mon and Tue, easy swims/rides/runs, quality workouts later in week; avoid staleness (sluggish heavy legs).

## L2 Workouts & Protocols

### [TRPM-L2-001] Level 1 Active Recovery workouts (Spin; 1 Hour)

- Goal: recover from a hard workout the day before or cool down after a hard one. Flushes waste products, keeps riding rhythm, maintains muscle suppleness.
- When used: recovery days.
- Option 1 "Spin" (about 1.5 h): warm-up 15 min easy, 48–51% FTP, 65% FTHR (Joe under 148 W); main set 1 hr spin, 90–95 rpm, 55% FTP, <68% FTHR (under 160 W); cooldown 15 min <48% FTP, 65% FTHR (under 140 W).
- Option 2 "1 Hour": warm-up 10 min easy 45–48% FTP, 65% FTHR (under 140 W); main 40 min spin at +5–8 rpm over normal cadence, 50–55% FTP, <68% FTHR (145–160 W); cooldown 10 min <48% FTP, 65% FTHR (under 140 W; normal cadence).
- Cues: it should be embarrassingly slow; many elite racers do not ride slow enough. Above the upper limit you are too hard to recover but not hard enough to train. It is OK for watts to exceed 55% a few times, but average power for the ride should be under 55%.

### [TRPM-L2-002] Level 2 Endurance workouts (2.5 Hours; 3.5 Hours with bursts)

- Goal: build endurance base and aerobic fitness. Over time: stronger heart muscle, more mitochondria, more capillaries, more stamina.
- 2.5 Hours: warm-up 15 min easy <56% FTP, 65% FTHR (Joe under 160 W); main 2 hr Endurance 69–75% FTP, 69–83% FTHR (200–220 W), cadence self-selected; cooldown 15 min <56% FTP, 65% FTHR (under 160 W).
- 3.5 Hours: warm-up 15 min easy <65% FTP, 68% FTHR (under 190 W); main 3 hr Endurance 69–75% FTP (200–220 W), 69–83% FTHR, with 8-sec bursts every 10 min, seated, 120 rpm in table (text: rpm to 130 and watts to 300), 103% FTP, 104–105% FTHR; cooldown 15 min <55% FTP, 65% FTHR (under 150 W in text).
- Notes: do enough long Level 2 rides to prepare for harder levels; the Level 3–7 workouts are short, so long Level 2 rides are best for endurance benefits.

### [TRPM-L2-003] Level 3 Tempo workouts (2.5 Hours; Cadence Work; fartlek)

- Role: Tempo gives some of the greatest physiological adaptations ("best bang for the buck"); should feel fast and take work. Do not worry if watts exceed 90% FTP on a few hills or headwinds; average (or normalized) watts matter. Coaches call it "no-man's-land": too much time here makes you good only at Level 3. VO2max power needs VO2max training. Good for limited time (e.g., 3 h/week: drill the upper Level 3), muscular endurance, and preparing for long races (100 miles: 2.5–3 h in this zone).
- 2.5 Hours: warm-up 15 min <68% FTP, <70% FTHR (Joe under 200 W); main 2 hr Tempo 76–90% FTP, 84–94% FTHR (220–260 W), emphasis on time at 240–260 W (82–90%); self-selected cadence; may exceed 260 W but do not sprint hills; cooldown 15 min <55% FTP, <68% FTHR.
- Cadence Work: warm-up 15 min <68%; 40 min Tempo 76–90%; 20 min at −15 rpm (strength endurance); 40 min Tempo; 20 min at +15 rpm (leg speed and muscular endurance), all 76–90% FTP, 84–94% FTHR; cooldown 15 min <55%, <68% FTHR. Steady, smooth, meter effort on hills.
- Fartlek variant: randomly accelerate and vary power within the Tempo wattage to simulate mass-start racing; highly race-specific; make rides shorter since stress is higher than steady Tempo.

### [TRPM-L2-004] Lower Level 4 Sub-Threshold (Sweet Spot) workout

- Sweet Spot: about 88–94% of FTP, on the cusp of Tempo and Lactate Threshold; not an official level but excellent to build FTP. Training effect peaks in upper 80s/low 90s % FTP (Fig 5.2: training effect, maximum duration and physiological strain curves balance there).
- Use: train heavily here at the start of the racing season before moving to FTP work (91–105%); revisit around mid-June for a second peak in the fall; even without a second peak include Sweet Spot once or twice every 14 days.
- Limits: does not significantly help sprint, VO2max power or anaerobic capacity; not best for criteriums; but if all training were here you probably would not get dropped.
- Workout (2–2.5 h): warm-up 15 min <68% FTP, <70% FTHR (<200 W); 5 min all-out/at 100% FTP (290 W), >106% FTHR; main 2 × 20 min (15 min RI) at 88–93% FTP (255–272 W), 95–98% FTHR, cadence self-selected or slightly faster; then 8–10 × 1 min (2 min RI) fast pedaling +105 rpm at 85–95% FTP (under 280 W), 90–98% FTHR; cooldown 15 min <55% FTP, <68% FTHR.
- Progression: start with three 12-min efforts, build to four; restart with three 15-min, build to four; then two 20-min. Cat III need not exceed two 20-min; higher categories build to four 20-min.
- Do at least six to eight of these workouts before specific threshold work; moving to upper Level 4 too soon compromises the foundation.
- Cue: do not hammer hills; keep pedaling pressure steady to the crest.

### [TRPM-L2-005] Level 4 Threshold workouts (Traditional; Ramp; Crisscross)

- Goal: improve FTP, done right at FTP; strenuous, need solid recovery between efforts and workouts.
- Traditional Threshold (2–2.5 h): warm-up 15 min <68% FTP, <70% FTHR (<200 W); 5 min all-out at 100% (290 W), >106% FTHR; 5 min easy <68% (<200 W); 2 × 20 min (10–15 min RI) at 96–105% FTP (288–305 W), 98–106% FTHR; cooldown 15 min <76% FTP (<220 W), <75% FTHR. Alternative: build up to 20-min efforts via the Sub-Threshold ramping strategy at FTP wattage. For a strong Cat III racer, build to at least 1 hour at this power.
- Ramp from Threshold (2 h): warm-up 15 min 68%; 5 min at 100%; recover 5 min <68%; 10 min at 100–107% (>106% FTHR) then increase 10 W each minute until limit (>100% FTHR); try to increase the minutes lasted and ability to hold constant load; cooldown 15 min <76%, <75% FTHR.
- Crisscross (2 h): warm-up 15 min <68%; 5 min blowout at 100%; 5 min recover <70% (<75% FTHR); drill 2–3×: 2 min at 110% (>106% FTHR), 4 min backing off 10 W each min, 2 min building +10 W each min, 7 min steady at 95–105% (try 110% in last 45–60 s); recover well between; cooldown 15 min <68%. Purpose: start hard to load lactate, demand steady output, scale back, hold at/just above threshold, ramp for a final push.

### [TRPM-L2-006] Level 4 Hour of Power (Bill Black)

- Origin: popularized by elite masters athlete Bill Black to combat indoor boredom in Maine winters; a very hard workout (a.k.a. Hour of Pain).
- Structure (1.5 h): warm-up 15 min easy <68% FTP, <70% FTHR; 20 min ramp up to Threshold, 91–105% FTP, 95–105% FTHR (reach 100% by the 20-minute mark); 1 hr steady Threshold at 100% FTP, >100% FTHR; every 2 min a 10-sec burst out of the saddle at 105% FTP, shifting down a gear and raising or dropping cadence 20 rpm; cooldown (text: 10 min; table: 15 min) easy <68%.
- Cadence: 80 rpm to work muscle tension; otherwise self-selected.
- Fig 5.3 example (hour): avg power 333 W, NP 335, max 411, work 1,202 kJ, TSS 96.4, avg HR 150 (max 169), avg cadence 79; HR drifted from ~136 to ~168 bpm, showing possible overheating/dehydration.

### [TRPM-L2-007] Level 5 VO2max workouts (Intervals; Build)

- Goal: improve VO2max; efforts 3–8 min, most at the lower end. Apply intervals-to-exhaustion.
- VO2max Intervals (2 h): warm-up 15 min <68% FTP, <70% FTHR (<200 W); 5 min at 100% (290 W); 5 min easy 68%; 6 × 3 min (3 min RI) at 117% FTP or more (avg 340 W), >106% FTHR; 10 min easy; 4 × 2 min (4 min RI) at 113–120% (330–350 W); cooldown 15 min 68%.

### [TRPM-L2-010] Level 7 Neuromuscular Power workouts (sprints; Micro-burst)

- Nature: efforts under ~10 s, loading the musculoskeletal more than metabolic systems. Do when most fresh. Do not watch the power meter (focus on bike handling); review data afterwards. Get all sprints done and add repetitions as you get stronger.
- Sprint technique: small-chainring sprints usually no gear changes; wind gear out to 120 rpm by end. Big-chainring sprints: one or two shifts, hard jump at start, wind out at 110–120 rpm before shifting. ### [TRPM-L2-025] Power calibration protocol (triathlon pacing)

- Goal: internalize perceived exertion at each power level so pacing on race day is correct.
- When used: before specific triathlon training; the sessions are spread over 10 days.
- Structure: 3–4 × 10-minute intervals with 5 minutes rest between intervals, done 5 times over 10 days at these intensities:

| Session | % of FTP |
| :--- | :--- |
| Day 1 | 70–72 |
| Day 3 | 75–76 |
| Day 5 | 80–82 |
| Day 8 | 88–93 (Sweet Spot) |
| Day 10 | 100 |

- Progression: once each intensity has been done at least twice, lengthen to two 20-minute efforts at each intensity to recalibrate for longer distance. The prescribed intensities are those experienced in the race (chosen by race length). Longer intervals teach you to push more consistently at higher intensities. RPE at the start of an interval differs from RPE at the end.
- Then lengthen to 60-minute efforts and repeat at least two more times through each intensity level.
- After mastering the basics, turn to the specific demands of the event.
- Cues / notes: do only one session a day; more can blur the physical responses to different levels. If possible, use the same road each time to minimize other influences. Make notes after each session.
