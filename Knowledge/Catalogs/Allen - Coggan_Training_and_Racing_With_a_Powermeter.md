> **This is a catalog, not a menu.** These are worked examples and named case studies from *Training and Racing with a Power Meter (Allen & Coggan)*, included for calibration only. They illustrate what a session designed under the author's principles can look like -- never treat this as an exhaustive list to pick from, and never reproduce one verbatim as your own prescription. Binding physiological targets, ratios, and constraints live in `Knowledge/Principles/Allen - Coggan_Training_and_Racing_With_a_Powermeter.md`.

#### Peak Sprint Workouts (referenced as NP-W5, NP-W6 in Appendix)

Small-ring sprints: wind out gear, increase cadence to 120 rpm by end; no gear changes. Big-ring sprints: start with hard jump from ~20 mph, shift 1–2 gears, wind to 110–120 rpm. Do not look at power meter during sprint; review average and peak post-effort.

### Triathlon-Specific Workouts (Ch. 12)

#### Power Calibration Protocol

| Session | Intervals | Duration | Intensity | Rest |
|---------|-----------|----------|-----------|------|
| 5 sessions over 10 days | 3–4 × | 10 min | Variable: sprint-distance race pace, Olympic pace, Half-Ironman pace, Ironman pace | 5 min between |

After calibration, lengthen to 2 × 20 min at each intensity (at least twice), then 60 min at each intensity at least twice. Goal: anchor RPE to power zones for race-day pacing where physiological state differs from training.

#### Ironman Pacing (Rich Strauss/Patrick McCrann formula from Ch. 12)

```
TSS budget for Ironman = 280 (aggressive athletes may use 300 max)
TSS per hour = TSS_budget / race_hours
IF² × 100 = TSS_per_hour → IF = √(TSS_per_hour / 100)
Average NP goal = IF × FTP
```

Hill pacing modifiers (applied to goal NP): <2 min hill → +10% of goal NP; >3 min hill → +5% of goal NP. Target Variability Index 1.04–1.07 indicates smooth execution. "Most triathletes do not understand how easy it is to ride too fast on the bike leg; it's the number one cause of DNFs in triathlons."

### Level 5: VO2max (106–120% FTP)

The authors specify a minimum interval duration of 3 minutes to adequately stress the VO2max system; maximum effective duration is ~8 minutes. "After 8 minutes, it's very difficult, if not impossible, for most people to maintain 106–120 percent of FTP."

#### VO2max Workout 1 — Standard 3-Minute and 2-Minute Set (Ch. 5, Joe Athlete example)

| Segment | Duration | Intensity | Rest |
|---------|----------|-----------|------|
| Warm-up | 15 min | <68% FTP | |
| FTP primer | 1 × 5 min | 100% FTP | 5 min easy |
| Set 1: 3-minute intervals | 6 × 3 min | ≥117% FTP avg | 3 min between |
| Easy cruise | 10 min | Level 2 | |
| Set 2: 2-minute efforts | 4 × 2 min | 113–120% FTP | 4 min between |
| Cooldown | 15 min | Level 1 | |

Total: ~2 hours. Apply intervals-to-exhaustion: stop 3-min efforts when avg falls below ~111% FTP (~5% drop from third interval); stop 2-min efforts when avg falls proportionally.

#### VO2max Workout 2 — Extending Time Trial (Ch. 5, progressive duration)

| Segment | Duration | Intensity | Rest |
|---------|----------|-----------|------|
| Warm-up | 15 min | Level 2 | |
| FTP primer | 1 × 5 min | 100% FTP | 5 min easy |
| Progressive VO2max efforts | 5 efforts: 5 min, 5.5 min, 6 min, 6.5 min, 7 min | 113% FTP (~330W for a 290W FTP rider) | 5–8 min Level 1 between |
| Hard finish | 2 × 3 min | ≥100% FTP all-out | 5 min between |
| Cooldown | 15 min | Level 1 | |

If unable to extend by 30 seconds each interval: reduce intensity by 10–15 watts (~3–5%) but do not go below 106% FTP; start with shorter base duration and build. "Do not reduce the intensity below 106 percent. Instead, go ahead and start with shorter periods of time, but work toward extending the duration."

#### VO2max Workout 3 — Pursuit Intervals Simulation (Ch. 10, Joe TriGuy Week 5)

| Segment | Duration | Intensity | Rest |
|---------|----------|-----------|------|
| Warm-up | 15 min | Level 2 | |
| Set 1: 3-minute VO2max | 5 × 3 min | 115–120% FTP (paced evenly) | 5–8 min Level 1 |
| Set 2: 2-minute AC | 4 × 2 min | ~135% FTP | 5 min Level 1 |
| Cooldown | — | Level 1 | |

Design intent: fatigue VO2max system first, then tax AC system in a fatigued state; builds fatigue resistance for course with multiple short hills. "This workout is designed to build Joe's fatigue resistance so that he'll be able to handle a series of hard hills in his upcoming triathlon."

#### VO2max Workout 4 — Two-Set Pacing Practice (Ch. 10, Joe TriGuy Week 10)

| Segment | Duration | Intensity | Rest |
|---------|----------|-----------|------|
| Warm-up | 15 min | Level 2 | |
| Set 1 | 5 × 3 min | 106% FTP (paced, not all-out) | 5 min between |
| Recovery | 10 min | Level 1 | |
| Set 2 | 5 × 3 min | 106% FTP | 5 min between |
| Cooldown | 15 min | Level 1 | |

Goal is pacing calibration — the deliberate sub-maximal target builds the mental and physical map of what 106% FTP feels like. "Teach him pacing. This workout contains two sets of 5 × 3 minute intervals done at 106 percent of FTP to ensure he's pushing hard and learning to pace himself."

#### VO2max Workout 5 — Final Sharpener (Ch. 10, Jill's Week 7, Sunday)

| Segment | Duration | Intensity | Rest |
|---------|----------|-----------|------|
| Warm-up | 15 min | Level 2 | |
| Fast pedaling × 4 | 4 × 1 min | >100 rpm, cadence focus, moderate power | 1 min between |
| Easy spin | 5 min | Level 1 | |
| AC burst intervals | 6 × 2 min | >135% FTP avg; just explode in last 10 sec | 2 min between |
| VO2max finisher | 1 × 6 min | All-out TT simulation | — |
| Cooldown | 15 min | Level 1 | |

### Level 6: Anaerobic Capacity (121–150% FTP)

All Anaerobic Capacity workouts are performed when relatively fresh in the training week. Apply the intervals-to-exhaustion principle: stop when avg power falls ~10–12% below the third interval's benchmark.

#### AC Workout — Hill Repeats (Ch. 5, general Level 6 ride 2)

| Segment | Duration | Intensity | Rest |
|---------|----------|-----------|------|
| Warm-up | 20 min | Level 2 | |
| Hill repeats | 8–10 × 45 sec–1.5 min | ~140% FTP avg; sprint in last 25 m | 4–5 min between |
| Cooldown | 20–30 min | Level 2 | |

Stop efforts when 10% power drop from third repeat is reached. Hills of 45 seconds–1.5 minutes are described as the optimal range for AC system stress. "Sprint in the last 25 meters or so to explode at the top of each hill."

#### AC Workout — 8+8 Two-Set Format (Ch. 5, general Level 6 ride 1)

| Segment | Duration | Intensity | Rest |
|---------|----------|-----------|------|

| Set 1: 2-minute efforts | ~8 × 2 min | Goal: >135% FTP avg; stop when falls below 120–122% FTP | 2–3 min between |
| Easy cruise | — | Level 1 | |
| Set 2: 1-minute efforts | ~8 × 1 min | Goal: >145% FTP avg; stop when falls below 128–131% FTP | 3 min between |
| Cooldown | — | Level 1 | |

### Level 7: Neuromuscular Power (<10 sec, >150% FTP)

Level 7 workouts are always performed when maximally fresh (beginning of the training week or after full recovery). Do not watch power meter during sprints; start and stop lap timer only. Review avg and peak watts post-sprint.

#### NP Workout — Small Ring and Big Ring Sprints (Ch. 10, Bob Rider Week 2)

**Small-ring sprints (6 reps):**

| Segment | Duration | Intensity | Notes |
|---------|----------|-----------|-------|
| Warm-up | 15 min | Level 2 | |
| Small-ring sprint | 6 × ~8–10 sec | Maximum; wind gear to 120 rpm | No gear changes; seated; accelerate through cadence |
| Rest | 3–5 min easy | Level 1 | Full recovery required for neuromuscular work |

**Big-ring sprints (6 reps, same session):**

| Segment | Duration | Intensity | Notes |
|---------|----------|-----------|-------|
| Big-ring sprint | 6 × ~15–20 sec | Maximum; start from ~20 mph; 1–2 gear changes | Hard jump from saddle; wind gear |
| Rest | 5 min easy | Level 1 | |
| Cooldown | 15 min | Level 1 | |

"Small-ring sprints will improve Bill's explosiveness and the big-ring sprints are designed to improve his fatigue resistance in a longer sprint effort."

#### NP Workout — Micro-burst with Endurance Sprint Combo (Ch. 5, Level 7 variation)

| Segment | Duration | Intensity | Notes |
|---------|----------|-----------|-------|
| Warm-up | 15 min | Level 2 | |
| Endurance + bursts | 60 min | 76–80% FTP base; 10-sec burst every 3 min at 150% FTP, out of saddle | ~20 bursts total |
| Cooldown | — | <80% FTP | Small ring, high cadence |

Total: ~2 hours. Addresses the neuromuscular demand of criterium-style repeated jumping without excessive metabolic fatigue.

### Multi-System / "Kitchen Sink" Workouts

These workouts combine multiple training levels within a single long session. They are described as exhausting muscular endurance across all physiological systems. "Exhausting muscular endurance without damaging muscles will produce some of the greatest adaptations to training."

#### Kitchen Sink Ride 1 — FTP + Endurance with Hill VO2max (Ch. 10, Bob Week 8–9 Saturday style)

| Segment | Duration | Intensity | Notes |
|---------|----------|-----------|-------|
| Warm-up | 30–45 min | Level 2 | |
| FTP intervals | 2 × 20 min | 95–100% FTP | 10 min between |
| Easy cruise | 30 min | Level 2 | Stop for caffeine/sugar if needed |
| Hill VO2max | 5 × hill ~3–5 min | VO2max watts | 3–5 min rest between |
| Endurance with NP bursts | 30 min | Level 2 base; 20-sec burst every 5 min | Cadence 110 rpm during burst |
| Sweet Spot finish | 45 min | 88–93% FTP | |
| Cooldown | 15 min | Level 1 | |

Total: ~4–5 hours. "Your muscles begin to twitch and threaten to cramp toward the end of the ride — that means you have pushed your body to the max during a great workout."

#### Kitchen Sink Ride 2 — WATTS-W5 (Jill's Week 6, Saturday)

| Segment | Duration | Intensity | Notes |
|---------|----------|-----------|-------|

### Race Tune-Up Workout (Appendix code: RACE-W2)

Performed the day before a peak event:

| Segment | Duration | Intensity | Notes |
|---------|----------|-----------|-------|
| Main ride | 1.5 hours | Level 2 (175–200W) | |
| 1-minute hard efforts | 3 × 1 min | Hard, >120% FTP; random placement | 5 min easy between |
| 30-second sprints | 3 × 30 sec | Out of saddle 15 sec; seated drive 15 sec | 5 min easy between; group all sprints together |
| Final easy ride | ≥15 min | Level 1 | Before finishing |

"It is critical that Bob do three hard 30-second sprints on this ride… This last push will get your top-end speed higher and work on your anaerobic capacity without stressing the muscles too much." The sprint structure — 15 sec standing attack + 15 sec seated drive — is explicitly designed to prime the neuromuscular system for race demands without creating residual fatigue.

## Training Plan Case Study Structures (Ch. 10)

### Bob Rider 16-Week Threshold Improvement Plan

**Athlete profile:** Age 42, Cat III, 155 lbs/70.5 kg, FTP 268W (3.8 W/kg), 8–12 hrs/week. Power Profile: moderate inverted V (pursuiter tendency, likely time trialist). Goal: 2-day stage race featuring 10-mile TT + 25-mile criterium (Saturday) and 50-mile road race with 5-mile climb (Sunday).

**Limiters identified:** Below-average explosive NP (Level 7), poor 10–20 min intensity fatigue resistance (Level 4/5 transition), poor muscular force.

| Block | Weeks | Focus |
|-------|-------|-------|
| Block 1 | 1–4 | Speed changes + threshold base + big-gear NP (Fridays) + progressive long weekend rides |
| Block 2 | 5–8 | AC development + sprint work + VO2max TT simulation + extended weekend endurance |
| Rest week | 4, 8, 12 | CTL stabilization; ATL reduction |
| Block 3 | 9–12 | Mid-season testing + race-specific efforts + increasingly long weekend rides with VO2max work |
| Block 4 | 13–16 | Three-day training blocks + 20km practice TT + final taper; tune-up day before peak event |

Weekly structure: Monday rest; Tuesday–Thursday = structured workouts (hard–hard–moderate); Friday = Active Recovery or specific NP/sprint work; Saturday = long group ride or race; Sunday = medium ride or race.

### Jill Racer 8-Week Peak Performance Plan

**Athlete profile:** Age 32, 5-year racer, 130 lbs/59.1 kg, FTP 221W (3.74 W/kg), 8–12 hrs/week. Power Profile: flat all-rounder. Goal: win state criterium championship.

**Limiters identified:** Below-average sprint (Level 7), extreme fatigue resistance failure at 5–28 seconds (long sprint), poor fatigue resistance in fast-twitch fibers during Level 6 efforts beyond 1:30. Possible sprinting technique issue.

| Block | Weeks | Focus |
|-------|-------|-------|
| Block 1 | 1–4 | Crisscross LT, sprint + AC, race-winning intervals, rest week (Week 4) |
| Block 2 | 5–8 | Escalating intensity: double Tuesday, kitchen sink Saturday, VO2max sharpener (Week 7 Sunday), AC tune-up (Week 8 Tuesday), final taper |

Weekly structure same as Bob. Plan B/C variations based on weekend race schedule. "Do not overdo it in the rest week — if she does, she won't reap the benefits of her hard work."

### Joe TriGuy 12-Week 70.3 Triathlon Plan

**Athlete profile:** Age 38, 180 lbs/81.8 kg, FTP 270W (3.3 W/kg), 70.3 target. Power Profile: classic time trialist / triathlete (upward sloping). Limiters: NP and AC below novice until 1 minute; VO2max needs development for short hilly sections.

**Note:** Sprint and NP training explicitly deprioritized — "doesn't make sense to try and improve [Level 7] or even spend time training at Level 7." Training time better spent on FTP.

| Block | Weeks | Focus |
|-------|-------|-------|
| Block 1 | 1–4 | Sweet Spot intro; testing week (Week 2: 5-min + 20-min FTP test); AC + FTP work; rest week |
| Block 2 | 5–8 | VO2max combo workout; kitchen sink long rides; rest week (Week 7) |
| Block 3 | 9–12 | FTP verification; peak intensity three-sport; final taper (Week 12: two full rest days Mon–Tue) |

Includes swim and run workouts (not detailed in power-meter context). Combined PMC management across all three sports required for taper planning.

### Bill Masters 12-Week Gran Fondo Plan

**Athlete profile:** Age 58, 150 lbs/68.2 kg, FTP 235W (3.45 W/kg), 6–9 hrs/week. Three major climbs: 45, 30, 18 minutes. Power Profile: time trialist/climber (upward sloping). WAC score peak at 74 at ~22:48; drops 43% at 5 hours (endurance is primary limiter). Quadrant Analysis on prior climbs shows 40% in QII — over-relying on force at expense of glycogen; needs to shift cadence into 70s–80s on long climbs.

**Limiters:** Endurance and stamina (primary); AC and NP secondary.

| Block | Weeks | Structure |
|-------|-------|-----------|
| Block 1 | 1–5 | Jump into intensity immediately; 5-hour ride at end of Week 5 |
| Rest | Week 6 | Full rest week |
| Block 2 | Week 7 | Hard (VO2max + FTP) |
| Rest | Week 8 | Second rest week (unconventional) |
| Block 3 | 9–11 | Final hard block: AC + VO2max + Sweet Spot; 5-hour kitchen sink rides; fatigue resistance focus |
| Taper | Week 12 | Reduce volume and intensity; carbohydrate loading; legs should "twitch with energy" |

"This plan does **not** follow the classic 'three weeks on and one week off' periodization cycle." The double rest at Weeks 6 and 8 is designed to allow deep adaptation before the final hard block. Higher training stress tolerance from the first five weeks requires more recovery to convert to fitness gains.

### Power Calibration Protocol — Full Sequence (Ch. 12)

| Phase | Sessions | Structure |
|-------|---------|-----------|
| Phase 1 — Calibration | 5 sessions over 10 days | 3–4 × 10-min intervals; each session at a single target race intensity; one session per day maximum; always same road |
| Phase 2 — Lengthening | At least 2 rounds | Lengthen to 2 × 20 min at each intensity level; repeat at least twice |
| Phase 3 — Extended | At least 2 more rounds | Lengthen to 60 min at each target intensity; repeat at least twice |

Intensities to calibrate against: sprint-distance race pace, Olympic-distance race pace, half-Ironman race pace, Ironman race pace. "After you have mastered the basics in pacing, turn your focus to the specific demands of the event."
