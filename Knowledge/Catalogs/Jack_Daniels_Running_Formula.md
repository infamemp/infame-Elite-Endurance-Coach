### [DRF-L1-005] Sample season plan: fall cross country (Ch 10)

- Phase I: Sunday = L run; all other days E runs (+ strides on 3 days).
- Phase II: Sunday = L; Q1 = session of R 200s; Q2 = combination of R 200s and R 400s; Q3 = session of R 400s; other days E.
- Phase III: Sunday = L; Q1 = I session of repeated 1,000 m; Q2 = steady 20-minute run at T pace + 4 × R 200s; Q3 = race or session of I 1,200s.
- Phase IV: Sunday = L; Q1 = T plus some R 200s; Q2 = T; Q3 = race or combination of T, I, and R; if a race that week is important, Q2 = the race and no Q3.
Design the plan well ahead; adjust for race dates, weather, schedules, setbacks; training speeds may be adjusted to current fitness. Each phase introduces one new stress and maintains previous benefits.

### [DRF-L2-005] Step-count workout and 20 × 200 windy-day interval session

Step-count workout:
- Goal: I-type session by time, equalizing runners of different ability.
- Structure: run hard 10 right footfalls, jog 10; then 20/20, 30/30, 40/40 ... up to 100/100; repeat 100/100; come back down 90/90, 80/80 ... 10/10.
- Total duration: about 24-25 min; distance 3-4 miles (slower runners less, faster slightly more).
20 × 200 (windy days when interval 1,000s are hard to time):
- Start one 200 each minute; if I pace is 40 s per 200, rest is 20 s. Slower runners: limit recovery to a set time, about half as long as the fast runs.

### [DRF-L2-008] Treadmill Uphill Interval Workouts (30/30 and 60/60)

- Goal: Aerobic capacity (V̇O₂max) stress using steep grades and slower belt speeds to reduce running velocity demand.
- When used: Quality interval days, adverse weather, or when training on treadmills with limited maximum speed.
- Structure:
  - Warm-up: Steady easy running.
  - Option A: 20 × 30 seconds hard uphill running with 30 seconds rest (off the belt).
  - Option B: 10 × 1 minute hard uphill running with 1 minute rest (off the belt).
  - Cool-down: Steady easy running.
- Intensity anchors: I-pace equivalent effort achieved via grade and speed combinations (see Table 7.1).
- Total duration: 20 minutes main set work/rest time.
- Progressions / variations: Start with Option A (20 × 30s/30s); if that is not too demanding, progress to Option B (10 × 1 min / 1 min).
- Cues / notes: Can be performed with two runners alternating work and rest on a single treadmill. Hopping off the belt during rest allows uphill-only benefits without downhill pounding.

### [DRF-L2-010] Cross Country Hill and Combination Quality Workouts

Workout 1: Phase II Hill Repetitions (R Pace)
- Goal: Develop speed, power, and running economy for hilly cross country terrain.
- When used: Phase II (Quality session Q1, Q2, or Q3). Stop all hill work in the final 2 weeks before important races.
- Structure:
  - Warm-up: 1-2 miles E running + strides.
  - Main set: 8 × 60-second uphill runs at R effort (running by feel and time, not distance). Full recovery jogging/walking back down. Route down must NOT be steep.
  - Flat finish: Conclude with 2-4 flat R-pace runs (or strides) to restore the sensation of fast leg turnover.
  - Cool-down: 1-2 miles E running.
- Intensity anchors: R effort by time/feel uphill; flat R pace for concluding reps.
- Cues / notes: Do not measure distance uphill; measure time.

Workout 2: Phase III Time-Based Interval Session (No-Race Week Option A)
- Goal: VO2max stimulus over undulating or cross country terrain without pace pressure.
- When used: Phase III, Q3 on weeks without a competition.
- Structure: 2 miles E + 5-6 × 3 min H (Hard / I effort) w/ 2 min easy jogging recovery + 2 miles E.
- Intensity anchors: H pace (equivalent to I effort / 95-100% HRmax).

Workout 3: Phase III T-I-R Mixed Session (No-Race Week Option B)
- Goal: Multi-pace stimulus combining threshold clearance, aerobic power, and neuromuscular speed.
- When used: Phase III, Q3 on weeks without a competition.
- Structure:
  - 2 miles E warm-up
  - 2 miles @ T pace (or 2 × 1 mile T w/ 1 min rest)
  - 3 × 2 min H w/ 1 min jog recovery
  - 4 × 200 m R w/ 200 m jog recovery
  - 1-2 miles E cool-down.
- Intensity anchors: T pace, H effort, R pace.

Workout 4: Steve Prefontaine 30/40 Track Workout (Historical Reference)
- Goal: High-intensity anaerobic and aerobic endurance conditioning.
- Structure: 3 miles continuous on track, alternating 200 m in 30 seconds and 200 m in 40 seconds (total 24 × 200 m continuous).
- Intensity anchors: 30 s per 200 m (~4:00/mile pace) alternating with 40 s per 200 m (~5:20/mile pace).

## L3 Exercises & Technique

### [DRF-L3-001] Bodyweight circuit routine (10 stations)

Setup: stations 1 and 4 use half of your 1-minute maximum; first establish your 1-minute maximum push-ups and crunches.
1. Push-ups: half of 1-min max. Plank on hands and toes, arms straight; lower until chest nearly touches floor; push back up.
2. Side leg lifts: 10 each leg. Lie on side, legs straight, supported on forearm with elbow bent; raise top leg so foot is above shoulder height; return.
3. High knees: 30 per leg, running in place; quickly bring one knee up, alternate for continuous motion.
4. Crunches: half of 1-min max. On back, knees bent, feet on floor; hands behind head (without holding it), behind ears, or across chest; steadily raise head and shoulders to upright and return.
5. Recovery run: 1 minute or 400 m (not fast).
6. Stretching: 2 minutes, any kind.
7. Squat thrusts (burpees): 10. From standing drop to squat, hands on ground outside feet; jump legs back to push-up position; jump back to squat; jump to standing = 1 rep.
8. Leg lifts: 10 each leg. First resting on forearms facing upward with hips off ground; bend one leg for balance, lift other to at least height of bent leg. Then turn over, rest on forearms facing down; keeping one leg straight, raise the other as far as comfortable. 10 each leg.
9. Arm and leg flapping: lie on belly, arms over head, legs straight; alternately flap arms and legs up and down until each raised and lowered 20 times.
10. Recovery run: 2 minutes or 800 m.
Prescription: go through the whole circuit 3 times, preferably 2 or 3 times per week. Stations 5 and 10 are recovery runs, not particularly fast, to recover before the next resistance station. Time the full three rounds every few weeks; you should get faster.
