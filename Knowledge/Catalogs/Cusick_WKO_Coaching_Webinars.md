## L2 Workouts & Protocols

### [WKOC-L2-001] The Grind (tempo with cadence sections)

- Goal: tempo development plus cadence/muscle memory and maintaining cadence as fatigue grows.
- When used: phase 1 (extensive aerobic).
- Structure: WU 15 min; MS1 30 min level 2; MS2 45-min tempo divided into 3 equal sections: first 15 min low cadence (65-75), next 15 medium (75-90), last 15 high (90+); MS3 30 min level 2; CD 5-15 min spin.
- Intensity anchors: tempo (iLevel 3); level 2 for fillers.
- Total duration: about 2 h 15 min plus WU/CD.
- Progressions: 1x45 -> 50 -> 55 -> 60 min; repeat a duration only if 45 was a struggle; always progress.
- Cues: low-to-high order avoids teaching the body to grind in winter (Zwift); starting high and ending low is counter-efficiency.

### [WKOC-L2-002] Long tempo

- Goal: extensive aerobic development; extend time at tempo.
- When used: phase 1.
- Structure: WU 15 min; MS1 30 min level 2; MS2 steady-state tempo targeted at a % goal (85%) of FTP, going longer and longer (target 150-200% of TTE); CD 5-15 min spin.
- Intensity anchors: 85% of FTP.
- Progressions: 45 min -> 1 h -> 1:15 -> 1:30 and beyond; don't cap tempo at an hour. Author has a pro doing 4 h of tempo; another pro doing 7 h of tempo was part of his problem; don't do 7 h of tempo.
- Cues: continue progressing to keep improving.

### [WKOC-L2-003] TAN tempo (Tough As Nails)

- Goal: intensive aerobic; mixed-pace tempo mimicking being in a break, not a crit.
- When used: phase 2.
- Structure: WU 15 min; MS1 30 min level 2; MS2 mixed-pace tempo targeted at 85% of FTP; sustain until 5% continual power drop-off; should be sustained longer than 150% of TTE; CD 5-15 min spin.
- Intensity anchors: target average power (not normalized power) at 85% FTP.
- Cues: stay seated, surge out of turns and up hills, no standing sprints at every turn and no huge VI; find a course and try to go a bit faster/add power each time. Variation is good versus holding a perfect "drone". Origin: Coggan's name; author's old training loops.

### [WKOC-L2-004] TTE FTP intervals

- Goal: intensive aerobic FTP work.
- When used: phase 2.
- Structure: WU 15 min; MS1 30 min level 2; MS2 break 110-150% of TTE (TTZ) into 2 or 3 intervals targeted at 94-105% of FTP; CD 5-15 min spin. Advanced: FTP interval targeted at 105% of TTE (TTZ).
- Intensity anchors: start at about 110% of TTE; 94-105% FTP.
- Progressions: modality by rider (4x10, 2x15; 40K TT specialist 2x20 -> 2x30 -> 1x50; surgers 3x10 -> 4x10 -> 5x10).

### [WKOC-L2-005] Max aerobic intervals and Tabata-style HI intervals (phase 3)

- Goal: raise VO2max (the ceiling) / max aerobic power.
- When used: phase 3, 2 (max 3) hard days per week.
- Structure A "Max Aerobic": WU 15 min; 3-7 max aerobic intervals (3-7 minutes, per optimized intervals) with 1:1 recovery; CD 5-15 min.
- Structure B "Max Aerobic HI": WU 15 min; 6 x [30 s on / 15 s off repeated 8 times (6 min)]; CD 5-15 min.
- Intensity anchors: on-periods at FRC/FTP target or higher, hard but not sprint; self-regulates by the 2nd-3rd rep.
- Cues: no tested performance difference between steady and Tabata formats (steady = slightly better cardiovascular response/time near VO2max; Tabata = slightly higher neuromuscular response); choose whichever you like or produce more power in. Classic Tabata is 20/10 (hard to execute); 30/15 easier. Do not rest more than 15 s (literature max 20 s; people cheat, so 15); 40/15 or 40/20 acceptable; do not extend on-period beyond 40 s (power drops); 30/30 crashes. Soft-pedal during off; don't stare at the power meter.

### [WKOC-L2-006] Base miles (progressive, no fade)

- Goal: base aerobic foundation without junk or long slow miles.
- When used: Phase 1 (aerobic foundation).
- Structure: steady riding within the level 2 range, progressive: start lower and finish equal or slightly higher, never softer. Example: instead of 199 W then 185, 170, 165 W, go 165 → 170 → 175 W (zone example 200–210 W is hypothetical).
- Intensity anchors: level 2, subtle increase through the ride.
- Cues: fights fatigue and breaks the habit of going out too hard and finishing soft.

### [WKOC-L2-007] The Grind (cadence-sectioned tempo)

- Goal: tempo with changing force/cadence; more aerobic demand at equal power; learn to spin when tired.
- When used: Phase 1 (tempo).
- Structure: WU 15 min; MS1 30 min level 2; MS2 45-min tempo in 3 equal cadence sections: low 65–75, medium 75–90, high 90+ (60 min = 20-20-20; 90 min = 30-30-30); MS3 30 min level 2; CD 5–15 min spin. (Text mentions warm-up 30 min of level two with 15, 10 or 5 as time-based options.)
- Intensity anchors: tempo; cadence 65–75 / 75–90 / 90+ rpm.
- Cues: low cadence early engages slower-twitch fibers; rising cadence at steady power raises aerobic stress; psychological benefit of spinning when tired.

### [WKOC-L2-008] Long tempo (TTE-targeted)

- Goal: extend TTE and fatigue resistance via longer steady tempo.
- When used: Phase 1, athletes with time; fits classic periodization with level 3 and SST work.
- Structure: WU 15 min; MS1 30 min level 2; MS2 steady-state tempo at a % goal of FTP (85%; range 82–87%), duration 150–200% of TTE; CD 5–15 min spin.
- Intensity anchors: 85% FTP (82–87%); duration start 150% of TTE (norm for most in tempo zone), recreational riders toward 200%; one pro rides 400% of TTE.
- Progression: lengthen each workout (e.g. 45 → 50 → 55 min); keep power, extend time (progression as overreach pushing the curve right); as TTE lengthens raise the target % of TTE (150 → 160 → 170 → 180). Track that it is effective.

### [WKOC-L2-009] TAN tempo ride (Tough as Nails)

- Goal: intensive aerobic mixed-pace tempo; extend fatigue resistance (also for ultra/mixed events).
- When used: Phase 2.
- Structure: WU 15 min; MS1 30 min level 2; MS2 mixed-pace tempo mimicking a break (not a crit): surges, attacks, ups and downs, VI around 1.1 (not 1.5); CD 5–15 min spin.
- Intensity anchors: target 85% of FTP as normalized power; sustain until about 5% continual power drop-off (not brief dips); should exceed 150% of TTE.
- Notes: Cusick rides a metric-century rolling course at high tempo and goes deeper as he gets fit; not a breakthrough workout, but specific for category racers and recreational riders.

### [WKOC-L2-010] TTE SST and FTP intervals

- Goal: avoid more-more-more by basing SST volume on TTE and extending time beyond it.
- When used: Phase 2.
- Structure: WU 15 min; MS1 30 min level 2; MS2 total time in zone (TTZ) = 110–125% of TTE, split into 2 or 3 intervals at 89–93% of FTP; CD 5–15 min spin. Alternative FTP work: TTZ about 105% of TTE in short, more intense intervals of 10–15 min at 103–107% of FTP (usually 105%), upper edge of FTP/FRC zone.
- Notes: TTE should lengthen as you train, so targets move up. [Chapter note: these ranges were later superseded; see Appendix A.]

### [WKOC-L2-011] FTP/FRC and FRC/Pmax edge intervals (phase 3)

- Goal: raise the VO2max ceiling; extensive to intensive anaerobic training, used alongside FTP building once aerobic gain is maximized.
- Extensive version: WU 15 min; 2 sets of 5 × FRC/FTP edge intervals with limited recovery (1.5 or 2 to 1; e.g. 2-min interval → 3–4 min rest); CD 5–15 min spin. Do 2–3 microcycles, then move to intensive.
- Intensive version: WU 15 min; 6 × short FRC/Pmax edge intervals with FULL RECOVERY (7–10 min); CD 5–15 min.
- Intensity anchors: edge of iLevel 5 FRC/FTP (e.g. 619 W for 1 min for that athlete); do not target the full edge value ("back it off"). Use power duration AND iLevels to guide; overload one way or another.
- Notes: "go hard, puke, go home" (Coggan).

### [WKOC-L2-012] FTP speed work, moto pacing and training races (phase 4)

- Goal: translate built capacity into speed; the last pickup once the "house" is taller.
- Content: FTP speed work, moto pacing, over-intervals, training races, racing. Moto pacing makes faster (not necessarily stronger); training races are an underused option to develop speed.
- Safety: moto pacing is inherently dangerous; needs two people who know what they are doing (one on scooter, one on bike).

### [WKOC-L2-013] Max long intervals (acute aerobic)

- Goal: raise VO2max; accumulate time at VO2max with sustained near-max aerobic effort.
- When used (period/phase, prerequisites): acute phase (end of build), after a deep chronic base.
- Structure: optimized max aerobic target or own build; intervals > 2-3 min (most use 3 min; flat power curves can use shorter and harder); target max aerobic ~2.5 min to 8 min (> 2.5 min and < 8 min). Recovery controlled ~1:1, not over 5 min, ACTIVE (keep pedaling at about a third of the watts, on flat or low grade; avoid always climbing then coasting down, as passive recovery loses residual VO2max and changes lactate buffering). Longer recovery -> more active; shorter -> may be passive. As fitness increases, max rest decreases to <= 3 min.
- Intensity anchors: under FRC power targets; >= 90-110% vVO2max (master chart).
- Total duration: 7-10 minutes (maybe 7-12) NET time at VO2max.
- Progressions / variations: low degradation between intervals; if degradation occurs extend rest (e.g. 4 min rest to 5 min; 3 min rest to 5 min), never more than 5 min; strain persists even as watts fall; stop when there is a significant drop-off. Planning is power and time progressive, focus on power increase.
- Cues / notes: it takes 1-2 min of effort to reach VO2max, so a 5-min interval may give only 3-4 min at VO2max; estimate 60-70% of prescribed time at VO2max (given right power target and completion). Track time at VO2max, not time in zone.

### [WKOC-L2-014] Max short intervals (acute aerobic)

- Goal: build capacity via full-max efforts at FRC/Pmax with full recovery, accumulating time at VO2max.
- When used: acute phase.
- Structure: optimized Pmax/FRC or FRC target, or fully maximal intervals under 45 seconds; extended recovery 7-12 minutes (passive per master chart) because the whole energy system must recover; no/low degradation between intervals.
- Intensity anchors: Pmax/FRC to FRC max; < 45 s (>= FRC effort).
- Total duration: 7-10 minutes at or above VO2max (not time in zone; high correlation).
- Progressions / variations: none stated.
- Cues / notes: sprint TECHNIQUE matters to hit true max (even pros often lack it); patience is the challenge (many intervals with long rests; more common on the track than road). The author says it feels long but after about three intervals athletes are glad to have 7-10 min to accumulate; it is physically hard but you recover fully.

### [WKOC-L2-015] Repeated sprint training (RST)

- Goal: accumulate VO2max time via micro/undulating on-off sprints (Tabata-style; also called micro, undulating).
- When used: transitionally from chronic to acute; NOT used heavily in acute because it can be the least effective of the four, especially for fit athletes.
- Structure: on portion 5-30 s (less than 5 too little; more than 30 too much); recovery < 30 s (author prefers 20 s; Tabata well tested at 20; some research supports 30). If recovery < 15 s: passive (coast / very soft pedaling); if > 15 s: active at about 50-70% of VO2max. 7-12 min between sets (master chart).
- Intensity anchors: max average; 100-120% vVO2max to elicit high VO2 response (target). Examples: 30 on/30 off, 20 on/20 off.
- Total duration: target 7-15 minutes at VO2max; only ~10-50% of prescribed total time will be at VO2max, depending on format.
- Progressions / variations: planning is power and time progressive but focus on time.
- Cues / notes: athlete fitness is a major factor: the fitter the athlete, the lower the time at VO2max (faster recovery, slower lactate and aerobic demand ramp). Tip: consider RST carefully in highly fit athletes. Track with VO2max charts; very individual. Warns that high-intensity-only approaches help unfit people fast but then plateau and can decline without an aerobic foundation.

### [WKOC-L2-016] Sprint interval training (SIT)

- Goal: build the peripheral side of aerobic function via high muscle O2 demand and lactate handling.
- When used: acute phase, a toolkit item.
- Structure: sprints at or just below Pmax/FRC optimization, or 20-30 s all out; short controlled recoveries < 3 min (about 1 min typical).
- Intensity anchors: max average; >= 90-110% vVO2max (target); Pmax/FRC to FRC; < 45 s.
- Total duration: 7-12 minutes of prescribed work.
- Progressions / variations: planning is interval progressive; more is more until it is not.
- Cues / notes: higher blood lactate accumulation; residual VO2 carried over; progressive shift to oxidative metabolism with repeats; low time at VO2max observed (power falls because of incomplete rest) but very high muscle O2 demand.

### [WKOC-L2-017] EPD SST intervals (explosive power development sweet spot)

- Goal: teach the body to deal with accumulation of metabolic waste (lactate etc.) at a high rate of exercise; fatigue resistance (accumulation side).
- When used: base period, fatigue resistance workouts.
- Structure: WU 10–15 min progressing to zone 2 (PW Z2 / HR zone 2 / PE 4–5), cadence self-selected with 2 × 1 min fast pedals. MS1: 2 × 16 min EPD SST intervals: start each with 3 × 12 s max sprint with 24 s very light pedaling recovery between sprints; IMMEDIATELY after the third sprint (no rest) complete a 15-minute interval in the FTP zone (PW Z4 / HR Z4 / RPE 6–7). Rest 10 min between intervals, easy riding. CD 5–15 min easy in L1 (power Z1, HR Z1, RPE <2).
- Intensity anchors: sprints max; main 15 min at PW Z4 / HR Z4 / RPE 6–7.
- Total duration: not stated.
- Cues: cadence self-selected but do not let RPM drop as you fatigue.

### [WKOC-L2-018] SST crisscross intervals

- Goal: vary efforts to teach the body to clear lactate.
- When used: base fatigue resistance.
- Structure: same WU (10–15 min to zone 2, 2 × 1 min fast pedals). MS1: 2 × 20 min crisscross sweet spot intervals: target 88–93% of FTP (PW level 3.5 / HR zone 3.5 / PE 6–7); every 2 minutes increase effort to 110% of FTP for 30 seconds, then return to SSI power; do not drop off (below 88% of FTP) or recover until the interval is complete. CD 5–15 min.
- Intensity anchors: 88–93% FTP, surges 110% FTP × 30 s.
- Notes: cadence self-selected. Related: TAN (tempo and normalized power): ride tempo/SST in an up-down fashion (attack out of a corner, settle in), targeting normalized power; steady state can buff off neuromuscular power.
- Total duration: rest between intervals not stated [INCOMPLETE IN SOURCE].

### [WKOC-L2-019] Optimized extensive FRC and optimized intensive Pmax/FRC workouts

- Goal: build anaerobic fatigue resistance by driving FRC battery size.
- When used: build phase (4–8 weeks; typically no more than 6 weeks of high intensity).
- Structure:
  - Warm-up: 10–15 min progressing to zone 2 (PW: iLevel Z2), cadence self-selected, with 2 × 1 min fast pedals.
  - Main set (Extensive FRC): **6 × extensive FRC** at target FRC duration and intensity from the WKO4 optimized interval chart. Standing start for 5–10 s to get up to power, then completed seated. Ride all other time in zone 2.
  - Main set (Intensive Pmax/FRC): **7 × intensive Pmax/FRC** at target Pmax/FRC duration and intensity from the optimized interval chart. Standing start for 5–10 s, then completed seated. Ride all other time in zone 2.
  - Cool-down: 5–15 min easy (PW: iLevel Z1), small chainring.
- Rest intervals: Slide 41 lists a flat 7 min rest between intervals, but the author noted in the webinar Q&A that this 7-minute figure was a slide typo for extensive FRC repeats and should follow the optimized interval chart ratio (**1:10 to 1:20 work-to-rest ratio range**). Full recovery is required because repeated anaerobic efforts deplete ATP, creatine phosphate, and glycogen; shorter sprints can use somewhat less rest.
- Progressions: Progress the number of completed repetitions (e.g., if the athlete completes 6 and fails on 7, start at 6 and systematically build to 7, 8, then 9 reps). Do not over-exhaust the athlete beyond their mental recovery threshold.

### [WKOC-L2-020] HVLIT workouts: Aerobic Endurance Progressive; Aerobic Power Cadence Ramps

- Goal: build aerobic endurance progressively; aerobic power with cadence work.
- When used: early base / HVLIT period.
- Structure (Aerobic Endurance: Progressive): stepped warm-up, three long steady blocks each stepping slightly higher; start a little easier and finish a little stronger (change of 5-10 W). 1:00:00, 38 TSS (slide example Feb 18, 2021).
- Structure (Aerobic Power: Cadence Ramps): warm-up, repeated short blocks of alternating slightly higher/lower power while ramping cadence. 1:33:00, 72 TSS (example Mar 31, 2021).
- Intensity anchors: aerobic endurance at or near LT1 (do not be too precise); aerobic power just above LT1 (extra watts come from cadence work).
- Cues / notes: avoid the Zwift group-ride habit of going out first 8-10 minutes about 10% harder than the settle pace (unless Zwift racing). Be progressive. Exact interval durations are not given in text.

### [WKOC-L2-021] Evolution-phase workouts: Fatigue Resistance Tempo (30) and transition tempo intervals

- Goal: evolve aerobic power into late tempo; build fatigue resistance by placing intervals late in the workout (body is tuned to intervals early; big mental/psychological/physiological effect).
- When used: transition from HVLIT to pyramidal, before regular tempo.
- Structure: transitionary example: 6 minutes of tempo as intervals with 1-minute rest (not the best way to do tempo, but mentally prepares; one, maybe two of these). Slide example: Fatigue Resistance Tempo (30): long steady endurance section first, then five tempo intervals with short recoveries late in the workout; 2:00:00, 112 TSS (Apr 1, 2021).
- Intensity anchors: tempo (iLevel 3).
- Progressions: ABP (always be pushing): coach finds the fastest progression the athlete can manage while still fresh enough to recover and adapt. If the same workout is done more than three times, you are not pushing enough, or threshold/iLevels are wrong.

### [WKOC-L2-022] Pyramidal-phase workouts: Tempo Hour of Power, Over/Under SST, Hard Start SST

- Goal: intensive aerobic work in tempo and sweet spot.
- When used: deep in the pyramidal cycle (mid/late base); about 3 times per week as the basis, sometimes 2, sometimes a 4th depending on athlete.
- Structure: Tempo Hour of Power (60 min): long tempo block broken by regular short surges; 1:45:00, 119 TSS. Sweet Spot Hard Start Intervals: two sweet-spot intervals each opening with a short spike above threshold (flushes lactate, forces better lactate management), separated by recoveries; 1:20:00, 91 TSS. Sweet Spot with FTP Surges: two sweet-spot blocks with periodic short surges to FTP; 1:15:00, 86 TSS. Classic over/under approaches also.
- Notes: exact interval lengths and watts not given; workouts are thinking examples, not prescriptions.
