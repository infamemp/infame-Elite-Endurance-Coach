> **This is a catalog, not a menu.** These are worked examples and named case studies from *Hansons Half-Marathon Method (Keith and Kevin Hanson)*, included for calibration only. They illustrate what a session designed under the author's principles can look like -- never treat this as an exhaustive list to pick from, and never reproduce one verbatim as your own prescription. Binding physiological targets, ratios, and constraints live in `Knowledge/Principles/Hansons_Half_Marathon_Method.md`.

## 8. Structured Plans / Programs

**PLAN-001 — Beginner Program**
- For: new to half-marathon but with base (e.g., ~30 mi/wk, several 5K/10Ks), OR prior half-marathoners who used minimal plans. Not for absolute newbies (need base first).
- Duration: 18 weeks.
- Structure: initial 4 weeks base-building (all easy, no SOS) → speed + tempo block → strength + longer tempo + peaking long runs → 10-day taper.
- Mileage: starts 10 mi/wk → builds to upper 40s peak (~45). ~30–35 avg.
- Long run: begins 10 mi, +≤2 mi/wk, peaks 12 mi.
- Tempo: 3 → 6 mi (6–7 mi at pace at peak).
- Warm-up/cooldown: 1–2 mi each.
- SOS days: Tue (speed→strength), Thu (tempo), Sun (long).

**PLAN-002 — Advanced Program**
- For: ≥1 previous half-marathon, OR high-mileage runners (~50+ mi/wk) new to the distance.
- Duration: 18 weeks.
- Structure: SOS begins in Week 1 (speed + tempo from the start; no separate base block). Speed early because runners often race shorter events concurrently.
- Mileage: higher from Week 1; peaks just over 55 mi/wk (text also "around 50"); ~40 avg. Extra mileage comes from longer weekday EASY runs, not more SOS.
- Long run: peaks 14 mi (a 10-mi run counts as merely "easy" here, vs "long" in Beginner).
- Tempo: up to 7 mi (vs Beginner's 6). Peak tempo day (incl. WU/CD) 12–14 mi / ~90 min.
- Speed sequence: 12×400 → 8×600 → 6×800 → 5×1000 → 4×1200 → 3×1600, then back down (4×1200 → 5×1000 → 6×800 → 12×400 final week).
- Warm-up/cooldown: 1.5–3 mi each (to add mileage).

**PLAN-003 — Just Finish Program**
- For: absolute novices, charity runners, anyone wanting to complete 13.1 mi noncompetitively.
- Premise: increase mileage by removing intensity → build general endurance safely. No SOS on Tue/Thu.
- Long runs: begin Week 8; slow, comfortable, build to covering the distance without stopping; walk breaks allowed.
- Approach: run largely by feel; harder on good days, slower on bad days; "keep moving."
- Mileage: ~20–25 mi/wk range.
- Goal: solid finish; foundation for future, more aggressive training. Time goal optional but a loose one is encouraged.
- tags: type:plan subtopic:just-finish-program level:beginner level:general-population sport:running phase:base

**PLAN-004 — Program readiness caveat**
Before Beginner or Advanced: if never raced any distance or never trained consistently (≥10–15 mi/wk for ≥3 straight weeks), first do shorter races (5K/10K) and build base. Do not skip shorter races. Exception: Just Finish Program.

**PLAN-005 — Post-race return-to-training plans (Tables 11.1/11.2)**
- New runners week 1 back: ~3–5 mi (~30 min) every other day (Table 11.1).
- Veterans week 1 back: easy running, more aggressive, based on body response (Table 11.2).
- Week 2 back: beginners → 5 days of 30-min runs; advanced → 45–60 min easy, 6 days/wk (all easy).
- Weeks 3–4 back: build mileage to typical volume.
- Weeks 4–6: resume structured training / new goals.
- Future segments can be shortened (speed/base segment 10–14 weeks).

**PLAN-006 — Elite Program: 9-day training cycle (Hansons-Brooks)**

| Day | Session |
|---|---|
| Sunday | Long 16–20 mi |
| Monday | Easy 10–12 (a.m.); easy 4 (p.m.) |
| Tuesday | Same as Monday |
| Wednesday | Tempo run 8–10 mi (or variation) |
| Thursday | Same as Monday |
| Friday | Same as Monday |
| Saturday | Track ~5 mi worth of speed, about 10K pace |
| Sunday | Same as Monday (easy) |
| Monday | Same as previous Monday |

- SOS every 3rd day; balances cumulative fatigue with injury prevention.
- tags: type:plan subtopic:elite-9day-cycle level:elite sport:running phase:build

**PLAN-007 — Elite Program volumes & SOS proportions** (TIER B)
- Male elites: up to 120 mi/wk (marathon buildup 110–140); female elites: 100–110 mi/wk.
- Easy days 10–12 mi (1:10–1:24); long runs 20+ mi (~1:55–2:10 for author).
- Peak-mileage paces: easy 6:00–6:30/mi; long runs ~5:30–6:30/mi; most runs 1:45–2:00/mi slower than goal race pace.
- SOS as % of weekly volume (elite vs Beg/Adv): long run ~16–17% (elite) vs ~23% (70-mi runner); speed 5% (elite) vs 7.5%; strength 10–11% (elite) vs 10–13%; tempo 12% (elite) vs 15%.
- Sleep/recovery advantages: 8–10 hr/night + naps; sponsored shoes; access to chiropractors/PTs.
- tags: type:plan subtopic:elite-volume level:elite sport:running metric:load
- [SRC: Hansons Half-Marathon Method | 2014 | Appendix A]

## 9. Discrete Protocol Library

### P-001 — 400 Repeats (Speed)
- Goal / target outcome: VO2max & anaerobic threshold development; establish pacing baseline
- Domain / context: endurance-sport
- Structure:
    - Warm-up: 1–3 mi
    - Main: 12 × 400 m @ 5K–10K goal pace; jog recovery 50–100% of interval time
    - Cool-down: 1–3 mi
- Intensity anchors: 5K–10K pace (~95–98% VO2max); repeat ≈ ~2 min for beginners
- When used: earliest speed session in the ladder (first speed workout in segment)
- Progressions: builds to 8×600, 6×800, 5×1K, 4×1200, 3×1600
- Execution cues: total 3 mi at intensity; keep jogging during recovery; do on track (marked, flat)
- tags: type:protocol subtopic:400-repeats sport:running system:vo2max metric:pace level:intermediate

### P-008 — 1-Mile Repeats (Strength)
- Goal: lactate tolerance/clearance; endurance at faster paces; maintain VO2max gains
- Structure: WU 1.5–3 mi → 3 × 1 mi @ (goal HM pace − 10 s/mi), 800 jog recovery → CD 1.5–3 mi
- Intensity: 60–80% VO2max (85–90% for faster runners); ~6 mi total at intensity
- When used: strength block (after speed block); everything now HM-specific
- Progression: → 1.5-mi, 2-mi, 3-mi repeats, then descend
- Execution: run on marked bike path/loop, not track
- tags: type:protocol subtopic:1mile-strength sport:running system:threshold metric:pace level:intermediate

### P-012 — Tempo Run
- Goal: internalize goal HM pace; race-specific endurance; running economy at goal pace; nutrition/gear rehearsal
- Structure: WU 1–3 mi → 5–10 mi @ goal HM pace (continuous, no breaks) → CD 1–3 mi
- Progression: beginner 3→6 mi; advanced 5→7 mi; peak tempo day (incl. WU/CD) 12–14 mi
- Execution: run goal pace even when it feels easy early; scheduled Thursdays
- tags: type:protocol subtopic:tempo-run sport:running metric:pace phase:build level:general-population

### P-013 — Long Run
- Goal: cumulative-fatigue stimulus; glycogen storage adaptation; fat utilization; confidence/coping; fast-twitch recruitment after slow-twitch fatigue
- Structure: continuous run at HM pace + 1:10–2:30/mi (easy-to-moderate)
- Sizing: 25–30% of weekly mileage; optimal 2–3 hr metabolic window; ≤3 hr; never exceed 25–30% (Daniels)
- When used: Sundays; preceded by Thu tempo + easy Fri/Sat (deliberate residual fatigue)
- Progressions: Beginner peaks 12 mi; Advanced 14 mi; every-other-week true long run
- tags: type:protocol subtopic:long-run sport:running system:fatoxidation phase:build level:general-population
- [SRC: Hansons Half-Marathon Method | 2014 | Ch. 3, Tables 3.2/3.3]

### P-014 — Elite: Steady-State Effort (long-run variation)
- Goal: stimulate aerobic threshold; fat-burning; mental focus
- Structure: begins like a normal long run; intensity gradually increases to ~30 s/mi slower than goal marathon pace (≈1 min/mi slower than HM pace), held for 50–75% of the run
- When used: well into training when fitness established; for experienced runners who can't add mileage
- Cues: pacing precision critical (too hard → premature wall; too slow → miss effect)
- tags: type:protocol subtopic:steady-state sport:running system:fatoxidation level:elite
- [SRC: Hansons Half-Marathon Method | 2014 | Appendix A]

### P-015 — Elite: The Cutdown (tempo variation)
- Structure: 10–12 mi total; starts (elite men) 6:00/mi and decreases by 10-sec increments to HM pace. Example: 6:00, 6:00, 5:50, 5:40, 5:30, 5:20, 5:10, 5:00, 4:50
- Cue: feels easy early, increasingly hard each mile
- tags: type:protocol subtopic:cutdown sport:running metric:pace level:elite

### P-018 — Dynamic warm-up routine (12 drills)
- Goal: reduce muscle stiffness, prep for fast running, engage fast/intermediate fibers & neuromuscular connection
- When used: AFTER a 1–3 mi warm-up, BEFORE speed/strength/tempo; 10–20 min total; any order
- Drills: (1) arm swings 6–10 reps each direction; (2) side bends 16–20; (3) hip circles 10–12 each direction; (4) half-squat 10–12; (5) leg kicks 10–12/leg; (6) leg swings 10–12/leg; (7) slow skipping 30–50 m; (8) high knees 30–50 m; (9) butt kicks 30–50 m; (10) cariocas/grapevine 30–50 m; (11) bounders 30–50 m; (12) sprints 4–6 × 75–100 m near-max (≤15 s; slower runners start 75 m; run with wind, jog back against it)
- Caution: avoid ballistic (bouncy, beyond-range) stretching — injury risk
- tags: type:protocol subtopic:dynamic-warmup sport:running system:neuromuscular level:general-population
- [SRC: Hansons Half-Marathon Method | 2014 | Ch. 7]

### P-019 — Static stretching routine (9 stretches, post-run)
- Goal: injury prevention, flexibility, running form, muscle relaxation
- Dosing: hold 20 s, 1–3 reps each; ~10–15 min post-run (or later in day — never pre-run); never to pain/shaking; slow/controlled
- Frequency: regular/daily (sometimes twice/day); ≥3 weeks before improvements seen
- Stretches: (1) low back; (2) shoulder; (3) chest; (4) calves; (5) gluteal; (6) groin; (7) hamstrings; (8) hip flexors/quads; (9) hips
- Caution: static stretching BEFORE running/racing reduces muscle force & risks tears — worst time
- tags: type:protocol subtopic:static-stretching sport:running caution level:general-population
- [SRC: Hansons Half-Marathon Method | 2014 | Ch. 7]

### P-020 — Core/strength routine (5 exercises)
- Goal: form, injury prevention, fight fatigue via fast-twitch recruitment late in race
- Dosing: choose 2–3 exercises after easy running days; start 1–2 days/wk, 1–2 sets; progress slowly
- Exercises:
    - Crunch — start 3×10, progress to 3×25, then add sets/reps
    - Back extension — hold 2–3 s, 12–15 reps
    - Superman (on exercise ball, opposite arm/leg) — hold 1–2 s, 12–15 reps/side
    - Bridge — hold 1–3 s, 12–15 reps (progress to one-legged)
    - Side plank — hold 10–20 s, increase with strength
- Principle: complements (never replaces) running; corrects weaknesses/imbalances; short & simple; no gym needed
- Reassurance: hard to add significant bulk with correct exercises/volumes
- tags: type:protocol subtopic:strength-training sport:running system:neuromuscular level:general-population
- [SRC: Hansons Half-Marathon Method | 2014 | Ch. 7]
