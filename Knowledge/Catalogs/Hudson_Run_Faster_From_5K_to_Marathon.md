## 8. Structured Plans / Programs

### PLAN-1 — Overall training-plan design: 8 steps
1. Choose peak race + race goal.
2. Pick start date + plan duration.
3. Decide running volume/frequency + weekly workout structure.
4. Divide into introductory/fundamental/sharpening periods.
5. Plan the peak training week.
6. Schedule tune-up races + recovery weeks.
7. Schedule progressions for interval/threshold/long-run key workouts.
8. Fill in remainder (easy/moderate runs, hill sprints, strides, drills).

### PLAN-5 — Tune-up race scheduling
- Do ≥1 tune-up race before peak race. Get ≥8 solid weeks of training before first tune-up.
- Surround with ≥2 easy days on each side.
- Rule: tune-ups shorter or ≤ peak race distance (exception: 10K ok before a 5K peak race).
- Do shorter tune-ups before longer ones.
- Don't race >2 weekends/month (missing long runs + fatigue). Shorter races take least out of you.
- Marathon peak: a Sunday long run usually beats a 5K/10K tune-up.
- Standard schedules (matching Ch.12 durations):
  - 5K (12 wk): 5K @ Week 9
  - 10K (14 wk): 5K @ Week 8, 10K @ Week 11
  - Half (16 wk): 5K @ Week 8, 10K @ Week 12
  - Marathon (20 wk): 5K @ Week 8, 10K @ Week 12, Half-marathon @ Week 16

## 9. Discrete Protocol Library

### P-1 — Steep Hill Sprints
- Goal: build maximal running-specific strength & stride power; injury prevention; stride stiffness/efficiency.
- Domain: endurance-sport
- Structure: max-effort sprint up a steep hill; walk back down to recover; repeat.
- Intensity/dose: Maximal effort. Duration 8→10→12 sec. Gradient 6–8% (start) → up to 10%.
- When used: throughout the cycle; introduce cautiously (1–2 reps first session, esp. if over 30). Placed after an easy run, typically Monday (± Thursday).
- Progression: add 1–2 sprints/session/week to 8–10; then 10-sec on steeper hill; then 12-sec on 10% gradient. Ceiling 10–12×10–12 sec twice/week → maintenance 6–10×/week once.
- Regressions: fewer reps; shorter duration; gentler gradient.
- Cues: full recovery between reps (cover same distance each rep); truly maximal.
- tags: type:protocol subtopic:hill-sprints system:neuromuscular metric:rpe level:general-population
- [SRC: Ch.4, Ch.2]

### P-2 — Hill Repetitions
- Goal: bridge strength→speed; strength for injury-prone/fast-twitch runners; less impact than track speed work.
- Domain: endurance-sport
- Structure: uphill intervals; jog slowly back down (full recovery). Basic 4-week progression: Wk1 6×200m, Wk2 6×300m, Wk3 6×400m, Wk4 5×600m.
- Intensity/dose: 200–300m @ ~1,500m effort; 400–600m @ ~3K effort. Gradient 4–6% (shallower than hill sprints).
- When used: later introductory / early fundamental period; 4–6 sessions suffice. Do ≥1 before first hard track speed session. Can return later if track intervals break you down or you lack specific strength.
- Cues: time each rep to ensure no slowing in later intervals.
- tags: type:protocol subtopic:hill-repetitions system:neuromuscular metric:rpe level:intermediate
- [SRC: Ch.4]

### P-3 — Uphill Progression
- Goal: increase aerobic + strength-building stimulus without taking too much out of the runner.
- Domain: endurance-sport
- Structure: prolonged uphill running (10+ min) at the end of an otherwise easy run.
- Intensity/dose: moderate to hard effort (by feel).
- tags: type:protocol subtopic:progression-run system:aerobic metric:rpe level:general-population
- [SRC: Ch.2, Ch.3]

### P-4 — Progression Run (6 formats, Table 3.2)
- Goal: moderate aerobic stimulus — stronger than easy run, weaker than most threshold runs; squeeze extra hard work in without overtaxing.
- Domain: endurance-sport
- Intensity by feel ("moderate" or "hard"), not race-pace targets.
- Formats:
  - Moderate Progression: 6 mi easy + 20 min "moderate" — introductory prep / squeeze extra work.
  - Moderate Uphill Progression: 6 mi easy + 20 min moderate (uphill) — build aerobic + strength.
  - Hard Progression: 6 mi easy + 20 min "hard" — late fundamental, coax aerobic support toward peak.
  - Hard Uphill Progression: 6 mi easy + 20 min hard (uphill) — fundamental, after moderate uphill base.
  - Long Run + Progression: 12 mi easy + 30 min moderate — throughout cycle; transform endurance→specific.
  - Fartlek Run + Progression: 2 mi easy / 4 mi (1 min @10K / 1 min easy) / 2 mi easy / 2 mi hard — late fundamental.
- Best use: most useful in introductory period (fitness low, not ready for threshold). Longer-distance runners use more.
- tags: type:protocol subtopic:progression-run system:aerobic metric:rpe level:general-population
- [SRC: Ch.3 Table 3.2]

### P-5 — Threshold Run (4 formats, Table 3.3)
- Goal: aerobic-system adaptations enabling faster paces for longer; support (not be) the toughest race-specific workouts. Finish "hungry for a little more."
- Domain: endurance-sport
- Formats (all book-standard between long warm-up & cool-down):
  - One interval: 2 mi easy / 4 mi @ HM pace / 2 mi easy — early fundamental (shorter) → late fundamental & sharpening (longer).
  - Two intervals: 2 mi easy / 15 min @ HM pace / 2 min easy / 15 min @ 10K pace / 2 mi easy.
  - Three intervals: 2 mi easy / 10 min @ marathon / 1 min easy / 10 min @ HM / 1 min easy / 10 min @ 10K / 2 mi easy — hits all 3 threshold paces.
  - Aerobic Test: 2 mi easy / 4 mi @ HM heart rate / 2 mi easy — measure progress (see T-1).
- Hardest elite threshold workout: 3×15 min (1st @2.5-hr pace, 2nd @90-min pace, 3rd @1-hr pace), 4-min active recoveries + long WU/CD. Average competitive runner: substitute 10–12-min efforts.
- Progression: increase duration OR pace OR proportion at faster paces per session — **not both volume and intensity simultaneously** (raises stress faster than aerobic system can adapt).
- Begin formal threshold runs at start of fundamental period.
- tags: type:protocol type:test subtopic:threshold system:threshold metric:pace metric:hr level:intermediate
- [SRC: Ch.3 Table 3.3]

### P-6 — Long Run (5 formats, Table 3.4)
- Goal: (job 1) build raw endurance to cover peak-race distance; (job 2) increase speed sustainable over long distances.
- Domain: endurance-sport
- Formats:
  - Easy Long Run: 14 mi easy — introductory long runs for less-fit runners.
  - Moderate Long Run: 14 mi moderate — introductory for fitter runners; "just finish" half/marathon runners.
  - Long Fartlek Run: 4 mi easy / 5×1K @ marathon pace / 1K easy / 4 mi easy — increased intensity, late fundamental & sharpening.
  - Long Progression Run: 10 mi easy + 4 mi hard — increased intensity, late fundamental & sharpening.
  - Marathon-Pace Run: 10 mi easy / 6 mi @ marathon pace — marathon peak sharpening.
- Frequency: 1 designated long run/week, almost every week. First long run 10–15% longer than longest recent run.
- tags: type:protocol subtopic:long-run system:aerobic metric:pace level:general-population
- [SRC: Ch.3 Table 3.4]

### P-7 — Marathon-Pace Run
- Goal: develop efficiency/fatigue resistance at marathon goal pace.
- Domain: endurance-sport (marathon)
- Structure: easy warm-up miles + block(s) at (near) marathon pace + easy cool-down miles. Examples: "1 mi easy / 10 mi @ marathon pace + 10–20 sec/mi / 1 mi easy"; "2 mi easy / 13.1 mi @ marathon goal pace / 2 mi easy."
- Peak-level marathon spec workout: ≥18 mi total, ≥10 mi at goal pace.
- tags: type:protocol subtopic:marathon-pace phase:peak metric:pace level:advanced
- [SRC: Ch.3, Ch.5]

### P-8 — Fartlek Intervals
- Goal: introduce speed/specific-endurance stimulus early; manageable dose of faster running within easy aerobic runs.
- Domain: endurance-sport
- Structure: 20–60-sec efforts at 1,500m–10K pace within an easy run; unstructured spacing with enough active recovery.
- Intensity/dose: e.g., 8×30 sec @ 10K–3K pace → progress duration (30→40→50 sec) and number.
- When used: introductory period (aerobic base building simultaneously). Best on quiet roads / smooth trails.
- tags: type:protocol subtopic:fartlek system:vo2max metric:pace level:general-population
- [SRC: Ch.4, Ch.5]

### P-9 — Repetition (Speed) Intervals
- Goal: transform speed into specific endurance; neuromuscular support.
- Domain: endurance-sport
- Structure: uniform-distance/intensity intervals, e.g., 6×800m @ 5K pace; 10×400m @ 3K/2-mi pace w/2-min jog. Progress: shorter/faster early → longer/slower (down to 10K pace) in fundamental.
- Add-on variant: small number (or one long) speed interval at end of a slower-running workout, e.g., 4×2K @10K pace + 1×1K @ max effort; or 20-min threshold @HM + 4×300m @3K.
- tags: type:protocol subtopic:speed-intervals system:neuromuscular metric:pace level:intermediate
- [SRC: Ch.4 Table 4.1]

### P-10 — Ladder Intervals
- Goal: integrate speed with specific endurance.
- Domain: endurance-sport
- Structure: intervals in ascending distance/descending pace (or vice versa). Preferred: 6-5-4-3-2-1 min, 1-min active recovery jog between; first @~10K pace, each faster, final 1-min @~1,500m pace.
- Shorter/faster variant for periodic speed dose: 1-2-3-2-1-2-3 min @ 1,500m–5K pace, equal-duration active recoveries; run shortest fastest, longest slowest.
- When used: 6-5-4-3... ladder = early fundamental (baseline speed workout); shorter ladders throughout fundamental/sharpening.
- tags: type:protocol subtopic:ladder-intervals system:neuromuscular metric:pace level:intermediate
- [SRC: Ch.4, Ch.5]

### P-11 — Strides
- Goal: neuromuscular coordination & efficiency at high speed; warm-up priming.
- Domain: endurance-sport
- Structure: ~100m at ~1,500m race pace; jog back to recover. Done after easy jog portion of warm-up, before any workout at 10K pace or faster.
- Cues: keep stride compact, relaxed, powerful.
- tags: type:protocol subtopic:strides system:neuromuscular metric:pace level:general-population
- [SRC: Ch.4]

### P-12 — Drills (warm-up)
- Goal: dynamic flexibility, stride power/stiffness/efficiency; injury prevention (hip stabilizers).
- Domain: endurance-sport
- Set: do 1 set (each drill + 1 stride once) → progress to 2–3 sets, after the jog portion of warm-up before ≥10K-pace efforts.
  - **Slow Skipping:** drive knee up hard, hop-step, alternate; 30m, leap as high as possible. → stride power & stiffness.
  - **High Knees:** fast tempo, slow forward progress, thighs to 90°, up on toes, spine straight; 30m. → hip dynamic flexibility, ankle stiffness.
  - **Butt Kicks:** slow forward progress, thighs in line with torso, heel to butt each stride; 30m. → hamstrings, knee dynamic flexibility.
  - **Sideways Jumping Jacks:** jumping jack pushing harder with one foot to travel ~1 ft laterally; 30m each direction. → hip abductors/adductors; hip/pelvis/spine stability; efficiency + injury risk reduction.
- tags: type:protocol subtopic:drills system:neuromuscular level:general-population
- [SRC: Ch.4, Ch.11]

### P-13 — Core-strength training
- Goal: stabilize knees, hips, pelvis, spine during running → reduce injury risk.
- Domain: endurance-sport (cross-training)
- Structure: 5–6 exercises, 2–3×/week. Target: upper/lower back, buttocks, hips, lower abdomen, thighs. Examples: stomach crunches, side step-ups. "A little goes a long way."
- tags: type:protocol type:recovery subtopic:core-strength system:musculoskeletal level:general-population
- [SRC: Ch.2, Ch.11]

### P-14 — Cross-training (alternative cardio)
- Goal: maintain fitness when running painful/impossible (injury); off-season variation.
- Domain: endurance-sport
- Structure: bicycling, inline skating, elliptical, anti-gravity treadmill. Duplicate the missed running workout as closely as possible. Example: planned 10-mi progression w/ last 2 mi moderate → 75-min bike ride w/ last 15 min moderate intensity.
- Rule: at pain in a bone/muscle/joint, stop running immediately, switch to cross-training until pain-free.
- tags: type:protocol type:recovery subtopic:cross-training level:general-population
- [SRC: Ch.2, Ch.8]
