## L2 Workouts & Protocols

### [TUA-L2-001] Warm-up: principles and two stages

- Goal: prepare muscles to work in optimal state. Higher intensity workout/race = longer warm-up.
- When used: Zones 1-2 workouts: ease into pace for 10 minutes. High-intensity: use the routine below. Tired days: allow longer warm-up to assess how you feel and adjust the workout.
- Stage 1: sedentary body is parasympathetic-dominated; this stage activates the sympathetic system, dilating capillaries in skin and muscles and adjusting blood pressure so working muscles get blood and oxygen. Raise body temperature with light aerobic exercise; sweating signals capillary dilation and elevated temperature. At least 10 minutes. Also brings the aerobic system fully online.
- Stage 2: limbering calisthenic movements and dynamic stretching at gradually increasing pace and effort, activating joint stretch reflexes. Should feel no strain. 5-10 minutes. Before an interval session finish with 2-3 minutes in Zone 3 using the main workout's mode (running or skiing).

evidence: author-experience
src: Ch 5 "Warm-up"

### [TUA-L2-002] Example Warm-Up Routines (running and skimo)

- Easy aerobic running or skiing in Z1 until you sweat; maybe 10 to 15 minutes.
- If a low-intensity workout is planned, move directly into it. If high-intensity, continue.
- Running, exercises with gradually increasing intensity: 10 air squats, no rest, then 10 leg swings each leg, no rest, then 10 trunk rotations each side, no rest, then high-knee running in place 20 seconds, no rest, then bouncing on toes 20 seconds, no rest, then 2 to 3 minutes in Z3-Z4 uphill.
- Skimo: 3 min uphill skiing in Z3, no rest, then 2×10 sec uphill skiing in Z5 with 2-min recovery between.

### [TUA-L2-003] Cool-down

- Goal: gradually return systems to homeostasis, reduce core temperature, metabolize some waste products, relax psychologically. Higher intensity = longer cool-down.
- A long low-intensity workout may need only a couple of minutes walking at the end.
- After a high-intensity workout or race: typical cool-down 20 to 30 minutes in Recovery Zone or Zone 1.

evidence: author-experience
src: Ch 5 "Cool-down"

### [TUA-L2-004] Pick-ups within long Zone 1-2 workouts

- Goal: build specific strength endurance and speed/strength before harder interval work.
- When used: begin after a few weeks of aerobic base training; one distance workout per week is plenty.
- Structure: short speedy segments within long Z1/Z2 workouts. Duration 8-10 seconds early, up to 15 seconds later. Initially no more than 6-8 per workout. Take at least 2 minutes easy running between reps.
- Intensity: a fun, fast pace, never a full sprint.
- Placement: early in the workout (fresher, faster, more speed/strength effect, structured repetition style) or late (legs push fast when fatigued, more endurance effect) or thrown in as terrain and feeling motivate.

evidence: author-experience

### [TUA-L2-005] Tempo workout (Zone 3 continuous)

- Goal: train mixed metabolic processes above AeT and around LT; improve endurance.
- Structure: continuous Zone 3 effort, 20 to 60 minutes, anywhere across Zone 3 (AeT to LT). With a well-developed aerobic capacity, AeT is within 10 percent of LT so the Zone 3 HR range is narrow, often fewer than fifteen beats. Warm up and cool down with 10-20 minutes of Zone 1 before and after.
- Cues: hold as steady an effort/HR as possible; do not turn into hard efforts or time trials (defeats the purpose).
- Variation: well-conditioned athletes can add a 20-30 minute Zone 3 tempo segment in the middle of a long Zone 1/2 workout.
- Caution: seductive; do not let most distance runs morph into this type.

### [TUA-L2-006] Progressive distance workout

- Goal: tempo-like effect with added stress of faster running at end of a long workout (prefatigued state).
- Structure: start very slowly with warm-up and gradually raise pace/effort/HR. Main section just below and above LT lasts 10-30 minutes. Can be as short as 60 minutes but much more effective beyond 90 minutes and up to 2 hours.
- Example: 40 minutes Zone 1; then 30 minutes Zone 2; assess whether to push or back off and finish in Zone 1; if good add 20 minutes Zone 3; if still strong, a final 5 minutes Zone 4.
- Frequency/recovery: one or two recovery-workout days afterward. One such workout each 10-14 days after early Base Period is sufficient even for well-trained.

### [TUA-L2-007] Threshold intervals (Zone 3)

- Goal: raise LT speed and increase duration it can be maintained; strong muscular endurance effect. Done sport-specifically.
- Structure: 20 to 60 minutes of hard work total depending on development. Newcomers: short reps of 3-6 minutes with short active recoveries at work:rest 3:1 or 4:1. Experienced: work up to 3×20 or 2×30 minutes.
- Note: fun-hard and tempting to include in nearly every session; they make up only a small percentage of yearly volume for the most successful athletes.

evidence: author-experience

### [TUA-L2-008] Aerobic power intervals (Zone 4) and introducing Zone 4

- Goal: maximally use all systems involved in endurance.
- Intensity: above LT; usually near 95 percent of max HR. Total work volume 15 to 30 minutes.
- Prerequisite: muscular endurance (hence special muscular endurance and Zone 3 period). If legs tire and HR falls below Zone 4, you no longer get Zone 4 benefit; do when rested. Less fit = shorter work periods.
- Introduction: when comfortable with Zone 3 (around 10 percent of total weekly time in Zone 3), add Zone 4 by replacing Zone 3 time: replace two minutes of Zone 3 with one minute of Zone 4. If you struggle to recover, reduce Zone 4 volume.
- Spacing: Zone 3 and Zone 4 workouts on separate days, at least 72 hours apart.
- Work periods 30 seconds up to 8 minutes; work:rest 1:1 up to two minutes (two minutes adequate for longer work periods). Rests active, not full rest, to keep the aerobic system revved.

### [TUA-L2-009] 30/30s

- Goal: introduce Z4 intensity and add speed to slow, hard uphill efforts; especially useful for skimo (speed and turnover).
- Warm-up: good warm-up ending with 3-4 minutes at Z3.
- Structure: 30 s hard / 30 s slower. Control pace: build effort and HR so you hit Z4 by the middle of the second 30/30 repetition. Slow only enough for HR to drop 5-7 beats. Repeat until power/speed drop or HR cannot reach Z4.
- Progression: start 2×6 min of 30/30s with a couple minutes easy between sets; next 2×8 min, then 2×10 min, until 2×15 min, then one 20-minute session. 30 minutes total in one bout is a good but tough goal.

### [TUA-L2-010] Hill Sprint / Hill Bounding workout

- Goal: anaerobic capacity, leg power, recruit fast-twitch fibers; longer stride, better economy. Early effect mainly neurological.
- When used: once a week during the Base Period.
- Terrain: 30 percent grade hill (about 17 degrees; rises 3 ft/m per 10 ft/m horizontal) or steep stairs. Steeper generally better, but over 50 percent traction is hard. Skip steps for bounding. Fire escape stairs (30-40 degrees, landings ~10 ft/3 m) are acceptable.
- Warm-up (very important): (1) 20 min running, gradually increasing pace, finishing with 2-3 min at Z3, break a sweat; (2) 10 min dynamic stretching: bouncing on toes, lunges, leg swings, ballistic toe touches; (3) 2×20 or 30 s medium-to-hard hill run or bound, 1-2 min slow downhill walk between; (4) 2×20 s skipping uphill, 1-2 min slow downhill walk between.
- Main set: once warm and pain-free, 6-8×10 s maximum power bounding with long stride, or running sprints; 2-minute easy downhill walk between, 3-minute rest between sets if reps exceed eight. Cool-down: 20 min easy jog.
- Intensity anchors: inexperienced: start at 80-85 percent of maximum effort and build output during reps for the first 2-3 workouts (another statement: start with 90-95 percent of maximum force; add force only after handling 2-3 workouts without problem).
- Progression: add reps only; never lengthen reps beyond 10 s; add no more than 2 reps per week.
- Hill Sprints: uphill sprint with normal (grade-modified) running technique. Hill Bounding: exaggerated stride length, more airtime with both feet off ground, lower leg turnover; skiers can use poles.
- Cues/notes: early sessions may not feel tiring; if you are not tiring it is because slow-twitch units are doing the work, and "if it feels ineffective, that's a sign you need it even more." Endurance athletes hate resting; extended rests are vital ("intensity is king").
- Caution: injury is a real risk.

### [TUA-L2-012] Anaerobic endurance intervals

- Goal: handle surges and maximum outputs; produce as much lactate as possible and keep it high. Maximum, exhausting training.
- Frequency: only 2-3 workouts spaced 7-10 days apart may reach the needed level for the book's events; not for all phases. Fast-twitch athletes can do more.
- Method 1: maximal bouts of 2 minutes, work:rest about 2:1, repeated 4-6 times or until exhaustion. Short recovery may halt the workout early, limiting volume.
- Method 2 (Kílian's preferred): maximum speed 20-30 seconds, recovery 2-3 minutes, repeated 8-12 times; higher speed and likely more volume.
- Kílian's note: before every race season, 2-3 short-interval sessions for max speed on a slight hill, bouts of 20 to max 30 s; ten rounds, rest, another ten; maybe a third round if legs and heart still have bounce (usually only second or third session). Gives an extra gear for short hard efforts.
- Caution: needs significant recovery time and aerobic regeneration work afterward. Less well-trained individuals: use with caution only in preparation for important short races like the VK; benefits small, risks high.

### [TUA-L2-013] Core Strength Workouts Stage 1-3

- Goal: build core (TA, obliques) strength with strict form; form breakdown (sagging hips, shaking, wobbling) means motor units overloaded and adequate stimulus.
- When used: finish of warm-up before Leg Strength routine.
- Stage 1 (struggling, cannot activate core): Plank from the knees, 6×15 sec hold or until loss of form, rest one minute between sets. Windshield wiper with bent knees, six sets of max reps, rest one minute.
- Stage 2 (near a minute plank or a few straight-leg wipers): Plank, six sets of max holds until form lost, rest two minutes. Windshield wiper, six sets of max reps, rest two minutes.
- Stage 3: select three or four exercises; resistance so no more than ten reps (total or per side) or thirty seconds isometric hold before form deteriorates; no more than five sets each; rest two minutes between sets. Options: Weighted Plank (weight on butt; or weight vest/plate); Weighted Windshield Wiper (ankle weights/heavier boots, straight legs); Marching Plank (Fig 7.19: lift each limb in succession, hold until losing form then lift next limb); Kayaker (Fig 7.20: seated, feet off ground, heavy dumbbell, rotate to touch weight to floor each side); Standing Trunk Rotation (Fig 7.21: feet shoulder width, weight in front, rotate aggressively each direction; stopping and starting rotation gives the effect; heavy weight, swing fast). Others that challenge same muscles allowed.

### [TUA-L2-015] Gym-Based Muscular Endurance (ME) Workout

- Goal: improve local muscular endurance of legs (uphill and downhill performance), especially without mountain access. First used by author Johnston in 1991 with an elite XC skier before an Olympic year; effective despite non-specific nature.
- When used: Base Period; after basic strength adequate. Spacing: up to ten days apart; if more than twelve days, repeat previous workout rather than advance.
- Warm-up (be sweating): 200 total reps of four or five different core exercises of choice without stopping, minimum 20 reps of each before moving on with no rest. Then 15 min aerobic (bike, run, stairs, rowing or mix), finishing with 2-3 min moderately hard effort (Z3).
- Main set: 6×10 reps each leg box step (rest 1 min; 10 reps one leg before switching); 6×10 each leg front lunge (rest 1 min); 6×10 each leg split jump squat (rest 1 min; alternate front legs each jump; count reps on one leg); 6×10 squat jump (rest 1 min). Complete all six sets of each exercise; no more than two minutes rest between exercises. Tempo about one rep per second.
- Progression: 1st and 2nd workout body weight only; 3rd and 4th add 10 percent of body weight; 5th eight sets each, +10% BW; 6th and 7th six sets, +10% BW, rest 30 seconds; 8th eight sets, +10% BW, 30 s rest. Longer-term: 9th six sets 15% BW rest 1 min; 10th six sets 15% BW rest 45 s; 11th six sets 20% BW rest 1 min; 12th six sets 20% BW rest 45 s.
- Notes: weight via vest, barbell on shoulders or dumbbells. Percentages are suggestions; start lighter, increase only as you feel effects of the previous workout; err conservative. Not necessary to complete all progressions; may add more in off-season. Squat Jump (Fig 8.4): spring up explosively, swing arms, land quietly absorbing with toes and knees. Split Jump Squat (Fig 8.3): from lunge stance spring up and switch feet; add resistance best with vest or weighted pack.

### [TUA-L2-016] Outdoor Muscular Endurance (ME) Workout

- Goal: make legs the limit of sustaining intensity (tired legs before breathing shuts you down); like a Z3/Z4 uphill interval workout; for skiers and runners without gym.
- When used: Base Period; one session per week to ten days; maintain high volume of Zones 1-2.
- Terrain: hill of 20 to 50 percent gradient; steeper is better. Load factors: steepness, stride length, added weight. Added weight: small pack, start no more than 10-15 percent of body weight; add more only after seeing effects. Stairs of tall buildings also usable.
- Warm-up: run 15 minutes with gradually increasing intensity so the last 2-3 minutes are in Zones 3-4.
- Traditional Z3 uphill method: run/hike up for 8-10 minutes with low-level leg burn; repeat two times with 5-minute moving recovery (walking down OK); can build to full 40 minutes of steady uphill. Weighted pack version useful for ski mountaineers carrying heavy packs.
- Bounding variation: long bounding stride (high muscle load, not sustainable). Start 10×10 sec bounding up steep trail, walk down until legs recovered. Fit/fast recovery: 15×15 sec with about 1 min run/walk down recovery; progress to 20×20 sec with 2 min recovery run. Less fit: 10×10 with 2 min active recovery. (Kílian example: two minutes of long strides, short rest, then two minutes of short strides; if bounding aggressively start with 10-20 s or until legs burn, recover, then short-stride/high-tempo until breathing/HR limit; repeat many times.)
- Notes: expect soreness a couple days; hills on normal aerobic workouts should feel easier within 2-3 such workouts. No improvement week to week = probably doing too much, not enough recovery.

### [TUA-L2-017] Upper-body ME for skimo: roller skiing (arms only)

- Goal: upper-body muscular endurance for poling; muscle mass small enough not to tax cardiac O2 supply, so limit is arms/shoulders. For more advanced skiers in Base Period; easy to add even in heavy running off-season; may aid leg recovery.
- Equipment/caution: classic roller skis and carbide-tipped poles; significant learning period; no brakes, so downhills can be very dangerous. Included for completeness; appeals to a minority.
- Technique: single-poling with feet and upper body still, slight crunch (C shape) position; load adjusted by hill gradient (Fig 8.5). Early Base on relatively flat terrain.
- Beginner: poling on flats more than a couple minutes fatiguing signals big potential gains. Start continuous single poling 10-15 min on flats; then 5×1 min moderately hard on very gradual uphill with 2 min recovery; progress to 3×10 min with 5 min recovery (easy skiing on flats or very gradual uphill). Select grade to suit upper-body strength.
- Advanced: same on steeper grades; mix steep short reps with longer lower-angle reps.
- Frequency: up to twice per week in early Base Period as a second workout on leg-focused days; continue on snow as maintenance or in racing breaks.

### [TUA-L2-019] Skimo technical skills circuit

- Goal: develop technique (transitions, kick turns, skin management, boot packing, high-tempo skinning, fast downhill skiing).
- When used: ongoing; learn first in a low-pressure, low-intensity setting with some coaching.
- Structure: after a warm-up, run a short course containing several skills taking 10 to 15 minutes. Rest a few minutes between laps, thinking about mistakes and how to avoid them.

## L3 Exercises & Technique

### [TUA-L3-001] Leg/hip exercises: knee band squat, lateral band, step-up, step-down, front lunge

- Squats with Knee Band (Fig 7.4): squat with rubber band just above knees to strengthen hips.
- Single Leg Lateral Band (Fig 7.5): band around knees (above or below), balanced on one leg, extend unweighted leg to the side and back at about 45 degrees.
- Box Step-Up (Fig 8.1): stand about one half of your stride length in front of a box/step just below knee height. Step up mainly using the front leg to pull you up with minimal assist from the rear leg. Keep knee tracking over toes.
- Box Step-Down (Fig 7.7): step down controlled; if knee collapses inward (poor hip strength) use a lower box.
- Front Lunge (Fig 7.8): from standing step forward onto a bent front knee; push back and up; alternate legs. Add resistance with weight vest, barbell at shoulders, or dumbbell/kettlebell at chest. (Ch 8 cue: step forward about half your body length dynamically so front leg catches most weight; knee behind toes of front foot.)

### [TUA-L3-002] Upper-body exercises: push-up and pull-up progressions

- Inclined Push-Ups (Fig 7.10): hands elevated on stairs, bench, countertop, or table. Maintain strict plank form (neutral spine, no sagging or inverted V hips). Progress by decreasing incline angle as strength increases.
- Knee Push-Ups (Fig 7.12): weight distributed between hands on the floor and knees. Transition cue: start set on toes; when form deteriorates, immediately drop to knees to complete remaining target repetitions.
- Standard Push-Up: hands placed directly below shoulders, neutral spine, weight supported on hands and toes only.
- Decline Push-Ups (Fig 7.14): once 15 standard push-ups can be performed, advance to:
  1. Decline on box or bench: feet elevated on a sturdy box, lower chest to floor and push to full extension.
  2. Decline on stability ball: shins/toes balanced on an exercise ball, lower and press.
  3. Single-leg decline on box: one foot on box, other leg extended upward in the air throughout movement.
- Weighted Push-Up (Fig 7.15): wear a weight vest or balance a weight plate across the upper back.
- Pull-Up / Chin-Up (Fig 7.9, 7.16): chin-up (palms facing you) or pull-up (palms facing away). Start with elbows slightly bent and shoulder blades actively retracted and depressed (Fig 7.9 right); avoid a passive dead hang with elevated shoulders to prevent joint strain. Pull until chin clears the bar. For weighted pull-ups, attach a weight plate to a dip belt around the waist.
- Assisted Pull-Up (Fig 7.11): place one foot on a stool or loop a heavy elastic resistance band over the bar and under the feet/knees, or use a lat pull-down machine.
- Isometric Bar Hang (Fig 7.13): hold chin over the bar or at a 90-degree locked-off elbow angle to build ski-poling shoulder strength.

### [TUA-L3-003] Core exercises: plank progressions, windshield wiper, kayaker, trunk rotation

- Forearm Plank (Fig 7.17): elbows directly under shoulders, weight supported on forearms and toes, spine completely neutral (no sagging hips or arched back). Hold position until shaking or loss of form. Can be initiated from the knees if necessary. Progressions include single-limb lift / marching plank (Fig 7.19) or placing a weight plate / weight vest on the lower back/butt.
- Marching Plank (Fig 7.19): from standard forearm plank, lift each limb in succession forward or upward, hold until form begins to break down, then switch to the next limb.
- Windshield Wiper (Fig 7.18): lie supine, arms outstretched perpendicular to torso at shoulder level, palms down. Raise legs together with feet perpendicular to ceiling (12 o'clock). Slowly rotate hips to lower feet to one side until lower foot lightly touches the floor, press down firmly with hand on that side to counteract body rotation, return to 12 o'clock, and repeat to opposite side. Keep knees locked and feet together. Regression: bend knees 90 degrees. Progression: wear boots or ankle weights once 10 reps per side are achieved.
- Kayaker / Seated Russian Twist (Fig 7.20): seated on floor with feet elevated off the ground and torso reclined. Holding a heavy dumbbell at chest, rotate aggressively to touch the weight to the floor on each side.
- Standing Trunk Rotation (Fig 7.21): stand with feet shoulder width, knees slightly bent, holding a weight plate in front of the body with arms extended. Rotate aggressively side to side; swing fast and use the stopping and starting of the rotational inertia to generate core strength.

### [TUA-L3-004] Muscular endurance dynamic exercises: split jump squat and squat jump

- Split Jump Squat (Fig 8.3): from a deep front lunge stance, spring vertically upward as explosively as possible, switch legs mid-air, and land softly with the opposite foot forward. Count repetitions on one leg only (e.g., 10 reps each leg = 20 total jumps). Resistance is added using a weighted vest or backpack.
- Squat Jump (Fig 8.4): from a full squat stance, swing arms powerfully overhead and spring explosively upward into maximum vertical jump. Land quietly, absorbing impact through toes and knees.

### [TUA-L3-005] Ski simulation drills: Ski Striding, Moose Hoofing, Ski Bounding

- Ski Striding: long Zones 1–2 uphills; long stride imitating glide; uphill only, run the flats.
- Moose Hoofing (Elghuf): Zone 3 workouts; loping long stride between bounding and striding; exaggerate stride, reach far ahead with uphill leg; sustainable for minutes.
- Ski Bounding: short sets of seconds at maximum power; maximum air time, explosive push-off.
