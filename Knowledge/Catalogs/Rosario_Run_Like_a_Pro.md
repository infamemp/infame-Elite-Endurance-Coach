### PLAN-MARATHON-DIST-LOGIC — Long-run measurement rules by distance
- Duration-based long runs generally preferred (equal stimulus across abilities).
- Marathon plans use *distance*-based long runs (a marathon is genuinely longer for slower runners; ensures they're prepared for full distance).
- Ultra plans use *duration*-based long runs, capped at 5:00:00 (no fitness gain beyond ~5 hrs; "anything longer isn't training—it's punishment").

## 9. Discrete Protocol Library

### P-1 — Coach Ben's Favorite 5K Workout
- Goal / target outcome: Race-specific readiness for 5K (fast running on fatigued legs)
- Domain / context: endurance-sport
- Structure:
    - Warm-up: 1 mile easy (or 15–20:00 easy per level) + Drills and Strides
    - Main: 7 × 3:00 @ CV +0:08–0:10 per mile / 1:00 rest (3:00 rest after final rep); then 1-mile all-out time trial
    - Cool-down: 1 mile easy (or 15–20:00 easy)
- Intensity/dose anchors: CV+8–10 s/mi (≈15K pace) for reps; all-out for TT
- When used: ~9–10 days before goal 5K race
- Execution cues: reps just fast enough to fatigue while keeping HR high; TT simulates middle→final mile of 5K; enjoyable = effective
- Result cited: two athletes ran 1K reps in 2:55, mile TT 4:16; 9 days later 13:29 (4:20/mi) and 13:36 (4:22/mi) for 5000 m
- tags: type:protocol sport:running system:threshold system:vo2max metric:pace subtopic:5k level:advanced
- [SRC: Run Like a Pro | 2022 | Ch.10 Coach's Tip]
- [EVIDENCE: case-study]

### P-2 — Coach Ben's Favorite 10K Workout
- Goal: 10K-specific challenge (sustained high HR, tired-leg finishing kick)
- Domain / context: endurance-sport
- Structure:
    - Warm-up: 15–20:00 easy + Drills and Strides
    - Main: 8 × 3:00 @ CV / 1:00 rest (3:00–5:00 rest after last rep); then 4 × 300 m @ VHI, cutdown (each faster than previous) / 1:00 rest
    - Cool-down: 15–20:00 easy
- Intensity/dose anchors: CV (30-min pace) for reps; 300s progress from ~mile pace down (example: 52, 51, 50, 49 s)
- Rationale: 3:00 at 30-min pace = akin to 1:00 at 10-min pace; multiple reps on short rest = exponential specificity. 300s train final-kick (in a 10K on the track, ~300 m to go is where you switch gears).
- Pro version: 10 × 1K (≈3:00 each) instead of 8 × 3:00; nonelites use time-based version.
- 300 pacing derivation: start from mile pace = target for rep #3; make #1 two seconds slower, cut down to end. Reps <600 m need no altitude conversion.
- Result cited: Kellyn Taylor (team 10,000 m record holder) 10×1K in 3:18 @7,300 ft (=3:07 sea level) + 4×300 (52/51/50/49); ~2.5 wks later 31:07.60 for 10,000 m.
- tags: type:protocol sport:running system:threshold metric:pace subtopic:10k level:advanced
- [SRC: Run Like a Pro | 2022 | Ch.11 Coach's Tip]
- [EVIDENCE: case-study]

### P-3 — Coach Ben's Favorite Half-Marathon Workout
- Goal: HM-specific — run hard on ~1 hr of accumulated running volume
- Domain / context: endurance-sport
- Structure:
    - 15:00 @ LTP + 10 s/mi (i.e., 10 s/mi *slower* than LT pace)
    - 8:00 jog recovery
    - 3 × 5:00 @ LTP − 10 s/mi (10 s/mi *faster* than LT pace) / 2:00 rest (5:00 after #3)
    - 15:00 @ LTP + 10 s/mi
- Intensity/dose anchors: LT pace ± 10 s/mi. Worked example (LT 6:00/mi): tempos at 6:10; five-min efforts at 5:50.
- Total ~90 min including warm-up/cool-down; final tempo done on ~1 hr of prior running (simulates late-race fatigue).
- Philosophy: HM best run off an *eclectic* block (accidentally be ready) — hills, track, roads, slower- and faster-than-race-pace work. (Rory Linkletter example: 1:01:44 HM off a wide-range block; example sessions listed in Section 11.)
- tags: type:protocol sport:running system:threshold metric:pace subtopic:half-marathon level:advanced
- [SRC: Run Like a Pro | 2022 | Ch.12 Coach's Tip]
- [EVIDENCE: case-study]

### P-4 — Coach Ben's Favorite Marathon Workout ("The 10/10")
- Goal: Simulate the final miles of a marathon (cement-legs feeling on tired legs)
- Domain / context: endurance-sport
- Structure: 2:00:00 continuous — first 1:00:00 @ SSP + 1:00/mile ("medium"); second 1:00:00 @ SSP ("hard"/marathon effort). Plans append +1:00 @ SSP.
    - Warm-up/cool-down in plans: 10–20:00 easy each side.
- Intensity/dose anchors: first half = marathon pace + 50 s/mi (for pros); second half = marathon/steady-state pace. For pros "marathon pace" ≈ SSP (2–2.5 hr).
- Rationale: marathon problem isn't pace, it's whether tissues tolerate 26.2 mi of pounding; needs many 10–16 mi at/near race pace on tired legs from high weekly volume.
- Recovery: requires an easy day after (even 2:09 marathoner Scott Fauble needs it — 4 mi easy next day).
- Origin: adapted from Terrence Mahon coaching Ryan Hall (Mammoth Lakes).
- Result cited: Tuliamuk, Bruce, Taylor did it 1 month out from 2020 Olympic Trials; their 22nd mile of the day (incl. 2-mi warm-up) at marathon effort → finished 1st, 6th, 8th.
- tags: type:protocol sport:running system:aerobic system:threshold metric:pace subtopic:marathon level:advanced
- [SRC: Run Like a Pro | 2022 | Ch.13 Coach's Tip]
- [EVIDENCE: case-study]

### P-5 — Coach Ben's Favorite Ultramarathon Workout (Back-to-Back Long Runs)
- Goal: Callus body/mind for time-on-feet; practice starting fatigued
- Domain / context: endurance-sport (ultra)
- Structure: Day 1 = 3:00:00–4:00:00; Day 2 = 2:00:00–3:00:00 (combined 5–7 hrs over a weekend). In plans (L3 Week 17): Sat 3:30:00 easy (no calories before/during) + Sun 3:00:00 easy.
- Execution notes: study race terrain and replicate; plan routes for total 5–7 hrs; measure by *vert* (vertical gain) not splits; expect large pace fluctuations; steep inclines may require fast walking/hiking; day 2 begins in deficit and best mimics race (fight blisters/cramps/aches + quit signals — override unless true emergency); needs fueling/safety/recovery plan and a partner.
- Source of advice: Stephen Kersh (7th Western States 2019; 2nd JFK 50 2020).
- tags: type:protocol sport:running system:aerobic subtopic:ultramarathon phase:build level:advanced
- [SRC: Run Like a Pro | 2022 | Ch.14 Coach's Tip]
- [EVIDENCE: author-opinion / case-study]

### P-6 — "The PG" (mixed-zone high-volume session)
- Goal: Hit multiple training zones in one session with nothing all-out (variety)
- Domain / context: endurance-sport
- Structure (in order):
    - 3-mile effort @ steady-state pace; 4:00 rest
    - 4 × 800 m @ LT pace / 1:00 rest (2:00 after last)
    - 2-mile tempo @ slightly slower than LT pace; 3:00 rest
    - 4 × 400 m @ 10K pace / 1:00 rest
    - 1 fast mile @ slightly slower than 10K pace
- Design brief: high-volume, many zones, nothing all-out. Named for intern Patrick Gildea.
- Execution notes: adjust volume for your level; the point is multiple zones without going all out.
- tags: type:protocol sport:running system:threshold system:vo2max metric:pace subtopic:variety level:advanced
- [SRC: Run Like a Pro | 2022 | Ch.4 Coach's Tip]
- [EVIDENCE: author-opinion]

### P-7 — Fast-Finish Long Run
- Goal: Endurance + fast running in a fatigued state; teach even pacing
- Structure: Long easy run with a faster final segment (e.g., last 3 miles much faster than preceding 12–15). In plans, e.g., "1:00:00 easy + 10:00 @ LTP."
- Fastest Mile Last Rule variant: make the final mile faster than any preceding mile.
- tags: type:protocol sport:running system:aerobic metric:pace subtopic:long-run level:intermediate
- [SRC: Run Like a Pro | 2022 | Ch.5, Plans]

### P-8 — Thirty-Fifteen Intervals
- Goal: VO2max-region stimulus; effort-managed
- Structure: alternating 0:30 "on" (pace sustainable ~15 min) / 0:15 "off" (jog). Sets: intro 3×(8 reps), intermediate 3×(10 reps), advanced 3×(12 reps), 3:00 easy jog between sets.
- Cue: manage by effort (~9/10 overall); metrics not useful due to short segments; expect to blow pacing first time.
- tags: type:protocol sport:running system:vo2max metric:rpe subtopic:intervals level:intermediate
- [SRC: Run Like a Pro | 2022 | Ch.5]

### P-9 — The Guardrail (hill pacing game)
- Goal: Fine pacing perception; progressive speed increases
- Structure: 10 uphill intervals of 1:00 each; cover slightly more distance each rep; first rep = highest speed leaving room for 9 small increases; last rep all-out. (Use markers, e.g., dropped socks; ~8–10 additional feet per rep.)
- Invented by Coach Ben on a Flagstaff hill.
- tags: type:protocol sport:running system:neuromuscular subtopic:pacing-games level:intermediate
- [SRC: Run Like a Pro | 2022 | Ch.5]

### P-10 — Billat Accelerations
- Goal: Pacing skill + VO2max stimulus; continuous acceleration from easy jog to all-out sprint without running out of gears
- Structure/levels: intro = 11:00 + 3:00 accelerations (each with lengthy walk-to-jog recovery); intermediate = 11:00 + 6:00; advanced = 11:00 + 6:00 + 3:00.
- tags: type:protocol sport:running system:vo2max subtopic:pacing-games level:intermediate
- [SRC: Run Like a Pro | 2022 | Ch.5; from Véronique Billat, *The Science of the Marathon and the Art of Variable Race Running*]

### P-11 — Leg-speed maintenance session (Coach Ben)
- Goal: Maintain leg speed when other components are higher priority
- Structure: 10 × 20-second relaxed sprints / 1:00 active recovery. Tougher than easy run, far less taxing than hard speed formats.
- tags: type:protocol sport:running system:neuromuscular subtopic:leg-speed level:intermediate
- [SRC: Run Like a Pro | 2022 | Ch.5]

### P-12 — Danielle Shanahan indoor 5000 m sharpener (pro example)
- Structure: 4 sets of [3 × 200 m @ VO2max pace / 200 m jog], each set followed by 400 m @ VO2max / 400 m jog. Total 4000 m at VO2max pace (4:45/mi = slightly faster than 5000 race pace). Done 11 days before race; result: 15:26 PB.
- tags: type:protocol sport:running system:vo2max metric:pace subtopic:5k level:elite
- [SRC: Run Like a Pro | 2022 | Ch.2]

### P-13 — Plan workout-type glossary (Training Plans Overview) [TIER A of execution intent]
Easy runs; long runs (basic + depletion + surges + tempo + fast-finish + progression); fast-finish runs; progression runs; fartlek runs; hill-repetitions runs (short = VHI, long = HI); tempo runs (@LTP); over/under intervals (fluctuate around LTP ±0:05/mi); steady-state runs; critical-velocity runs; variable-speed intervals (toggle LTP↔MAS to maximize time >90% VO2max); acceleration runs (Billat); VO2max runs (descending/lactate/fartlek/fast-finish/progression targeting MAS); leg-speed runs (VHI, passive or walk-to-jog rest); 5K/10K/HM/MP pace runs.
