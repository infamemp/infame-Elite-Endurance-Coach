## L2 Workouts & Protocols

### [HPC-L2-001] Pre-Race Warm-up Protocol (Trevor Connor)

- Goal: Elevate muscle temperature, induce post-activation potentiation (PAP), and prime VO2 without accumulating fatigue markers (elevated lactate, decreased pH).
- When used (period/phase, prerequisites): Race day or hard test day; specifically for explosive or hard-start events (criteriums, time trials, track events). Not needed for events that start easy or long road races (where rolling around the parking lot is sufficient).
- Structure:
  - Aerobic warm-up: 15 to 20 minutes of moderate riding to warm muscles.
  - Neuromuscular / PAP activation: 3 to 4 sprints of 6 to 10 seconds.
  - Pre-race recovery window: Finish the last sprint 10 to 12 minutes before the event start time to allow fatigue markers (elevated lactate, low pH) to clear while retaining elevated muscle temperature and potentiation.
- Intensity anchors: Moderate riding for aerobic warming; maximal/explosive sprints for PAP; complete rest or light spinning in final 10–12 minutes.
- Total duration: ~25 to 32 minutes total including the final recovery window.
- Progressions / variations:
  - Long road races: No structured warm-up needed; easy spinning in the parking lot to assess competition.
  - Cold weather: Use heated clothing on core and legs after warm-up.
  - Extreme ambient conditions (excessive heat or cold): Forgoing warm-up entirely to remain in an air-conditioned room or warm car may produce better performance than warming up.
- Cues / notes: Traditional 45-minute warm-up with multiple ramp-ups and 6–8 sprints causes excessive fatigue that outweighs physiological benefits. Keep warm-up short and simple. Trainer offers control/focus on unfamiliar roads, but warming up on the course is optimal for crits and time trials.

### [HPC-L2-002] Appendix introduction: structure and general rules for all workouts

- Goal: Workouts are sorted into the six abilities of Figure 3.1: aerobic endurance, muscular force, speed skills, aerobic capacity, stamina, sprint power. Field tests are also included. This is not a catalog of all possible workouts, only common and basic ones.
- New sessions: change the structure of these workouts or combine two or more of them. Combining is recommended in Build and Peak, because races seldom have a single focus. Mass-start races are unpredictable: an initially disordered group places numerous demands on you, often in quick succession.
- Structure of every workout: warm-up, then main set, then cool-down.
  - The warm-up prepares you physically for the main set.
  - The cool-down gradually returns the body to a normal, recovered state. Markers of an appropriate cool-down are a lowered heart rate and reduced breathing.
  - Low-intensity rides need a relatively short warm-up.
  - The higher the intensity of the main set, the longer the warm-up and cool-down should be.
  - For high-intensity main sets (especially zones 4, 5a, 5b, 5c), the warm-up should build gradually into the higher zones. Use repeats lasting a few seconds to a minute, with long easy recoveries between. Such warm-ups should be 20 to 30 minutes.
- Intensity measurement:
  - Heart rate is preferred for aerobic endurance workouts (AE1 and AE2).
  - A power meter is best for muscular force, aerobic capacity, stamina and sprint power.
  - Few speed skills workouts require intensity measurement.
  - See Table 3.1 for heart rate and power zones.
- Variation: durations and intensities vary with the period of the season, recent performance, present fitness specific to the workout's demands, and current mental and physical readiness.
- Readiness rule: if unsure of your readiness for a long or high-intensity workout, do less. Pushing to or beyond current limits or readiness is counterproductive and favors illness, injury, burnout or overtraining. "When in doubt, leave it out. Doing less is seldom a bad decision."

### [HPC-L2-003] AE1 Recovery

- Goal: recovery ride the day after a hard ride or race, or when you don't feel up to a slightly harder ride. It also promotes development of aerobic capacity.
- When used: all periods. The aerobic endurance workouts are the focus of Preparation and Base, and continue into Build and Peak to maintain early-season aerobic gains.
- Structure: a relatively short ride on a flat course. Do the entire ride in zone 1 heart rate. Pedal in the small chain ring at a comfortably high cadence.
- Intensity anchors: HR zone 1.
- Cues / notes:
  - Do it because you feel fatigued or aren't mentally up for even a slightly more demanding ride.
  - Taking a day off is often the better choice when very tired or unmotivated.
  - An indoor trainer is advised to ensure low effort.
  - With an online app, do not "race" or try to keep up with other riders.

### [HPC-L2-004] AE2 Aerobic Threshold

- Goal: improve the body's capacity to deliver oxygen to the working muscles. With many such rides, heart pumping volume increases, blood oxygen-carrying capacity increases, and mitochondrial ability to use oxygen improves. It boosts aerobic capacity (VO2 max) and positively affects high-intensity workouts. It is one of the most important workouts. A second purpose is lipolysis (fat use for fuel).
- When used: focus of Preparation and Base; continued in Build and Peak to maintain gains.
- Structure: done in zone 2 heart rate on a relatively flat course. Ride steadily in zone 2 throughout.
- Avoid steep hills and frequent surges that push HR into zone 3, even for about a minute. Zone 3 raises lactate production, which interferes with lipolysis, and repeatedly going in and out of zone 3 diminishes the aerobic benefits.
- Intensity anchors: HR zone 2.
- Efficiency factor (EF) check:
  - After the ride, compute EF = power (usually normalized power) divided by average heart rate for the workout. Some apps (e.g. TrainingPeaks) do the math.
  - Go back about 8 weeks in the diary and find a previous AE2 ride of similar duration, course and conditions, then compare EF.
  - If AE2 workouts are frequent and consistent, EF should usually be higher now than 8 weeks ago, which is a very good sign that aerobic fitness is improving.
  - If it is not higher, you may need more AE1 and AE2 workouts.
- Variation, easy-hard combination (especially late Base and Build):
  - Hold steady in zone 2 in the first part of the ride.
  - With about 30 minutes remaining, raise intensity to zones 3, 4 or 5 in any manner (fartlek, intervals, repetitions, steady state; hills or flat).
  - Afterwards leave at least 10 to 15 minutes of cool-down in zone 1 HR.
  - The zone 2 portion must always come before the higher-intensity portion.
  - Do this combination no more than twice a week, separated by at least two days.
- Variation, cross-training in zone 2 HR (typically Preparation and early Base, see Table 4.1):
  - Options: running, hiking, walking (with or without a weighted backpack), Nordic skiing, snowshoeing, or similar aerobic activity. Keep it easy and avoid hilly courses.
  - Highly intense activities such as tennis, basketball or football are not aerobically beneficial.
  - Stay in HR zones 1 and 2 for that sport, which are unlikely to equal your cycling zones 1 and 2.
  - Use the talk test (Chapter 3): count aloud from "one thousand one" to "one thousand six". The HR at about one thousand six is quite close to your aerobic threshold (start of zone 3) for that sport.

### [HPC-L2-005] MF1 Flat Force Reps

- Goal: greater neuromuscular fitness in the legs, hips and lower back. It applies gym strength work on the bike, and is an early-stage step in developing power.
- When used: Base period, when strength training (weightlifting) starts the strength maintenance (SM) phase. Not for riders with especially sensitive knees, hips or lower back.
- Structure:
  - Long warm-up. Use a road with very light traffic.
  - Shift to a high gear (e.g. 53 x 14) and slow almost to a stop.
  - Accelerate by driving the cranks for only 6 or 8 strokes, i.e. 3 or 4 pedal strokes per leg. Stay seated.
  - Effort is extremely high on each stroke at a very low cadence of around 50 rpm or lower. Cadence rises gradually with speed.
- Progressions:
  - First time: one set is adequate.
  - If unsure about your knees: do one set of 6 to 8 total strokes, with conservative gear and effort.
  - A second set in a later workout: recover 3 to 4 minutes riding slowly in a low gear after the first set.
  - Experiment with gearing the first couple of times. If you err, err on the low-gear (easy) side.
- Cues / notes: do only one of these workouts weekly. High injury risk, especially to the knees: "Better safe than sorry."

### [HPC-L2-006] MF2 Hill Force Reps

- Goal: same as MF1 but on a hill, to increase stress on the legs.
- When used: Base period. Prerequisite: at least three MF1 sessions done, with no knee pain afterwards.
- Structure:
  - Warm up well.
  - Hill: short (30 to 50 yards or meters), moderately steep (about 6 percent grade), with little or no traffic. You must be able to make a safe U-turn at the top and bottom.
  - Coast to the base, shift to a high gear (somewhat lower than MF1, e.g. 53 x 16 instead of 53 x 14), and come to a very brief balanced stop without unclipping.
  - Stay seated and alternate driving the pedals down 3 to 6 times with each leg, otherwise pedaling normally. That is 6 to 12 total strokes. Never stand.
  - Up to 3 sets per workout.
  - Between sets: shift to a low gear and pedal gently 3 to 5 minutes on flat terrain. Do not shorten the recovery, and make sure the legs are well recovered before the next set.
- Intensity anchors: power is the only gauge (not HR). Strive for very high wattage on each stroke.
- Cues / notes:
  - High-risk, high-reward.
  - At the first sign of any knee tenderness, stop the workout, even if slight.
  - No more than once per week.

### [HPC-L2-007] MF3 Hill Repeats

- Goal: muscular force (neuromuscular fitness) on the bike.
- When used: Base period.
- Structure:
  - Hill of about 6 to 8 percent grade that takes 20 to 30 seconds to climb.
  - 3 to 8 climbing repeats with 3 to 4 minutes recovery between. Recover by coasting down before the next rep.
  - Climb seated with minimal upper-body movement. Do not stand.
  - Gear that gives a cadence of only about 70 rpm or lower.
- Intensity anchors: power zones 5a and 5b for each climb.
- Cues / notes:
  - Stop the workout if knees become sensitive to the load.
  - No more than once per week.
  - Do not do it at all if prone to knee injury.

### [HPC-L2-008] SS1 High-Cadence Pedaling

- Goal: refine pedaling skill. Speed skills workouts are best done in Base, and matter most in the first three years of serious riding.
- Structure:
  - Indoor trainer, after a brief warm-up.
  - Pedal at 95 rpm or higher for a minute or more in zone 1 HR.
  - Take long breaks of 2 minutes or more at easy, comfortably low cadence, then raise the cadence again.
  - Challenge yourself to see how high a cadence you can sustain in zone 1.
  - Repeat 4 to 9 times.
- Intensity anchors: HR zone 1; cadence 95 rpm or higher.
- Cues / notes: to raise cadence, relax the entire body, especially toes and fingers. Hold the handlebars gently and try to wiggle your toes as cadence increases.

### [HPC-L2-009] SS2 Cornering

- Goal: improve cornering, which often decides performance in races, especially criteriums.
- When used: Base period.
- Structure:
  - Start by riding behind someone with good handling skills on a course with lots of turns. Or practice alone in a low-traffic area with clean, dry corners.
  - Practice several techniques: lean both bike and body into the turn; lean the body while keeping the bike upright; keep the body upright while leaning the bike.
  - Practice several speeds and different angles of approach.
- Intensity anchors: HR and power are not important. The only concern is cornering skill.

### [HPC-L2-010] SS3 Descending

- Goal: learn to descend at high speed. This is perhaps the most challenging skill for many riders, and the risk of a bad crash is quite high.
- When used: Base period.
- Structure:
  - Ride with someone who has very good skills.
  - Follow that rider's wheel using the same braking patterns and lines on straights and corners.
- Cues / notes:
  - The first several times you are unlikely to ride as fast as your model. Be patient, since skills take patience and persistence.
  - In the meantime be conservative with speed on descents.

### [HPC-L2-011] AC1 Group Ride

- Goal: simulate racing and develop aerobic capacity (VO2 max). Aerobic capacity is one of the best predictors of fitness. It is not necessary to know your VO2 max number for training, and the T2 field test gives a power number to track.
- When used: Build and Peak periods.
- Structure: an unstructured group ride, or a C-priority race. Include frequent maximal efforts, as in fast group rides. Reach zones 5a and 5b power for several seconds to a few minutes, several times during the ride.
- Intensity anchors: power zones 5a and 5b.
- Cues / notes:
  - Each high-intensity surge is a "burned match" that should closely simulate race efforts.
  - In analysis, note how many matches you burned and the total time in zones 5a and 5b.
  - Be cautious about how intense it may become, and about road safety: pay attention to traffic and to riders who may not be skilled at group riding.

### [HPC-L2-012] AC2 VO2max Intervals

- Goal: develop aerobic capacity.
- When used: Build and Peak periods.
- Structure:
  - Warm up 20 to 30 minutes.
  - Several work intervals of 30 seconds to 4 minutes each. Flat to rolling course (frequent small hills), no stop signs, light traffic. As Build progresses, introduce hill climbs.
  - Recovery: easy zone 1 pedaling for as long as the preceding work interval (e.g. 3 minutes after a 3-minute interval). As fitness improves, gradually reduce recovery relative to work duration.
- Progressions: start Build with about 5 minutes of total work interval time (e.g. 10 x 30 seconds). Over several sessions build to 15 to 20 minutes per session (e.g. 5 x 3 minutes and 5 x 4 minutes).
- Intensity anchors: power zones 5a and 5b, set with the power meter. Cadence at the high end of your comfort range.
- Cues / notes: best done only once weekly.

### [HPC-L2-013] ST1 Fartlek

- Goal: begin stamina training. Stamina is how high a power output you can maintain for a long time (more than an hour). The longer your A race, the longer you should be able to hold a given power-based intensity.
- When used: late Base, as stamina training is initiated. Stamina workouts run from late Base through Build and Peak.
- Structure:
  - "Fartlek" is a Swedish term first used in the 1930s for an interval session whose intensity and durations are based on feel.
  - Long warm-up, then several work intervals mostly in power zone 3 (Table 3.1).
  - Stop each interval when you feel you've gone long enough, well before fatigue sets in.
  - Take a long recovery (your call) before the next interval.
  - End the workout and begin a long cool-down even though you could have done more.
- Intensity anchors: power zone 3 (as printed, "zones 3").
- Cues / notes: be very conservative with durations. It is very early in the season and probably your first higher-intensity effort.

### [HPC-L2-014] ST2 Tempo Intervals

- Goal: increase stamina.
- When used: Base (late) through Build and Peak.
- Structure:
  - Long warm-up.
  - 3 to 5 work intervals of 12 to 20 minutes in power zone 3, with recoveries about one-fourth as long (3 to 5 minutes). E.g. after a 16-minute interval, recover 4 minutes.
  - Recover by pedaling easily in zone 1 HR.
  - Stay seated for each interval.
  - Mostly flat course, rolling hills, or indoor trainer. Avoid heavy traffic and frequent stop signs.
- Progressions: start with about 40 minutes of total zone 3 time. Over several weeks increase to 90 minutes or even longer.
- Intensity anchors: power zone 3.

### [HPC-L2-015] ST3 Cruise Intervals

- Goal: follow-up to ST2 Tempo Intervals, to increase stamina. Especially key for time trialists.
- When used: once ST2 is mastered.
- Structure:
  - Higher intensity and shorter work intervals than ST2.
  - 3 to 5 intervals of 8 to 12 minutes in power zone 4, totaling about 40 to 60 minutes (e.g. 5 x 8 minutes or 5 x 12 minutes).
  - Recover in zone 1 with easy pedaling for one-fourth of the preceding interval (e.g. 2 minutes after 8 minutes).
- Intensity anchors: power zone 4, very similar to 40 km time trial pace.
- Cues / notes: on a time trial bike, use the cadence you would use at that race distance.

### [HPC-L2-016] SP1 Form Sprints

- Goal: develop sprint technique and form. All riders must be able to sprint in some situations. Sprinting requires massive power and highly refined skills.
- When used: Base period.
- Structure:
  - Early in a ride, after a warm-up, do 4 to 6 sprints on mostly flat terrain.
  - Each sprint is about 5 to 10 seconds, followed by 3 to 5 minutes recovery.
  - Alternate standing and sitting while focusing on posture and technique.
- Intensity anchors: power zone 5b (could go higher, but don't). You should not be breathing hard after each.
- Cues / notes:
  - Best done alone, to avoid "competing". Head-to-head competition would be counterproductive this early in the season. Save it for later.
  - The most powerful sprints are standing with the butt over the saddle, hands in the drops, elbows bent. This lowers the torso to reduce drag. For safety in this position, keep your head up and eyes forward.
  - Cadence is a key skill to develop early in Base. Pure sprinters can't grind out a sprint finish in a high gear, so cadence should be high, probably well over 100 rpm.
  - Riders whose primary ability is not sprinting, e.g. time trialists and climbers, may find slightly higher gears and lower cadences more effective.

### [HPC-L2-017] SP2 Competitive Sprints

- Goal: race-like sprint power and tactics.
- When used: Build and Peak periods.
- Structure:
  - Within a ride, include several short and long race-like sprints. Do them with another rider or a group to better simulate racing intensity and tactical maneuvering.
  - Designate sprint primes, such as signs.
  - Use all the techniques of the form sprints, now at high intensity.
  - Recover several minutes between sprints.
  - Try different distances, terrains, road conditions, wind conditions, early and late in a ride, with and without lead-outs.
- Intensity anchors: power and effort maximal.
- Cues / notes: experiment to find what works. Determine the tactics that work best for common distances and conditions, so you know what to do in a race.

## L3 Exercises & Technique

### [HPC-L3-001] Sprint technique and body position execution cues

Execution cues for maximal sprinting (mastered during the Base period):
- Body position: Done standing with butt positioned over the saddle.
- Hand and arm placement: Hands in the drops, elbows bent to lower torso and reduce aerodynamic drag.
- Head and gaze: Head up and eyes looking forward for maneuvering and safety.
- Cadence:
  - Pure sprinters: High pedaling cadence, well over 100 RPM (cannot grind out sprint finishes in high gear).
  - Non-sprinters (climbers and time trialists): Slightly higher gears and lower cadences may be more effective when working on sprint skills.

### [HPC-L3-002] Leg weightlifting exercises (Figures 5.1-5.6)

Choose TWO leg exercises per session (twice weekly in Prep and Base 1); do not do all of them in each session; may change the two periodically for variety. All are free weights (barbell or dumbbells) except leg press (machine).
- Fig 5.1 Two-leg squat: feet pedal-width apart, about 7-8 inches/18-20 cm. Squat until knees are at about 90 degrees.
- Fig 5.2 One-leg squat (Bulgarian split squat, rear foot on bench, dumbbells): squat until forward knee is about 90 degrees.
- Fig 5.3 Deadlift (trap/hex bar): feet pedal-width apart, about 7-8 in/18-20 cm. Head up, back straight. Lift with legs, not back.
- Fig 5.4 Step-ups: platform height 13-15 inches/33-38 cm. Use dumbbells.
- Fig 5.5 Lunges: dumbbells. Forward knee about 90 degrees.
- Fig 5.6 Leg press (incline machine): feet about pedal-width apart. Knees about 90 degrees.
Depth: knees about 90 degrees; more acute angle raises injury risk and is not what happens when driving the pedal down.
Back-friendly: single-leg squats, lunges, step-ups, leg presses. Riskiest for back trouble: deadlift. Choose low-risk exercises relative to known knee, hip, back limitations.

### [HPC-L3-003] Core strength exercises (Figures 5.7-5.9)

Do ALL of these in each gym session; may add others (bridge, bicycle crunch, bug) or substitute ones that work for you.
- Fig 5.7 Front plank (forearm): hold 20 to 30 seconds for each of three sets.
- Fig 5.8 Side planks (forearm): hold 20 to 30 seconds for each of three sets, both left and right sides.
- Fig 5.9 Reverse plank: hold 20 to 30 seconds for each of three sets.
Why: core supports spine and pelvis, stabilizes body at high power, prevents power leak, improves economy. Signs of core weakness: lower back discomfort on or off bike; shifting butt toward saddle nose when working hard.

### [HPC-L3-004] Hip mobility exercises (Figures 5.10-5.12)

Include all three in each gym session (or use familiar alternatives).
- Fig 5.10 90-90 rotations: 10 to 20 repetitions in each of three total sets.
- Fig 5.11 Windshield wipers: three total sets, 10 to 20 reps per set.
- Fig 5.12 Cossack squats: three total sets of 10 to 20 reps each. Head high, butt low. May use light weight. Done as warm-up before weightlifting.
Why: cycling is in the sagittal plane; hips become weak/tight in side-to-side and rotational planes; hip flexors play a role in pedaling; weakness may limit performance, cause tightness/injury and left-right pedaling force imbalance (more in one leg).
