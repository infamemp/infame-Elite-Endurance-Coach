### [TPOP-L1-004] Luc Van Lierde: final-week tapers for Olympic-distance triathlon and Ironman

Tapering for Olympic-Distance Triathlons:
- Day –6: cycle–run transition training consisting of 60 km cycling of which the last 20 km are at race pace, followed by a 2 km run also at competition pace
- Day –5: low-intensity recovery training session
- Day –4: swim training session with intervals at race pace
- Day –3: low-intensity recovery training session
- Day –2: short, high-intensity running fartlek
- Day –1: low-intensity recovery training sessions

Tapering for Ironman Triathlon:
- Day –8: last extensive endurance training session in cycling (5 hr)
- Day –6: 1 hr run at about race pace
- Days –5 and –4: short and easy training sessions
- Days –3, –2 and –1: 1 hr bike session, a 20 min run and a 1 km swim

Caveat: these worked for him, but this does not mean they will be ideal for other athletes preparing for similar events.

### [TPOP-L2-004] Queally: 3-day taper cycle protocols (neural, neural-metabolic, metabolic)

- Goal: maintain speed and extend the distance over which it can be sustained.
- Structure of cycle:
 • "Neural component" (day 1): maximal efforts of 5 to 18 s (maximal power 2,200 W; power falls from 2,200 to 900 W over this time).
 • "Neural-metabolic sets" (day 2): maximal efforts of 30 to 33 s (2,200 W falling to 700 W).
 • "Metabolic training" (day 3): interval-type efforts and flying 1 km segments, gauged but bringing absolute fatigue on completion (maximal average power for intervals 800 W for 120 s; flying kilo 800-900 W for 60 s).
- Protocol A: Day 1 flying 100 and 200 m ×2, followed by 2 to 3 standing-start quarter laps. Day 2 standing-start 500 m ×3. Day 3 track intervals and flying kilos: (1) flying 500 m followed by rolling 750 m recovery, 4 repetitions per set, 2 sets per session; (2) flying 1 km ×2.
- Protocol B: Day 1 flying 100 and 200 m ×2, followed by two motorbike-paced efforts of one lap. Day 2 standing-start 500 m ×3. Day 3 turbo intervals: type 1: 30 s of 800 W followed by 60 s recovery, repeated 4 times per set, 2 sets per session; type 2: 20 s of 650 to 700 W followed by 10 s recovery, repeated 7 times per set, 2 sets per session.
- Notes: turbo intervals generate fatigue more effectively than track intervals, because on the track you must keep pedaling and focus during recovery, whereas on the turbo you can stop pedaling and recover properly. Type 1 came from a general exercise physiology textbook (30 s on, 60 s recovery, 4 reps); type 2 from Tabata and colleagues (1997). Only type 1 was used in the taper for Sydney 2000. The current protocol is the same pattern but excludes flying kilos and includes another type of interval (both intervals were part of specific training in 2000, used only occasionally and not in tapering).

### [TPOP-L2-005] Fairweather case: 1991 World Championships taper (complete rest)

- Goal: Arrive fully rested, relaxed, and confident for the World Archery Championships (Kraków, Poland).
- When used: 1991 WAC (age 21, self-coached). Traveled early from Australia to Europe to overcome jet lag; shot a preparatory warm-up tournament in Italy; arrived at the venue 1 week prior to competition.
- Structure:
  - Complete cessation of strength training and running from the moment of leaving Australia (2-week cessation based on literature indicating fitness would not be lost in 2 weeks while conserving energy).
  - Arrow volume tapered primarily through external venue restrictions (strictly scheduled range access and shared target lines).
- Intensity anchors: Pre-taper baseline was high volume shooting, lifting heavier weights than running, shooting a heavy-poundage bow.
- Total duration: ~2 weeks.
- Result: Gold medal (individual world champion); shooting performance progressively improved across the tournament rounds.
- Cues / notes: Low-stress amateur environment, high team camaraderie; basic lodging and scarce food did not impair performance due to high youth enthusiasm and psychological ease.

### [TPOP-L2-006] Fairweather case: 2000 Olympics taper (reduced running, kept easy running)

- Goal: Peak mental and physical sharpness under high-pressure Olympic home conditions (Sydney 2000) under a new 18-arrow match-play format with minimal margin for error.
- When used: Sydney 2000 Olympic Games; coached by Korean national team coach for 3 years, with rebuilt shooting technique.
- Structure:
  - Did not drop aerobic conditioning completely: reduced running volume but retained frequent easy running to relax and dissipate nervous energy/excess tension.
  - Reduced weight training compared to 1991, prioritizing running and match-play specificity.
  - Final week: easy shooting at an off-site local club to fine-tune technical nuances without facility stress.
- Intensity anchors: Easy jogging; light technical shooting.
- Result: Individual Olympic gold medal; equaled the Olympic record for an 18-arrow match in the opening round.
- Cues / notes: High pressure offset by professional support staff and coach; decision to retire post-Games allowed the athlete to "let go" of chasing results and focus purely on execution and positive process. A small technical correction early in the final week triggered constructive memories of his 1991 win.

### [TPOP-L2-007] Protocol: Captain's Run

- Goal: Take the team through the game plan, execute planned moves, acclimate to prevailing weather conditions at match time, and initiate mental focus and preparation for the test match.
- When used (period/phase, prerequisites): Day before a test match, scheduled at the match venue and as close as possible to kickoff time.
- Structure: Structured field exercise session led directly by the team captain. The captain walks and runs the squad through the tactical game plan, executing the planned plays and set moves for the upcoming contest.
- Intensity anchors: 60% to 80% of maximum intensity.

### [TPOP-L2-008] Protocol: Water Polo Major Tournament 60-Minute Training Session

- Goal: Maintain sprint sharpness, passing and shooting accuracy, and tactical preparation during major tournaments without inducing unnecessary fatigue.
- When used (period/phase, prerequisites): During major championships (World Championships, Olympic Games). Conducted twice on rest days (between games played every second day) and once on game days (optional, subject to travel time and down time considerations).
- Structure:
  - Part 1 (40 min total): Squad is divided into two equal groups:
    - Block 1 (20 min): Group 1 performs sprints and recovery; Group 2 performs passing and shooting drills.
    - Block 2 (20 min): Groups swap activities (Group 1 does passing and shooting; Group 2 does sprints and recovery).
  - Part 2 (20 min): Full-team tactical work addressing specific areas needed against the upcoming opponent (e.g., 6-on-6 defense, 6-on-5 attack).
- Intensity anchors: High quality and 100% intensity for sprint and tactical work; controlled volume.
- Total duration: 60 min (1 hr).
- Progressions / variations: On game days, evaluate travel time against resting time before deciding whether to execute the session.
- Cues / notes: High tempo, sharp execution, short duration.
