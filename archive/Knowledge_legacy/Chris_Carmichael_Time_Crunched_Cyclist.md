## 2. Philosophy & Training Principles (TIER A)

### P-01 — Core value proposition
Time-crunched athletes (≤6–8 hr/week) can achieve race-winning fitness by substituting **high intensity for volume**. Workload = time × intensity; if time drops, intensity must rise. Being time-crunched is NOT a disadvantage.
- tags: sport:cycling type:philosophy level:intermediate
- [SRC: TCC | 3rd ed. 2017 | Intro / Ch. 1]

### P-02 — The classic model breaks below 8 hours
The classic (top-down, pro-derived) endurance model relies on high-volume base training and requires >8–10 hr/week to work. Below ~8 hr/week there is insufficient stimulus to overload the body; athletes stagnate. TCTP was "reverse engineered" to solve a time constraint, not a change in sport demands.
- tags: type:philosophy type:methodology
- [SRC: TCC | 3rd ed. 2017 | Ch. 1, Ch. 2]

### P-03 — Time-crunched training yields time-crunched (top-heavy) fitness
Fitness is "top-heavy" — high-end power without a deep aerobic base to support it long-term. It will "collapse under its own weight" if stretched too far. This is a deliberate trade-off, self-limiting (reduces overtraining risk), and comes with defined Terms & Conditions.
- tags: type:philosophy type:caution
- [SRC: TCC | 3rd ed. 2017 | Ch. 3]

### P-04 — Value proposition framing (non-negotiable mindset)
"Do you want to be really good a couple of times a year, or mediocre all year long?" Accept limits (see Terms & Conditions) to excel at the events you actually do.
- tags: type:philosophy
- [SRC: TCC | 3rd ed. 2017 | Ch. 3]

### P-05 — Recovery IS training
Fitness gains occur during recovery/adaptation, not during the workout (the workout applies stress). For time-crunched athletes, recovery is rarely the problem — accumulating enough overload is. Low volume leaves ample built-in recovery.
- tags: type:philosophy type:recovery
- [SRC: TCC | 3rd ed. 2017 | Ch. 2]

### P-06 — Simplicity of workouts
Effective workouts are simple and "rarely sexy." Complexity lives in scheduling/balancing, not in the intervals themselves. "Keep it simple" (Ed Burke). Time-crunched athletes should not need mental calculus to execute a workout.
- tags: type:philosophy type:methodology
- [SRC: TCC | 3rd ed. 2017 | Ch. 5]

### P-07 — Every hour and every interval counts
With limited time, training must be systematic; haphazard training only works for novices before plateauing. Intervals are the primary training stimulus and take priority when time is cut.
- tags: type:philosophy type:methodology
- [SRC: TCC | 3rd ed. 2017 | Ch. 2, Ch. 5]

### The Five Principles of Training (from The Ultimate Ride, retained)
### P-08 — Overload and Recovery
Body adapts to properly applied overload + adequate recovery. Must apply to individual sessions AND blocks of training so loads accumulate. For time-crunched athletes, volume/intensity relationship is non-linear: increasing intensity yields an **exponential** increase in workload — enabling large workloads at low volume.
- tags: type:physiology type:methodology
- [SRC: TCC | 3rd ed. 2017 | Ch. 2]

### P-09 — Individuality
Program must be personalized (mileage, intervals, terrain, cadence). Train with friends but stay true to your own plan. Rarely a problem for time-crunched athletes (their schedule already forces individuality).
- tags: type:methodology
- [SRC: TCC | 3rd ed. 2017 | Ch. 2]

### P-10 — Specificity
Training must resemble target activity. For time-crunched athletes, apply specificity "from the opposite direction": limited fitness must be aimed at a specific narrow set of events/goals.
- tags: type:methodology
- [SRC: TCC | 3rd ed. 2017 | Ch. 2]

### P-11 — Progression
Must progressively increase load (via time and/or intensity — more/longer/harder intervals, shorter recoveries). Time-crunched athletes are maxed on time, so progression comes from manipulating interval type, number, length, and recovery. Progression is fast: workouts that felt hard early feel easy by week 11.
- tags: type:methodology
- [SRC: TCC | 3rd ed. 2017 | Ch. 2]

### P-12 — Systematic approach
Training must be planned/organized (like an architect's blueprint), especially critical when time is limited.
- tags: type:methodology
- [SRC: TCC | 3rd ed. 2017 | Ch. 2]

## 3. Physiology Foundations (TIER A)

### PH-01 — Three energy systems
1. **Immediate (ATP/CP):** high-power efforts <~10 sec; very limited stored ATP; minimal relevance to endurance cyclists.
2. **Aerobic:** body's primary/preferred system; burns carbohydrate, fat, protein simultaneously; waste = water + CO₂; powered by mitochondria.
3. **Glycolytic (anaerobic glycolysis):** meets short high-intensity demand; burns carbohydrate ONLY; produces lactate; less ATP per glucose molecule.
End product of all three = ATP.
- tags: system:aerobic system:anaerobic system:neuromuscular type:physiology
- [SRC: TCC | 3rd ed. 2017 | Ch. 2]

### PH-02 — Mitochondria & training goal
Primary endurance training goal: increase oxygen absorbed, delivered, processed — largely via increased **mitochondrial density** (size + number). More/bigger "power plants" = more aerobic energy per minute.
- tags: system:aerobic type:physiology metric:power
- [SRC: TCC | 3rd ed. 2017 | Ch. 2]

### PH-03 — Lactate & lactate threshold
Lactate = partially used carbohydrate, constantly recycled. Problem is not production but when removal/processing can't keep pace with production → disproportionate accumulation in muscle/blood = lactate threshold marker. Lactate threshold (LT) = maximum sustainable effort level. Key adaptation: process lactate back into aerobic metabolism faster → more work before accumulation + faster recovery. Best way to clear lactate = reintegrate into aerobic metabolism.
- tags: system:lactate system:threshold type:physiology
- [SRC: TCC | 3rd ed. 2017 | Ch. 2]

### PH-04 — VO₂max
Maximum amount of oxygen body can take in and use per minute; "engine size." High VO₂max ≠ guaranteed champion. Heavily dependent on cardiac output/stroke volume. Genetics dominant but trainable to a degree. LT responds to training better (percentage-wise) than VO₂max; a small VO₂max improvement has significant impact at lower intensities.
- tags: system:vo2max type:physiology
- [SRC: TCC | 3rd ed. 2017 | Ch. 2, Ch. 4]

### PH-05 — VO₂max reference values [DERIVED context]
- Average sedentary human: ~40 ml/kg/min
- Moderately trained cyclist: ~50–55
- Well-trained (Cat. III–IV / good in fast group rides): ~55–65
- High-level amateur / domestic pro: >70
- Elite pro (career): >80
- Tour contender performance marker: 6.8 W/kg for 30 min (pre-Tour test); Tour climbs raced ~4–5 W/kg after hours of racing
- tags: system:vo2max level:elite metric:power type:physiology
- [SRC: TCC | 3rd ed. 2017 | Ch. 2, Ch. 4]

### PH-06 — Endurance String Theory
View energy pathways as one continuous string (aerobic → glycolytic → VO₂max/ATP-CP), arranged by energy contribution. Improving one region "lifts" the whole string. **Targeting VO₂max has a greater lifting effect on LT and aerobic metabolism than aerobic training has on lifting LT/VO₂max.** "A rising tide lifts all boats." By challenging VO₂max even 10–20 min/week you pull up power at LT and moderate efforts — key to minimizing detraining.
- tags: system:vo2max system:threshold system:aerobic type:physiology
- [SRC: TCC | 3rd ed. 2017 | Ch. 2, Ch. 3]

### PH-07 — Fuel utilization by intensity
- Rest: fat provides ~80–90% of energy
- Very low intensity (20–25% VO₂max): ~70% fat
- 40–60% VO₂max: ~50/50 fat/carb (fitter athletes reach 50/50 at higher relative workload)
- >60% VO₂max: carbohydrate contribution rises sharply (glycolysis)
- Above LT (70–90% VO₂max depending on fitness): >80% of energy from carbohydrate
- Moderately trained athlete at subthreshold: ~50/50 fat/carb
- tags: system:fatoxidation system:aerobic type:physiology
- [SRC: TCC | 3rd ed. 2017 | Ch. 2, Ch. 3, Ch. 10]

### PH-08 — Why high intensity builds aerobic fitness
ATP depletion during intense exercise may trigger biochemical cascade increasing mitochondrial production (Willett 2006). Post-training: lower blood lactate at early test stages, reduced carbohydrate oxidation, reduced glycogen utilization, greater fat use at same absolute workload → increased endurance performance.
- tags: system:aerobic system:vo2max type:physiology
- [SRC: TCC | 3rd ed. 2017 | Ch. 2, Ch. 3]

### PH-09 — Carbohydrate storage limits
Muscles + liver store only ~1,600–2,000 calories of carbohydrate (glycogen). Fat stores are virtually unlimited. Bonking = low blood glucose. Deeper aerobic fitness → sustain higher power before eating into limited carbohydrate reserves.
- tags: system:fatoxidation type:physiology type:nutrition
- [SRC: TCC | 3rd ed. 2017 | Ch. 3, Ch. 10, Ch. 11]

### PH-10 — Cardiac adaptation & health note
Heart is a muscle; adapts to stress (higher stroke volume, lower resting HR). Recovery is key to positive adaptation. Under-recovery may negatively affect heart muscle similarly to skeletal muscle. Author's position: benefits of exercise (incl. strenuous) outweigh cardiac risks by a large margin; over 17,000 athletes (2000–2016), no observed increase in arrhythmia/cardiac events. If risk exists, likely due to lack of recovery in poorly designed programs, not intensity itself.
- tags: metric:hr type:physiology type:caution level:masters
- [SRC: TCC | 3rd ed. 2017 | Ch. 3 "Will High-Intensity Training Kill Me?"]

## 4. Glossary — Author's Definitions (TIER A)

- **Time-Crunched Athlete:** athlete with ≤6–8 hr/week available to train. [SRC: Ch. 1]
- **TCTP:** Time-Crunched Training Program — high-intensity, low-volume program; max 4 workouts/week. [SRC: Ch. 1]
- **Workload:** product of time (volume) × intensity; most accurately expressed as kilojoules of mechanical work. [SRC: Ch. 1, Ch. 2, Ch. 4]
- **Volume-at-intensity:** amount of time spent at higher intensities; TCTP retains/increases this while stripping moderate volume. [SRC: Ch. 2]
- **Lactate threshold (LT):** maximum sustainable effort level; point where lactate removal can't keep pace with production. [SRC: Ch. 2]
- **VO₂max:** maximum oxygen the body can take in and use per minute; "engine size." [SRC: Ch. 2, Ch. 4]
- **Kilojoule (kJ):** unit of mechanical work; 4.184 kJ = 1 kcal; because human cycling efficiency ~20–25%, kJ produced ≈ kcal burned (~1:1). [SRC: Ch. 4]
- **Power (watts):** = Torque × Angular Velocity; direct measure of work being done now. [SRC: Ch. 4]
- **Power-to-Weight Ratio (PWR):** sustainable power ÷ body weight (W/kg); always time-dependent (e.g., "4.0 W/kg for 20 min"). [SRC: Ch. 4]
- **Cardiac drift:** gradual HR increase over exercise duration at constant power/effort due to plasma volume loss; more pronounced >2 hr. [SRC: Ch. 4]
- **RPE:** Rating of Perceived Exertion; how hard you feel you're working. [SRC: Ch. 4]
- **Zero offset:** power meter sensor value at zero torque; establishes baseline; set before every ride. [SRC: Ch. 4]
- **CTS Field Test:** two 8-min all-out time trials separated by 10 min easy spinning recovery; establishes training intensities. [SRC: Ch. 4]
- **PowerInterval (PI):** maximum-intensity (VO₂max) interval; the most important TCTP workout. [SRC: Ch. 2, Ch. 5]
- **SteadyEffort PowerInterval (SEPI):** PI with gradual ramp-up then constant max output (flat plateau on power file). [SRC: Ch. 5]
- **Peak-and-Fade PowerInterval (PFPI):** PI starting with all-out acceleration then holding as power fades. [SRC: Ch. 5]
- **Cyclist's Paradox:** highly aerobically fit cyclists are poorly prepared for weight-bearing activity due to one-dimensional training. [SRC: Ch. 16]
- **Queen stage:** hardest day in a stage race (or self-planned tour). [SRC: Ch. 8]
- **Glycogen window:** ~30–60 min (up to 90 min) post-exercise period of most rapid glycogen replenishment. [SRC: Ch. 11]
- **WUT diagram:** Weight, Urine color, Thirst — daily hydration self-assessment (Cheuvront & Sawka 2005). [SRC: Ch. 13]
- **Form (Strava/TrainingPeaks):** Fitness − Fatigue = Form; readiness to perform. [SRC: Ch. 4]
- **Sweet spot (pack position):** ~10–25 riders deep in field — steady pace + safe draft. [SRC: Ch. 15]
- tags: type:glossary

Anchor = **highest** of the two average power outputs AND/OR highest of the two average heart rates from CTS Field Test. Multiply anchor by the percentages below.

| Workout | Primary Training Goal | % of CTS Field Test POWER | % of CTS Field Test HEART RATE |
|---|---|---|---|
| EnduranceMiles (EM) | Basic aerobic development | 45–73 | 50–91 |
| Tempo (T) | Improved aerobic endurance | 80–85 | 88–90 |
| SteadyState (SS) | Increased power at lactate threshold | 86–90 | 92–94 |
| ClimbingRepeat (CR) | Increased power at lactate threshold | 95–100 | 95–97 |
| PowerInterval (PI) | Increased power at VO₂max | Max effort (101 at absolute minimum) | 100–max |

### RPE scale (author's 1–10, outside lab)
- 4–5 = endurance/cruising; 6 = challenging aerobic tempo; 7–8 = LT work; 8 (sometimes 9) = climbing/TT; 10 = VO₂ intervals & all-out sprints.
- RPE × 10 ≈ % of VO₂max currently maintained (author's approximation).
- Lab standard = Borg Scale 6–20; Borg number × 10 ≈ HR.
- tags: metric:rpe type:formula
- [SRC: TCC | 3rd ed. 2017 | Ch. 4]

### Per-workout RPE assignments
FP = 7; EM = 5; T = 6; SS = 7; CR = 8; OU = 9; PI = 10.
- tags: metric:rpe type:workout
- [SRC: TCC | 3rd ed. 2017 | Ch. 5]

Strava's Fitness & Freshness uses FTP calculated from best 20-min power; will differ slightly from CTS Field Test result. Strava Form guidance: keep Form above −30 during purposeful training; before returning to training keep Form ≤ ~10–15; peaking Form ~20–25 (more is not better — training load too low, losing fitness). Same model as TrainingPeaks TSB (CTL/ATL).

## 6. Testing & Assessment Protocols (TIER B)

### T-01 — CTS Field Test (full protocol)
**Structure:** two 8-minute all-out time trials separated by 10 minutes easy spinning recovery. Budget ~1 hour total (warm-up + test + cool-down). Do a few days before starting TCTP; repeat each time you restart the program.

**Prerequisites:** well rested (do NOT test day after major race/hard century); properly fueled (see Ch. 11 pre-workout).

**Data to collect:** avg HR each effort; max HR each effort; avg power each effort; avg cadence each effort; weather conditions; course conditions; RPE each effort.

**Warm-up routine:** 10 min easy–moderate riding, then:
- 1 min FastPedal
- 1 min easy spinning
- 2 min FastPedal
- 1 min easy spinning
- 1 min PowerInterval (max intensity, 90–95 rpm; ramp up over first 30 sec, hold)
- 2 min easy spinning
- 1 min PowerInterval
- 4 min easy spinning
- Begin CTS Field Test

**Step 1 — Course:** flat, or consistent climb ≤5–6% grade. Avoid rolling hills, significant descents, steep climbs, stops. Indoor OK (no conversion factor between indoor/outdoor). Use typical weather (not unusually hot/cold/windy).
**Step 2 — Effort 1:** standing start; shift up progressively; reach top speed at 45–60 sec (not before).
**Step 3 — Pace/gear:** cadence >90 rpm on flat/trainer, >85 rpm on climb; avoid mashing big gears; breathe deeply (hyperventilating early = started too fast).
**Step 4 — Stay on it:** force pace through full 8:00; final minute "open the throttle."
**Step 5 — Recovery:** 10 min easy spinning; return to same start point (more important than exact 10 min). Keep pedaling.
**Step 6 — Effort 2:** repeat; do NOT pace off effort 1 — give everything (2nd effort may be higher).
**Step 7 — Cool down 15–30 min; consume carbs/fluids; record data.**

- Use the **higher** of the two efforts (power and HR may come from different efforts) to calculate intensities (Table 4.1).
- tags: sport:cycling metric:power metric:hr metric:cadence type:test
- [SRC: TCC | 3rd ed. 2017 | Ch. 4]

### T-02 — Why 8 minutes / recovery interpretation
Two 8-min efforts (vs. one longer TT) suit broad athlete range and reveal recovery ability. Well-developed aerobic engine → 2nd effort within 5% of 1st. If 2nd effort >10% lower than 1st: doesn't change prescription but flags a progress marker (recovery ability). Higher 2nd effort usually = held back on 1st OR insufficient warm-up.
- tags: type:test system:aerobic
- [SRC: TCC | 3rd ed. 2017 | Ch. 4]

### T-03 — CTS Field Test vs. lab LT / conversion factors [FORMULA]
- CTS Field Test avg power ≈ **10% above** lab-tested LT power. This 10% is **already factored into** Table 4.1 percentages — plug raw field test result directly into equations.
- Klika et al. (2007): field test power was 7.5% above lab LT (study at 9,000 ft; author retains 10% factor).
- Klika et al.: after 8-week CTS indoor program, avg +12.9% power at LT; CTS Field Test validated as valid fitness measure.
- Allen & Coggan 20-min TT method: multiply 20-min avg power × 0.95 → FTP. Relationship: 60-min TT ≈ LT power; 20-min ≈ 5% above that; CTS Field Test ≈ another 5% above 20-min.
- tags: type:formula type:test system:threshold
- [SRC: TCC | 3rd ed. 2017 | Ch. 4]

### T-04 — VO₂max testing (guidance)
Not necessary for TCTP. LT/field testing matters because VO₂max intervals are prescribed as "as hard as you can go" — accuracy not needed. VO₂max test can be tacked onto lab LT test if desired. Training aim: raise LT power to a higher % of VO₂max power (e.g., Craig Alexander went from LT = 77% to ~85% of VO₂max power). Ultrarunners often LT at 95–97% of VO₂max — need to raise VO₂max ceiling (Jason Koop approach).
- tags: system:vo2max type:test level:elite
- [SRC: TCC | 3rd ed. 2017 | Ch. 4]

### T-05 — Power meter setup for testing/training
- Zero offset before every ride.
- Set head unit to record ≥1×/second (reduce only for ultra events e.g. Dirty Kanza 200 battery management).
- Accuracy vs. consistency: consistency (day-to-day repeatability) matters more than absolute accuracy; a meter is "only accurate to itself" — use same meter for comparisons.
- Prefer double-leg (direct) measurement over single-leg (doubled arithmetic).
- tags: metric:power type:test
- [SRC: TCC | 3rd ed. 2017 | Ch. 4]

## 7. Methodology & Periodization (TIER A logic; TIER B numbers)

### M-01 — TCTP structure
Max 4 workouts/week: 2–3 weekday workouts of 60–90 min; 1–2 weekend rides of 1–3 hours. Weekly volume ~6 hr (option to extend to ~8 hr). Nothing longer than 3 hr (exceptions: Ultraendurance & Commuter chapters). Program length = 11 weeks.
- tags: sport:cycling type:methodology type:plan
- [SRC: TCC | 3rd ed. 2017 | Ch. 1]

### M-02 — Time-Crunched Periodization ("on/off switch")
Classic model = dimmer switch (4 periods: foundation, preparation, specialization, transition — gradual). TCTP = on/off switch, collapsing to **two hybrid periods**:
1. **Preparation/Specialization ("GO / ON")** = the 11-week program.
2. **Foundation/Preparation ("MAINTENANCE / OFF")** = 4–6 weeks between build cycles.
- tags: type:methodology phase:base phase:build phase:peak
- [SRC: TCC | 3rd ed. 2017 | Ch. 3]

### M-03 — Program timeline / peaking
- Peak performance ~week 8.
- Maintenance of peak through ~week 11.
- Less experienced riders may decline as early as week 9–10.
- Riders with >5 yrs experience can hold peak through week 11 (some stretch fitness 14–16 weeks total; best performances still in 8–12 week window).
- Weeks 9–11: workouts do NOT progress; workload constant (maintenance).
- Recovery/regeneration built in at week 4 (back off intensity for 1–2 workouts).
- tags: phase:peak phase:build type:methodology
- [SRC: TCC | 3rd ed. 2017 | Ch. 3, Ch. 5]

### M-04 — Race scheduling relative to program
- Criterium series (8 weeks): start TCTP 3–4 weeks before first race; best performances end of week 8–9.
- Series over 3–5 week period: start 4–5 weeks before first event.
- tags: phase:race type:methodology
- [SRC: TCC | 3rd ed. 2017 | Ch. 3]

### M-05 — Cycling the program / minimum recovery
- Use TCTP 2–3× per 12-month period (do NOT restart at week 1 immediately).
- **Minimum 4 weeks** between end of one TCTP and start of another; **6 weeks better**.
- Third cycle possible in warm climates (start ~February): spring peak, midsummer peak, fall/early-winter (cyclocross) peak.
- A recovery/maintenance period is required after ANY program in the book.
- Cannot go Century→Competitor directly; separate with 4–6 week recovery.
- After Endurance Training Block → TCTP: allow ≥1 recovery week.
- tags: phase:transition phase:offseason type:methodology
- [SRC: TCC | 3rd ed. 2017 | Ch. 3, Ch. 5]

### M-06 — Foundation/Preparation (Maintenance / "OFF" period) rules
- Recover from **intensity, not volume** — keep riding 6–8 hr/week; back off intensity.
- Ride at ~65–85% of max sustainable power (challenging tempo, > easy cruising).
- Include some intense efforts once/week (group ride, local climb) to prevent detraining.
- Expect significant decline in high-end fitness (VO₂max power, repeated max efforts) — normal, responds quickly on return.
- Aerobic endurance should be the highest priority during maintenance.
- tags: phase:base phase:transition type:methodology type:heuristic
- [SRC: TCC | 3rd ed. 2017 | Ch. 3]

### M-07 — Minimizing detraining during long layoffs
"If you don't have time for the long stuff, make sure to stick with the hard stuff." Even 10–20 min/week of VO₂max work (1–2 min PowerIntervals) preserves large portion of fitness (String Theory). Taylor Carrington: after 2 yrs of only 1–3 rides/week (endurance + PowerIntervals), field test power only ~10 W lower than 2 years prior (though efforts took more out of him / slower recovery).
- tags: phase:offseason system:vo2max type:methodology type:heuristic
- [SRC: TCC | 3rd ed. 2017 | Ch. 3]

### M-08 — Progression logic (why low volume can progress fast)
Volume's contribution to workload is so large in classic model that intensity must be added slowly. Low volume → intensity is greater % of workload + more recovery time → faster progression, but generates fatigue that limits peak duration.
- tags: type:methodology
- [SRC: TCC | 3rd ed. 2017 | Ch. 3]

### M-09 — Block training (2-day)
Power meters enabled 2-day training blocks: complete 2nd interval day in somewhat fatigued state to reinforce stimulus and give a "more urgent request for adaptation." Featured prominently in the programs.
- tags: type:methodology metric:power
- [SRC: TCC | 3rd ed. 2017 | Ch. 4]

### M-10 — The 3-Hour Limit (Terms & Conditions)
Best performances come in rides/events ≤3 hours. Derived anecdotally: TCTP athletes show marked performance change ~3 hr into long rides (tire, stop talking, skip pulls, struggle on climbs). Doesn't forbid epic rides — just means best performance is in ≤3 hr events. Covers most amateur events (most 30–50 yr old cyclists race 45–90 min; most amateur road races, even 60–70 mi, <3 hr). Century/Gran Fondo/Ultra programs address longer events via different interval emphasis.
- tags: type:methodology type:caution phase:race
- [SRC: TCC | 3rd ed. 2017 | Ch. 3]

### M-11 — Competition vs. endurance program emphasis
- Competition programs: develop power for repeatable efforts at VO₂max.
- Longer endurance programs: develop sustainable power at LT (more SS/OU, fewer PI).
- tags: type:methodology system:vo2max system:threshold
- [SRC: TCC | 3rd ed. 2017 | Ch. 3, Ch. 6, Ch. 7]

## 8. Training Plans (TIER B)

> Notation: RBI = Rest Between Intervals; RBS = Rest Between Sets. "3 × 8 min SS (5 min RBI)" = 3 SteadyState intervals of 8 min, 5 min recovery between. OU notation "3 × 12 OU (2U,1O), 5 min RBI" = 3 intervals of 12 min alternating 2 min Under / 1 min Over, 5 min recovery between intervals. TL notation "TL 1/3/5" = ThresholdLadder = 1 min PI, 3 min CR, 5 min SS. All rides are EnduranceMiles (EM) base with the intervals embedded within the stated total duration.

### PLAN-01 — New Competitor (Table 6.1)
For riders <5 yrs experience; crits, XC/short-track MTB, cyclocross, road races ≤~60 mi. Best performance weekend = week 8. Weeks 9–11 = hold fitness. (Similar to plan Rutberg used with John Fallon.)

## 9. Individual Workouts Library (TIER B)

### W-FP — FastPedal (FP)
- Goal: leg speed / pedaling smoothness (neuromuscular)
- Sport: cycling
- Structure: on flat road, light gearing/low resistance. Start ~15–16 pedal revs per 10-sec count (~90–96 rpm); after 1 min build to 18–20 revs/10 sec (~108–120 rpm) and hold for prescribed time. Continuous riding, few interruptions.
- Intensity anchors: RPE 7; HR NA; Power NA (do not use HR to judge)
- Cues: stay seated, hips smooth/no rocking, pull through bottom, push over top
- tags: sport:cycling system:neuromuscular metric:cadence type:workout
- [SRC: TCC | 3rd ed. 2017 | Ch. 5]

### W-EM — EnduranceMiles (EM)
- Goal: basic aerobic development
- Structure: continuous below-LT riding; keep power/HR in middle of range, allow fluctuation with terrain; pedal ~85–95 rpm; stay seated on hills
- Intensity anchors: RPE 5; HR 50–91% of highest FT avg; Power 45–73% of highest FT avg
- Caution: don't ride at top of EM range constantly (overlaps Tempo; spares power for intervals)
- tags: sport:cycling system:aerobic metric:power metric:hr phase:base type:workout
- [SRC: TCC | 3rd ed. 2017 | Ch. 5]

### W-T — Tempo (T)
- Goal: aerobic power & endurance
- Structure: intervals ≥15 min (up to 2 hr for pros); relatively large gear → cadence ~70–75 rpm; stay seated on hills; continuous, few interruptions
- Intensity anchors: RPE 6; HR 88–90%; Power 80–85%
- When used: NOT in TCTP; used in Endurance Blocks (Ch. 14)
- tags: sport:cycling system:aerobic metric:power metric:hr metric:cadence phase:base type:workout
- [SRC: TCC | 3rd ed. 2017 | Ch. 5]

### W-SS — SteadyState (SS)
- Goal: increase max sustainable power (power at LT)
- Structure: 6–20 min intervals near LT; recovery between = ~half the interval length; flat/small rollers; cadence 85–95 rpm; slightly below TT pace
- Intensity anchors: RPE 7; HR 92–94%; Power 86–90%
- Cues: if done on sustained climb, bump to ClimbingRepeat range; maintain zone intensity over cadence
- Progression note: struggling final 2–3 min is normal; not cause to skip next interval
- tags: sport:cycling system:threshold metric:power metric:hr phase:build type:workout
- [SRC: TCC | 3rd ed. 2017 | Ch. 5]

### W-CR — ClimbingRepeats (CR)
- Goal: increase power at LT with climbing-specific load
- Structure: long steady climb; just below LT power/HR; cadence 70–85 rpm; recovery ~half interval length; avoid interruptions
- Intensity anchors: RPE 8; HR 95–97%; Power 95–100%
- When used: not stand-alone in TCTP; component of OverUnders and ThresholdLadders
- tags: sport:cycling system:threshold metric:power metric:cadence phase:build type:workout
- [SRC: TCC | 3rd ed. 2017 | Ch. 5]

### W-PI — PowerIntervals (PI) — two subtypes
- Goal: increase power at VO₂max ("most important workouts in the TCTP")
- Structure: maximal efforts; any terrain except sustained descents; moderate gear, cadence ≥100 rpm best (>90 rpm minimum)
- Intensity anchors: RPE 10; HR 100% max; Power ≥101% of highest FT avg
- Recovery: typically 1:1 work:recovery (intentionally incomplete)
- **W-PI-SEPI (SteadyEffort):** ramp up over first 30–45 sec, hold constant max (flat plateau); used earlier weeks
- **W-PI-PFPI (Peak-and-Fade):** all-out from start, power falls after first 40–60 sec, keep pushing; keep cadence >90 rpm; reserved for later weeks (generates most lactate)
- tags: sport:cycling system:vo2max metric:power metric:cadence phase:build phase:peak type:workout
- [SRC: TCC | 3rd ed. 2017 | Ch. 5]

### W-OU — OverUnder Intervals (OU)
- Goal: advanced SS; handle pace changes; generate + process lactate at high intensity
- Structure: "Under" = SS range, "Over" = CR range. Ramp to SS in first 45–60 sec, hold Under time, surge to Over time, return to Under; always END on Over. Recovery ~half interval length. Flat/rollers/gradual climb (3–6%); cadence ≥90 rpm flat, ≥85 rpm climbing.
- Intensity anchors: RPE 9; HR 92–94% (Under) / 95–97% (Over); Power 86–90% (Under) / 95–100% (Over)
- Notation: "3×12 OU (2U,1O)" = three 12-min intervals; within each cycle 2 min Under + 1 min Over, repeat cycles to end of interval (4 cycles in this example)
- Advanced variant: alternate SS with PI intensities instead of SS/CR
- tags: sport:cycling system:threshold system:vo2max metric:power phase:build type:workout
- [SRC: TCC | 3rd ed. 2017 | Ch. 5, Figure 5.3]

### W-TL — ThresholdLadders (TL)
- Goal: mimic breakaways/race starts — hard accel then sustained power; boost power at threshold + repeat attacks
- Structure: 1–2 min all-out PI → immediately 3–4 min CR (95–100% threshold) → 5–6 min SS (86–90% threshold), no recovery within the ladder
- Notation: TL 1/3/5 = 1 min PI, 3 min CR, 5 min SS; TL 2/4/6 = 2 min PI, 4 min CR, 6 min SS
- Intensity anchors: use PI/CR/SS intensities per Table 4.1
- tags: sport:cycling system:vo2max system:threshold metric:power phase:build type:workout
- [SRC: TCC | 3rd ed. 2017 | Ch. 5]

### W-XPI-Runup — PowerIntervals with Run-ups (cyclocross)
- Goal: 'cross-specific fitness + dismount/run skill
- Structure: on 'cross bike; start PI (SEPI format) on bike toward hill/staircase (approach ≤45 sec); dismount, shoulder bike, run up as fast as possible; remount, finish PI with a couple pedal strokes; ~1 min recovery spinning back; repeat
- tags: sport:cycling system:vo2max phase:race type:workout
- [SRC: TCC | 3rd ed. 2017 | Ch. 6]

### W-XSkills — Cyclocross Skills Practice
- Structure: map a 3–5 min 'cross loop (sand, hills, stairs, singletrack, logs, barriers). All but last 10 min at moderate pace focusing on technique; last 10 min / last 2 laps all-out at race pace. Often add 60–90 min road ride; ~30–45 min skills.
- tags: sport:cycling phase:race type:workout
- [SRC: TCC | 3rd ed. 2017 | Ch. 6]

### Combination note
"60 min EM with 3×8 min SS" — the 60 min is TOTAL ride time; warm-up, intervals, recoveries, cool-down all included within it.
- tags: type:workout type:heuristic
- [SRC: TCC | 3rd ed. 2017 | Ch. 5]

## 10. Coaching Heuristics & Decision Rules (TIER A)

### H-01 — When to STOP an interval session (PI)
- If PI power drops ≥15% from one interval to next: add 1 min to the recovery immediately after; if next interval is no better despite extra time → done for the day. Do NOT add >1 extra min, do NOT change between-set recoveries.
- PIs are self-limiting: correct max efforts don't fade gradually — power "tanks," legs feel like bricks, ugly mechanics. When body says done, you're done.
- SS/OU: struggling final 2–3 min of an interval is NOT cause to skip next. Stop if you can't reach prescribed intensity within first 60 sec, or if holding power feels like all-out do-or-die TT.
- tags: type:heuristic metric:power phase:build
- [SRC: TCC | 3rd ed. 2017 | Ch. 5]

### H-02 — Fatigue assessment (power vs HR/RPE)
- HR & RPE often "lie" (suppressed HR + elevated RPE) but athletes can still match/exceed prior power → fatigue present but not enough to warrant rest day.
- Rest-day trigger: 2nd consecutive interval day power >10–15% lower than prior day AND HR suppressed >10% AND RPE elevated → take a rest day instead.
- tags: type:heuristic metric:power metric:hr metric:rpe
- [SRC: TCC | 3rd ed. 2017 | Ch. 4, Ch. 5]

### H-03 — When to SKIP an interval workout (decision ride)
Still get on the bike (don't lose the time). After 5–10 min moderate riding do "openers": 1 min FP / 1 min easy / 1 min FP / 1 min easy / 1 min SEPI (~15 min in). If SEPI feels dragging or power well below normal → switch to moderate EM ride. If merely low motivation → body responds and proceed with intervals.
- tags: type:heuristic phase:build
- [SRC: TCC | 3rd ed. 2017 | Ch. 5]

### H-04 — Schedule adjustment rules
- **Two-day interval blocks only:** OK to put 2 interval workouts back-to-back; then take a rest day. No 3-day interval blocks. Ideally 2 recovery days after a 2-day block; Wed/Thu/Sat/Sun pattern acceptable. Monitor next week; if struggling, convert a day to EM.
- **Hardest intervals first** in a block (freshest). Intensity/workload order (highest→lowest): PowerIntervals > OverUnders > SteadyState > Tempo > EnduranceMiles > Easy Spinning/Recovery.
- **No makeup days:** if you miss a workout, move on; don't reschedule (constant rearranging harms progress/balance).
- **Intervals take priority:** if cutting a ride short, keep short warm-up + interval set + short cool-down.
- tags: type:heuristic type:methodology
- [SRC: TCC | 3rd ed. 2017 | Ch. 5]

### H-05 — Do highest-quality work when freshest
Do intervals early in the ride (right after warm-up). Power declines through long rides (esp. >3 hr) and later climbs; doing CR/PI late risks missing target power.
- tags: type:heuristic metric:power
- [SRC: TCC | 3rd ed. 2017 | Ch. 4]

### H-06 — When to end program before week 11
Ideal: power stays same for identical workouts weeks 9–11. One low day = not alarming. Progressively worse through the week (even week 10) → start recovery period. Also stop if avg PI power drops and you can't complete workouts without dramatic final-set decline.
- tags: type:heuristic phase:peak
- [SRC: TCC | 3rd ed. 2017 | Ch. 3, Ch. 5]

### H-07 — Racing on weekends within a plan
- Replace Saturday interval workout with a race; continue plan as-is (race provides stimulus + more).
- Racing Saturday: Friday do 30–40 min easy w/ 3–5 × 1 min PowerIntervals to stay sharp.
- Racing Sunday: lighten Saturday to 60–90 min EM w/ 3–5 × 1 min PI + 2 × 3 min OU (2U,1O), recovery = interval times.
- Don't race both weekend days in first 6 weeks of any program; after that OK.
- tags: type:heuristic phase:race
- [SRC: TCC | 3rd ed. 2017 | Ch. 5]

### H-08 — Substituting training videos
Limit to 1 workout/week; match the workout's goal. PI-equivalent CTS videos: Speed Intervals, Tour of California Workouts, Max Power, Race Power, Climbing Speed, Race Simulation, Criterium, Cycling for Power. SS/OU-equivalent: Epic Climbing, Time Trial, Climbing, Climbing II, Threshold Power.
- tags: type:heuristic
- [SRC: TCC | 3rd ed. 2017 | Ch. 5]

### H-09 — Time-of-day for workouts
No evidence one time is superior; choose least disruptive to family/work (disruptive timing → skipped workouts).
- tags: type:heuristic
- [SRC: TCC | 3rd ed. 2017 | Ch. 5]

### H-10 — Repeat field test each program start
Yes — fitness fluctuates; ensures intensities match current fitness. Winter typically drops power more than a summer maintenance period → good winter training matters.
- tags: type:heuristic type:test
- [SRC: TCC | 3rd ed. 2017 | Ch. 5]

### H-11 — Commuting recommendations by athlete type
- Time-crunched athletes with short commutes (<5 mi): commute ≥3×/week (extra frequency helps quality workouts without unbalancing training).
- High-volume trainers (Masters/Cat. III training ≥12 hr/week): commute ≤10 min each way; a 30–45 min commute on top of 12 hr/week is too much.
- Occasional commuters can integrate the Ch. 6 plans (intervals Tue/Thu, drive other days).
- Racing bike messenger model = counterproductive (burnout).
- tags: type:heuristic level:masters level:advanced
- [SRC: TCC | 3rd ed. 2017 | Ch. 9]

### H-12 — Road vs. MTB for training
Do bulk of training (esp. midweek intervals) on road bike (time efficiency, consistent terrain, weather). MTB shows more coasting/lower pedaling time but higher power spikes (800–1,000 W). Specificity: MTB racers must ride MTB; from week 10 to race do weekend rides on MTB. Gravel/ultra: shift toward gravel/'cross bike as event nears (use road wheels if needed).
- tags: type:heuristic sport:cycling
- [SRC: TCC | 3rd ed. 2017 | Ch. 8]

### H-13 — Multiday self-planned events pacing
Days 1–2 relatively easy; put "queen stage" no sooner than day 3 (body settled into routine, bounces back better).
- tags: type:heuristic phase:race
- [SRC: TCC | 3rd ed. 2017 | Ch. 8]

## 11. Nutrition & Fueling Guidance (mixed; numbers TIER B)

### N-01 — Weight-loss philosophy for time-crunched athletes
Diet is already good; problem is HOW/WHEN/HOW MUCH, not WHAT. Focus on eating behaviors, not eliminating food groups. Primary focus = performance (health outcomes follow). No perfect weight; no perfect macronutrient ratio.
- tags: type:nutrition type:philosophy
- [SRC: TCC | 3rd ed. 2017 | Ch. 10]

### N-02 — Four key eating behaviors
1. Respond to hunger, not habit (hunger is trainable; ghrelin conditions to schedules).
2. Set expectations before eating (portion/meal intent governs amount).
3. Stop multitasking during meals (socializing OK; screens/work not).
4. Improve quality of choices.
Plus techniques: put utensils down between bites; start meals with low-energy-density food/drink (water, soup, salad); clean out cupboards; stock salty/sweet/savory snacks.
- tags: type:nutrition type:heuristic
- [SRC: TCC | 3rd ed. 2017 | Ch. 10]

### N-03 — Macronutrient basics [FORMULA]
Carbohydrate = 4 cal/g; Protein = 4 cal/g; Fat = 9 cal/g. Protein under normal conditions supplies 10–15% of exercise calories regardless of intensity. Endurance protein need: 1.2–1.7 g/kg/day (RDA 0.8 g/kg); >2 g/kg no added benefit.
- tags: type:nutrition type:formula
- [SRC: TCC | 3rd ed. 2017 | Ch. 10, Ch. 11]

### N-04 — Daily caloric expenditure calculation [FORMULA]
1. RMR (Mifflin-St. Jeor; weight kg, height cm, age yr):
   - Male: RMR = (10×weight) + (6.25×height) − (5×age) + 5
   - Female: RMR = (10×weight) + (6.25×height) − (5×age) − 161
2. × Lifestyle factor (Table 10.2): Very light 1.25; Light 1.55; Moderate 1.65; Heavy 2.0
3. + Exercise calories (kJ from power meter ≈ kcal, or Table 10.3 estimate)
Example: 37 yr male, 5'10", 150 lb → RMR 1,612; ×1.25 = 2,015; +~750–800 exercise = ~2,765–2,815/day.
- Best used over a rolling 3-day period, not day-to-day adjustments.

### N-05 — Ketosis / High-fat for time-crunched athletes (author's conclusion)
Not recommended for most. Ketosis: doesn't improve endurance performance (equal steady-state, higher energy cost of locomotion — Burke 2016); physiologically limiting for intermittent high-intensity; may prevent gastric distress in 9+ hr events (niche); disrupts training 2–3 weeks + months to adapt; weight loss is caloric restriction, not the macros; low compliance; will be corrupted by supplement industry; competitive disadvantage (inflexibility). Exogenous ketones = promising for "dual fueling" but unproven. Better: mixed diet + periodize carbohydrate availability.
- tags: type:nutrition type:caution system:fatoxidation
- [SRC: TCC | 3rd ed. 2017 | Ch. 10]

### N-06 — Train Low / Train High (carbohydrate periodization)
Do focused interval workouts + all competitions with HIGH carb availability; some longer endurance rides with LOW carb availability. Achieve "low" by fasted training or 2nd workout of day without replenishing. Effects: training low → ~8% lower power in 5-min max efforts (Yeo 2008); improved fat oxidation + resting glycogen. Lack of pre-workout carb preferred over restricting during-workout carb. Marquet 2016 "sleep low": +3% 10k run time (~73 sec), reduced fat mass, +12% time-to-exhaustion at 150% VO₂max. Recommended for time-crunched athletes (lower load absorbs the stress) — best during between-cycle weeks; NOT for elite amateurs on high-volume plans.
- tags: type:nutrition system:fatoxidation type:methodology
- [SRC: TCC | 3rd ed. 2017 | Ch. 10]

### N-07 — Carbohydrate = high-octane performance fuel
Glycolytic system burns carb only; carb powers accelerations/attacks/climbs. Key criterion: consume enough carb in overall diet to fully replenish glycogen before next session (start each session with full glycogen → longer time to exhaustion → more high-quality efforts). ~55–65% carb with adequate total energy virtually guarantees full replenishment in 24 hr; lower carb → likely but not guaranteed (monitor training performance).
- tags: type:nutrition system:anaerobic phase:build
- [SRC: TCC | 3rd ed. 2017 | Ch. 10, Ch. 11]

### N-08 — Post-workout nutrition (priority #1 due to time between workouts)
- Within 2 hr: 500–700 mg sodium + fluid = 1.5× water-weight lost (e.g., lose 2 lb/32 oz → drink 48 oz / ~1.4 L).
- Within 4 hr: 1.5 g carb/kg body weight (170 lb/77 kg athlete → ~115 g / 4 oz). Aim first 50–60 g within 30–60 min (glycogen window).
- Glycogen window matters most when training/racing 2×/day or back-to-back long days. With 24–48 hr between hard workouts, less critical (full replenishment in 24 hr from normal food).
- tags: type:nutrition type:recovery metric:load
- [SRC: TCC | 3rd ed. 2017 | Ch. 11]

### N-09 — Recovery drink guidance
Not needed after every workout. Warranted after rides accumulating ~1,500–2,000 kJ (very hard 90-min interval session or 3–4 hr moderate ride), or when training 2×/day / back-to-back long days. Ideal drink: fluid + carbs first, then electrolytes + protein; 2:1/3:1/4:1 carb:protein all fine; avoid protein-heavy/no-carb drinks and very high-calorie carb+protein drinks (overcompensation risk).
- tags: type:nutrition type:recovery
- [SRC: TCC | 3rd ed. 2017 | Ch. 11]

### N-10 — Protein myths (corrected)
- Large amounts NOT needed for recovery (1.2–1.7 g/kg/day suffices via meals).
- Best time to consume most protein = spread throughout day (protein not stored; satiating; slows digestion). Post-workout: focus carbs, some protein to speed carb uptake.
- Recovery drink not needed after every workout (see N-09).
- tags: type:nutrition type:recovery
- [SRC: TCC | 3rd ed. 2017 | Ch. 11]

### N-11 — Pre-workout meal
Get it digested before training. High-carb, moderate-protein, low-fat/low-fiber. Meal size smaller closer to workout: 1.5 g carb/kg at 3 hr prior; ~1 g/kg at 2 hr prior (170 lb → ~2.5 oz). Good: pasta, rice, potatoes, sandwiches, oatmeal+fruit, breakfast burrito. Avoid: fatty meats, cream/cheese-heavy dishes, high-fiber low-energy foods (salad).
- tags: type:nutrition phase:build
- [SRC: TCC | 3rd ed. 2017 | Ch. 11]

### N-12 — Pre-workout snack
In the hour before riding. Choose fast-clearing carbs (gels, sports drinks best). Author's routine before 2-hr rides: sports drink 30–60 min prior + gel 10–15 min prior (~50 g carb / ~0.65 g/kg). Consume 16–24 oz (0.5–0.7 L) fluid in the hour before. Consume ≥8 oz water with any gel/bar. Snack options list provides 50–75 g carb (reducible to 30–50 g).
- tags: type:nutrition phase:build
- [SRC: TCC | 3rd ed. 2017 | Ch. 11]

### N-13 — During-workout carbohydrate by ride length (Table 11.2)
| Ride length | Carb intake |
|---|---|
| ≤75 min | None necessary; drink water |
| 75 min–2 hr | Electrolyte drink or 1–2 gels |
| 2–5 hr | Carb and/or electrolyte drinks, water, gels, solid foods |
| 5+ hr | Taste, texture, variety increase in importance |
- If ride >75 min: start carbs within first 30 min. Body absorbs ~1–1.4 g ingested carb/min.
- tags: type:nutrition phase:base type:formula
- [SRC: TCC | 3rd ed. 2017 | Ch. 11, Table 11.2]

### N-14 — Longer-ride fueling numbers
- Standard: 30–60 g carb/hr (higher end as intensity rises). Racing/hard group rides ~50–60 g/hr; moderate century/group ~35–40 g/hr.
- Alternative: consume 20–30% of hourly kJ expenditure (e.g., 600 kJ/hr → 120–180 cal = 30–45 g carb).
- Typical hourly expenditure: hard interval ~800 kJ/hr; moderate group ~450–500 kJ/hr; ~600 kJ/hr good ballpark for amateurs.
- Product references: most gels ~25 g carb; energy bars 30–45 g; drinks 8–25 g/serving. Consume ~8 oz water with gel/bar.
- tags: type:nutrition phase:base type:formula
- [SRC: TCC | 3rd ed. 2017 | Ch. 11]

### N-15 — Hydration on rides
- Fluid loss up to 1.5 L/hr in a 1-hr workout (temp/humidity/sweat-rate dependent).
- Replace ≥80% of sweat loss. Minimum 1 standard bottle (~20 oz/500 mL) per hour (low-intensity, cool). Two bottles/hr for moderate–high intensity or warm; add a 3rd on hot days.
- Water is the one thing you can't skip even in a 1-hr session.
- tags: type:nutrition type:recovery metric:load
- [SRC: TCC | 3rd ed. 2017 | Ch. 11, Ch. 13]

### N-16 — Separate calories from hydration
Preferred strategy for most rides: water bottle = hydration; food in pocket = fuel. Allows independent adjustment (heat → more fluid without overloading carbs). At 35 g carb/20-oz bottle, two bottles = 70 g/hr (top of 60–90 g range) — more can cause gastric distress. Food can supply the 500–700 mg/hr sodium while drinking plain water.
- tags: type:nutrition type:heuristic
- [SRC: TCC | 3rd ed. 2017 | Ch. 11]

### N-17 — Sports drink composition
Simpler is better (lower osmolality → faster gastric emptying + carb/electrolyte transport). Key ingredients: carb (sugar), sodium, potassium, light flavoring. Lightly flavored, slightly tart, citrus, clears mouth well → drink more. Don't dilute heavily flavored drinks (reduces sodium concentration); instead choose a lighter-tasting drink at full strength.
- tags: type:nutrition
- [SRC: TCC | 3rd ed. 2017 | Ch. 11]

### N-18 — Early-morning workout fueling
~80% of liver glycogen depleted overnight; muscles still full. Need light carb (+ small protein) for blood sugar/brain focus: sports drink or gel, + 16 oz (~0.5 L) water/juice; optional espresso/coffee. Eat first (as soon as out of bed), then get ready; get started (don't burn time online).
- tags: type:nutrition phase:build
- [SRC: TCC | 3rd ed. 2017 | Ch. 11]

### N-19 — Caffeine
Effective ergogenic; safe ≤200 mg/dose. Improves motivation/alertness/concentration; may increase fat liberation; may accelerate glycogen replenishment post-workout.
- tags: type:nutrition
- [SRC: TCC | 3rd ed. 2017 | Ch. 11]

### N-20 — "Saving yourself" (bonk/cramp/GI rescue)
- Bonk: 2 gels + ≥half bottle water, or full bottle carb drink; convenience-store sugar+caffeine (Coke/Snickers) in a pinch.
- Cramp: consume 200–400 mg electrolytes immediately (1–2 gels and/or full bottle sports drink); pickle juice/mustard (strong taste, nervous-system-disruption theory). Slowing down + electrolytes likely helps, unlikely to worsen.
- GI distress: slow down, sip plain water, cool off (gut motility drops when overheated).
- Principle: come back from calorie deficit in minutes; hydration recovery takes much longer — prioritize fluid in the body over on the body.
- tags: type:nutrition type:caution
- [SRC: TCC | 3rd ed. 2017 | Ch. 11]

### N-21 — Focused fueling (weight-loss + performance)
Eat normally most of the day; add training-specific calories in the hour before + during longer workouts. Short workouts (≤2 hr) don't require baseline diet changes. Gels (~100 cal) hard to overeat vs. 600-cal coffee-shop treat. Base recovery-drink decision on ~1,500–2,000 kJ threshold.
- tags: type:nutrition type:heuristic
- [SRC: TCC | 3rd ed. 2017 | Ch. 11]

### N-22 — Food quality choices (summary)
- Fruits/vegetables: increase intake (low energy density, high nutrient density).
- Lean animal protein (<10% fat) + eggs: primarily for protein.
- Plant protein (nuts, seeds, beans, legumes): swap for sugary snacks; no-added-sugar nut butters.
- Whole grains: energy-dense carb, good for active athletes; modestly reduce grains (not fruit/veg) to cut calories while keeping nutrient density. Gluten-free only necessary for celiac.
- Dairy: fine if tolerated; full-fat filling but energy-dense + watch added sugar.
- Fats: unsaturated (nuts, veg, fish) preferable to saturated; bacon = strong flavor, use sparingly, no unique nutrients.
- Organic: no performance difference; personal/ethical choice; adaptability matters at races.
- tags: type:nutrition
- [SRC: TCC | 3rd ed. 2017 | Ch. 10, Ch. 12]

### N-23 — Commuter nutrition
Plain commutes: no dietary change (few hundred cal). Add fueling only when commute includes a hard workout or is in high heat. Morning training: simple breakfast (fruit, yogurt, whole-grain), tall glass water; recovery drink at office if commuting long distance (2 sessions/day). Evening training: ~1 electrolyte bottle 1 hr before; light snack if hungry; carry full bottle for ride home + rehydrate on arrival (up to 3 bottles for a 1-hr hot commute).
- tags: type:nutrition
- [SRC: TCC | 3rd ed. 2017 | Ch. 9]

### N-24 — Endurance Block nutrition
Hearty breakfast (larger cereal portion, toast, fruit, protein from eggs/yogurt; avoid greasy options). Start ride with 2 bottles sports drink (use 24-oz if available). Eat during rides: something every 45 min (every 30 min if only water); vary flavors/textures. Use recovery drink after back-to-back long rides (1.5 g carb/kg within 4 hr + ~⅓–½ that protein; fluids 1.5× weight lost). Relax and graze afternoon/evening.
- tags: type:nutrition phase:base
- [SRC: TCC | 3rd ed. 2017 | Ch. 14]

### N-25 — Ultra-event fueling & pacing
Burn rate falls with pacing: 2-hr hard road race ~850 cal/hr vs 10-hr MTB race ~500–600 cal/hr. Replenish only ~20–30% of burned calories on bike (closer to 30%). Adjust intake to expenditure; salty/bland options needed after ~5 hr (sweet gels/drinks may fail). Hydration crisis takes far longer to recover than calorie crisis.
- tags: type:nutrition phase:race sport:cycling
- [SRC: TCC | 3rd ed. 2017 | Ch. 8]

- Personal Leaderboard ("My Results"): track segment times over time; compare vs competitors ("Compare" → "Today").
- Analysis tab: avg power for ride sections; watch power decline (esp. >3 hr) to evaluate pacing/hydration/nutrition.
- Effort Comparison (Premium): compare vs PR/others on 5+ min segments; find point of divergence (fitness = later divergence, smaller slowing). Speed-based (susceptible to wind/temp).
- Power Curve (Premium): peak power across durations; VO₂max training raises 0–5 min region; LT training flattens middle.
- Fitness & Freshness (Premium): Training Load (power) / Suffer Score (HR); dose-response model (fitness builds slowly, fatigue faster; fatigue clears faster than fitness). Form = Fitness − Fatigue (see zone thresholds in Section 5).
- tags: metric:power metric:hr metric:load type:test
- [SRC: TCC | 3rd ed. 2017 | Ch. 4, Ch. 15]

### R-04 — Daily hydration monitoring (WUT diagram)
On waking assess: Weight (W), Urine color (U), Thirst (T). 1 indicator = dehydration less likely; 2 = likely; 3 = very likely. Use over rolling 3-day period. Thirst ~ correlates with ~2% body-weight dehydration (Kenefick 2012). Urine: clear–straw = OK (but clear can also = overhydration/hyponatremia); apple-juice-or-darker = dehydrated. Weight: 1–2% day-to-day loss = fluid change. Fix via increased daily water intake (not one big guzzle).
- tags: type:recovery type:test metric:load
- [SRC: TCC | 3rd ed. 2017 | Ch. 13, Figure 13.1]

### R-05 — Cooling for recovery
Post-exercise: reduce whole-body temperature (cold water/ice slurry drink, cold towels on torso, cool shower, cool—not icy—immersion). Avoid immediate ice baths after hard workouts — inflammation from normal exercise may be part of the adaptation stimulus (ice may blunt it). Cool room to aid sleep after hard/hot days.
- tags: type:recovery
- [SRC: TCC | 3rd ed. 2017 | Ch. 13]

### R-06 — Indoor training cooling
Overheating cuts power even in a 1-hr session. Multiple fans (airflow over max skin); air on the face (lowers thermal discomfort/RPE, extends time to exhaustion — Schlader 2011). Combine with cold drinks / cold towel on back/neck. No benefit to training in purposely elevated temp unless doing heat-acclimatization.
- tags: type:recovery metric:power
- [SRC: TCC | 3rd ed. 2017 | Ch. 13]

## 13. Adaptations by Athlete Type / Level / Sport

### A-01 — Experience level → program choice
- <5 yrs experience or returning/low fitness → New Competitor / New Century.
- ≥5 yrs experience → Experienced Competitor / Experienced Century-New Gran Fondo (faster progression, higher initial workload).
- Experienced riders retain adaptations, handle greater initial workloads, adapt faster, regain more former fitness.
- If <5 yrs but riding 3–4 yrs, may handle Experienced program — but use New program once first.
- Doubt → choose the New program.
- All programs start with LT intervals immediately; if off the bike / rarely above LT → spend 1–2 weeks of 60–90 min rides with 20–40 min Tempo before starting.
- tags: level:beginner level:intermediate level:advanced type:methodology
- [SRC: TCC | 3rd ed. 2017 | Ch. 6]

### A-02 — Peak duration by experience
Novices may decline ~week 9–10; riders with >5 yrs (10–15 yrs of miles, e.g., Swaim/Carrington) can stay competitive 14–16 weeks (best still 8–12).
- tags: level:beginner level:advanced phase:peak
- [SRC: TCC | 3rd ed. 2017 | Ch. 3, Ch. 5]

### A-03 — Highly trained cyclists
Volume effectively maxed; further gains require high-intensity intervals on top of racing (Laursen & Jenkins 2002 — added volume doesn't raise VO₂max/LT/mitochondrial density in highly trained). Pros already do "Train Low" and can benefit from carb-availability tweaks (long events, low body-fat premium).
- tags: level:elite level:advanced system:vo2max type:physiology
- [SRC: TCC | 3rd ed. 2017 | Ch. 2, Ch. 10]

### A-04 — Ultrarunners (cross-sport note)
Often LT at 95–97% of VO₂max; need to raise VO₂max ceiling via intervals (Jason Koop). Opposite problem to triathletes like Alexander (raise LT % of VO₂max).
- tags: sport:running level:elite system:vo2max
- [SRC: TCC | 3rd ed. 2017 | Ch. 4]

### A-05 — Climbers vs. big riders / PWR tactics
Higher PWR = advantage on climbs and steep-pitch accelerations. Small high-PWR climber: conserve before climb, attack steep pitches, surge over tops then aero descend. Big rider: keep pace high on flats/moderate grades to tire climbers; use power on flats/descents. Over rolling 40 km, higher PWR eventually rides away.
- tags: level:intermediate metric:power type:heuristic
- [SRC: TCC | 3rd ed. 2017 | Ch. 4, Ch. 15]

### A-06 — Rider stature & aerodynamics/TT
Tall riders: more power (longer levers/muscle) but hole in air grows less than proportionally → aero advantage. Small riders excel via high power-to-mass + hiding behind hands/tucking head.
- tags: level:advanced type:physiology
- [SRC: TCC | 3rd ed. 2017 | Ch. 4]

### A-07 — Masters / older athletes & strength
Metabolism decline is mostly reduced activity → lost lean muscle. Strength training maintains lean mass, metabolism, joint mobility, and non-cycling capability (combat Cyclist's Paradox). Not primarily to make you faster on the bike.
- tags: level:masters type:methodology
- [SRC: TCC | 3rd ed. 2017 | Ch. 16]

### A-08 — Female athletes with breast implants (heat stress)
Implants (saline/silicone) are inert heat sinks — not perfused, cool/heat out of sync with tissue. In heat: feel like a hot compress on chest → reduced thermal comfort/higher RPE. Solution: proactively cool the chest directly (ice, cold towels/garments, dousing) in hot weather. Unlikely meaningful pre-cooling ergogenic benefit.
- tags: level:intermediate type:physiology type:caution
- [SRC: TCC | 3rd ed. 2017 | Ch. 13]

## 14. Common Mistakes, Contraindications & Cautions

### C-01 — Don't overextend the program
Restart TCTP only after ≥4 (ideally 6) weeks recovery; don't run week 11 → week 1. Overextension causes fast performance collapse (top-heavy fitness). Don't exceed 12–16 weeks total.
- tags: type:caution phase:peak
- [SRC: TCC | 3rd ed. 2017 | Ch. 3]

### C-02 — Don't ride EM at top of range constantly
Overlaps Tempo → drains power for quality intervals. Keep EM mid-range.
- tags: type:caution system:aerobic
- [SRC: TCC | 3rd ed. 2017 | Ch. 5]

### C-03 — No 3-day interval blocks; no makeup days
See H-04.
- tags: type:caution
- [SRC: TCC | 3rd ed. 2017 | Ch. 5]

### C-04 — Don't force a broken PI workout
See H-01. Grunting through with tanked power = more muscle damage, no benefit.
- tags: type:caution metric:power
- [SRC: TCC | 3rd ed. 2017 | Ch. 5]

### C-05 — Heat is the enemy; dehydration cautions
- Dehydration ≥2% body weight impairs endurance; ~2% weight loss can = up to ~10% plasma volume loss.
- Effects: ↓VO₂max (via ↓stroke volume), disrupted pacing (higher %VO₂max at given power, higher RPE), competition for blood between skin/organs/muscles, accelerated overheating (reduced sweat rate as osmolality rises).
- May start day/workout already dehydrated → a "2% ride loss" can be ~4% total.
- tags: type:caution type:physiology metric:load
- [SRC: TCC | 3rd ed. 2017 | Ch. 13]

### TABLE 13.1 — Effects of dehydration & environment on VO₂max
| Study | n | Environment | Dehydration (% body mass) | ΔVO₂max (%) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Nybo et al. | 6 | 15°C perfused suit | −4 | −6 |
| Caldwell et al. | 47 | Room temperature | −4 | −3 |
| Webster et al. | 7 | Room temperature | −5 | −7 |
| Buskirk et al. | 13 | 26°C | −5 | −6* |
| Ganio et al. | 11 | 30°C | −4 | −9 |
| Nybo et al. | 6 | 44°C perfused suit | −4 | −16 |
| Craig & Cummings | 9 | 46°C | −2 | −10 |
| Craig & Cummings | 9 | 46°C | −4 | −27 |

- Note: cold-environment dehydration hurts far less than hot — control the heat.
- tags: type:caution system:vo2max metric:load
- [SRC: TCC | 3rd ed. 2017 | Ch. 13, Table 13.1]

### C-06 — Strength vs. power athletes & dehydration
Endurance impaired ≥2% dehydration in 68% of studies vs. only 20% of strength/power studies (Cheuvront 2014) — muscle cells retain water; wrestlers/boxers can cut weight + rehydrate between weigh-in and competition. Endurance athletes cannot.
- tags: type:caution type:physiology
- [SRC: TCC | 3rd ed. 2017 | Ch. 13]

### C-07 — Racer commuting caution
High-volume trainers should not commute far (steals recovery). See H-11.
- tags: type:caution level:advanced
- [SRC: TCC | 3rd ed. 2017 | Ch. 9]

### C-08 — Endurance Block skin/body care
Use sunscreen (SPF ≥15, liberal); protect skin from windburn; use chamois cream (chafing on 3+ hr rides). Adjust saddle if saddle sores.
- tags: type:caution
- [SRC: TCC | 3rd ed. 2017 | Ch. 14]

### C-09 — Endurance Block scheduling caution
Reduce training load the week before a block; don't combine block with heavy yard work/sightseeing; take required recovery week after Working Cyclist's block; consult family.
- tags: type:caution
- [SRC: TCC | 3rd ed. 2017 | Ch. 14]

### C-10 — Pacing mistake (century/long ride)
Riding aggressively from the start of a long ride → high early expenditure → crawling home. Aim for negative split (harder 2nd half); often yields faster overall time.
- tags: type:caution phase:race
- [SRC: TCC | 3rd ed. 2017 | Ch. 4, Ch. 15]

### C-11 — Ice slurry during exercise
Effective for pre-cooling, may be counterproductive during exercise (stomach thermoreceptors slow sweat/skin blood flow → net heat storage). During exercise prefer cool/cold (not icy) drinks. Prioritize fluid in the body over dousing when water is limited.
- tags: type:caution
- [SRC: TCC | 3rd ed. 2017 | Ch. 13]

### C-12 — Strength training injury caution
Push to failure only with GOOD FORM. Injury risk rises when weight/position control is lost (esp. bands, dumbbells, dynamic moves). Increase load (not reps/sets) to progress. LFHR carries lower injury risk than HFLR.
- tags: type:caution type:methodology
- [SRC: TCC | 3rd ed. 2017 | Ch. 16]

## 15. Formulas & Calculations (TIER B)

### F-01 — Workload
Workload = Time (Volume) × Intensity. Increasing intensity yields exponential (non-linear) workload increase.
- [SRC: Ch. 1, Ch. 2]

### F-02 — Power
Power (Watts) = Torque × Angular Velocity.
- [SRC: Ch. 4]

### F-03 — Energy conversions
4.184 kJ = 1 kcal. Cycling efficiency ~20–25% → ~4 kcal expended per 1 kJ produced → kJ on power meter ≈ kcal burned (~1:1). [DERIVED: pure conversion X kJ × 4.184 = Y kcal if 100% efficient.]
- [SRC: Ch. 4]

### F-04 — Power-to-Weight Ratio
PWR (W/kg) = sustainable power (W) ÷ body weight (kg); always time-specified. Examples: 250 W / 70 kg = 3.57 W/kg; 275 W / 68 kg = 4.04 W/kg.
- [SRC: Ch. 4]

### F-05 — Training intensity ranges
Intensity = (highest CTS Field Test avg power OR HR) × (% from Table 4.1). 10% field-test-vs-LT conversion already built in.
- [SRC: Ch. 4]

### F-06 — RMR (Mifflin-St. Jeor) & daily expenditure
See N-04. Male +5, Female −161 constant; × lifestyle factor (1.25/1.55/1.65/2.0) + exercise calories.
- [SRC: Ch. 10]

### F-07 — Post-workout replenishment
- Sodium: 500–700 mg within 2 hr. Fluid: 1.5 × water-weight lost within 2 hr. Carb: 1.5 g/kg within 4 hr (first 50–60 g in 30–60 min).
- [SRC: Ch. 11]

### F-08 — During-ride carb
30–60 g/hr, OR 20–30% of hourly kJ expenditure.
- [SRC: Ch. 11]

### F-09 — Endurance protein needs
1.2–1.7 g/kg/day (RDA 0.8 g/kg; ceiling of benefit ~2 g/kg).
- [SRC: Ch. 10, Ch. 11]

### F-10 — Form
Form = Fitness − Fatigue (Strava/TrainingPeaks TSB = CTL − ATL).
- [SRC: Ch. 4]

- tags: type:formula
- [SRC: TCC | 3rd ed. 2017 | Chs. 1–11]
