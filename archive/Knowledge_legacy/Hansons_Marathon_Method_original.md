## 2. Philosophy & Core Principles

### PH-1 — Cumulative Fatigue (foundational principle)
Core organizing philosophy, adapted from Arthur Lydiard. Cumulative fatigue = training that does NOT allow full recovery between training days, so fatigue accumulates strategically. Goal: take the runner *close to* the overtraining line but not over it. Simulate the *last 16 miles* of a marathon, not the first 16.
- Five interdependent components: **Mileage, Intensity, Balance, Consistency, Recovery.** Removing one nullifies the formula (domino effect).

[SRC: HMM | 2nd ed., 2016 | Ch. 1]
[EVIDENCE: author-opinion] (attributed lineage to Lydiard; framework is coach-derived)

### PH-2 — Meet the runner where they are
Stated non-negotiable goal: "meet you, the runner, where you are, and give you solid information you can put into use now." Transform runners into long-time marathoners, not just one-time finishers. Greatest predictor of exercise adherence = enjoyment.
tags: type:philosophy subtopic:retention level:beginner sport:running
[SRC: HMM | 2nd ed., 2016 | Preface; Ch. 1]
[EVIDENCE: author-opinion]

### PH-3 — Rejection of minimalist/3-day plans
HMM was created out of frustration with low-mileage, weekend-heavy plans. Author argues minimal plans (3 days/wk + megalong run) leave runners undertrained, produce bad race experiences, and drive attrition.
Characteristics of minimalist plans the author critiques:
- Low mileage: beginners 35–40 mi peak; advanced 45–55 mi.
- Low frequency: beginners 3–4 days/wk; advanced 5–6 days/wk.
- Megalong runs: reach 20 mi, some max at 26 mi; long run = 40–50% of weekly mileage.
- Very long duration: plans span 24–32 weeks.
- Misplaced intensity: all runs ≥70% VO2max.
tags: type:philosophy subtopic:minimalist-critique level:general-population sport:running
[SRC: HMM | 2nd ed., 2016 | Ch. 1]
[EVIDENCE: author-opinion]

### PH-4 — Build fitness "from the bottom up"
Improve aerobic threshold, anaerobic threshold, and aerobic capacity by pushing them UP from below (via large aerobic volume at ≤AT pace), not pulling from the top with high-end anaerobic work.
tags: type:philosophy subtopic:aerobic-development level:general-population sport:running system:aerobic
[SRC: HMM | 2nd ed., 2016 | Ch. 1]

### PH-5 — Balance / no single make-or-break workout
"Nothing is make or break." The long run alone does not make the marathon. All physiological systems must be trained: easy, speed, strength, tempo, long, recovery.
tags: type:philosophy subtopic:training-balance level:general-population sport:running
[SRC: HMM | 2nd ed., 2016 | Ch. 1]

### PH-6 — Best performance on race day, not in training
Programs designed so the runner feels best during the race, not during training. "You never want to execute your best performance in practice."
tags: type:philosophy subtopic:peaking level:general-population sport:running phase:peak
[SRC: HMM | 2nd ed., 2016 | Ch. 1]

### Three marathoner types the author coaches (segmentation)
- **Veteran runners:** logged many miles, run marathons before, want to reach next level.
- **Recreational runners:** not newbies (done shorter races), establishing a marathon baseline.
- **Just Finishers:** new runners / charity runners; goal is to cross the line, often plan to leave marathoning after.

## 3. Underlying Mechanisms

### MECH-1 — Marathon muscles / fiber types
- >600 muscles; 3 types: cardiac, smooth, skeletal. Skeletal = locomotion + energy storage.
- Muscle fibers bundled (one fiber type per bundle), each bundle controlled by a single motor neuron; fibers + motor neuron = motor unit. One neuron fired = weak contraction; multiple = stronger contraction.
- **Type I (slow-twitch):** aerobic, large capillary area, many mitochondria, slower shortening speed, fatigue-resistant, ~half the diameter of fast-twitch, smaller motor neurons (recruited first), efficient fat/carb use.
- **Type II (fast-twitch):** larger, faster, powerful, few mitochondria, anaerobic, high ATP use, fatigue quickly.
  - **Type IIa (intermediate):** more mitochondria/capillaries than other fast-twitch; considered aerobic; more forceful than slow-twitch.
  - **Type IIb:** powerful, anaerobic, fatigue quickly.

[SRC: HMM | 2nd ed., 2016 | Ch. 2 — Tables 2.1, 2.2 image-only]
[EVIDENCE: study] (general exercise-physiology consensus, no specific citation)

### MECH-2 — Fiber distribution & trainability
- Most people (any gender): type I distribution 45–55% in arms/legs.
- Fitness-conscious but untrained: ~60% type I.
- Trained distance runners: ~70% type I; elite marathoners even higher.
- Type I↔II conversion inconclusive; but type IIb→IIa transition documented after 10–12 week training block.
tags: type:physiology subtopic:fiber-distribution level:intermediate sport:running system:neuromuscular
[SRC: HMM | 2nd ed., 2016 | Ch. 2]
[EVIDENCE: study] (no specific citation given)

### MECH-3 — Fiber recruitment order
Slow-twitch recruited first; fast-twitch recruited only when: (1) pace increases, (2) resistance/hill encountered, or (3) run long enough to exhaust slow-twitch. Some runners rely on type I for 1 hr at modest pace, others up to 2 hr. Type I ~exclusive for first ~half of marathon; then IIa; undertrained runners forced to IIb → poor outcome.
tags: type:physiology subtopic:fiber-recruitment level:intermediate sport:running system:neuromuscular
[SRC: HMM | 2nd ed., 2016 | Ch. 2]

### MECH-4 — VO2max (definition & adaptations)
- VO2max = "volume of oxygen uptake" = max capacity to transport/utilize oxygen while running; e.g., 50 ml/kg/min = 50 milliliters O2 per kg body weight per minute. Higher = better.
- Elite marathoners tend to have slightly LOWER VO2max than elite 5K/10K runners; not the single best predictor of marathon performance.
- **Central (heart) adaptations:** improved coronary artery circulation; thickened ventricle walls (esp. left); enlarged ventricle chamber; decreased pulse rate.
- **Blood adaptations:** increased blood volume; decreased hematocrit (plasma ↑ → % ↓ but total red cells can ↑); less viscous blood.
- **Peripheral (muscle) adaptations:** ↑ capillary density; ↑ mitochondrial enzyme levels/activity; ↑ mitochondrial density; ↑ size of existing mitochondria.
tags: type:physiology subtopic:vo2max level:intermediate sport:running system:vo2max metric:hr
[SRC: HMM | 2nd ed., 2016 | Ch. 2 — Fig 2.1 image-only]

### MECH-5 — Anaerobic threshold (AT / lactate threshold)
- AT = point where lactate produced faster than cleared; a.k.a. lactate threshold / onset of blood lactate.
- Occurs at 60 to 90+ % of VO2max; best runners exceed 70% of VO2max.
- Identified as perhaps the best predictor of endurance performance. VO2max separates national-class from recreational; AT separates champions from contenders.
- Lactate is NOT the fatigue villain — it is an energy source. Real fatigue culprit: electrolytes (sodium, potassium, calcium). Potassium ion buildup outside the cell clogs the passageway, can't swap with sodium → weaker contractions (neuromuscular fatigue).
tags: type:physiology subtopic:lactate-threshold level:intermediate sport:running system:threshold system:lactate
[SRC: HMM | 2nd ed., 2016 | Ch. 2]
[EVIDENCE: study] (current research; no specific citation)

### MECH-6 — Aerobic threshold & fuel
- Fat: ~2× calories per gram of carbohydrate; near-endless stores; oxidized slowly.
- Fat = main fuel up to ~50% VO2max (painfully slow for most). Beyond that, body shifts toward carbohydrate.
- **Aerobic threshold** = the point where the body begins to burn through carb stores (fat can't be burned without oxygen).
- Carb (glycogen) stores ≈ 2 hours' worth; then blood glucose (runs out faster) → "hitting the wall"/"bonking."
- Optimal marathon pace ranges (as % VO2max): beginners 50–60%; trained recreational 55–65%; faster runners 60–80%.
- Training can't speed fat processing rate — so to use more fat you must burn a higher VOLUME (more/bigger mitochondria).
tags: type:physiology subtopic:aerobic-threshold level:intermediate sport:running system:fatoxidation
[SRC: HMM | 2nd ed., 2016 | Ch. 2 — Figs 2.2, 2.3, 2.4 image-only]

### MECH-7 — Running economy
- RE = how much oxygen required to run a certain pace. Lower O2 cost at a given pace = more economical (often faster).
- Depends on (1) high training volume/years, (2) speed training at target pace (train at a pace → become economical at it).
- Better predictor of race performance than VO2max.
- Example: two runners with VO2max 70 ml/kg/min; Runner B uses 55, Runner A uses 60 ml/kg/min to run 6:30/mi → B more economical.
tags: type:physiology subtopic:running-economy level:intermediate sport:running
[SRC: HMM | 2nd ed., 2016 | Ch. 2 — Fig 2.5 image-only]

### MECH-8 — Overload principle
Activity disrupting homeostasis → recovery mechanisms initiated → adaptation better prepares body for that stress. Basis for HMM training structure; cumulative fatigue applies overload without reaching overtraining.
tags: type:physiology subtopic:overload-principle level:general-population sport:running
[SRC: HMM | 2nd ed., 2016 | Ch. 3]

### MECH-9 — Metabolic efficiency (ME)
Body's ability to use stored fat vs carb at the right times. Poor ME → carb burned in higher amounts at lower intensity → early depletion/fatigue. 12 weeks endurance training at 65–85% VO2max increases fat use (study: at 64% VO2max, fat contribution rose from 60% → 65% of fuel). Easy runs occur at ~65% VO2max — the effort that develops ME.
- Worked example: 100 cal/mi with 40 cal from carbs; improved ME → 35 cal carbs/mi = 5 cal/mi saved × 26.2 mi = ~132 cal glycogen saved ≈ ~3.79 extra miles.

[SRC: HMM | 2nd ed., 2016 | Ch. 3 — Fig 3.3 image-only]

## 4. Glossary — Author's Definitions

- **Cumulative fatigue:** repetitive training that doesn't allow full recovery between training days; fatigue accumulates strategically. [Ch. 1]
- **SOS (Something of Substance) runs:** runs requiring more effort than an easy day; include speed, strength, tempo, and long runs. [Ch. 3]
- **Easy run:** run lasting 20 min–2.5 hr at 55–75% VO2max; paced 1–2 min/mi slower than goal marathon pace. [Ch. 3]
- **"Fast" easy run:** 1 min/mi slower than marathon pace. **"Slow" easy run:** 2 min/mi slower than marathon pace. [Ch. 3]
- **Long run (HMM):** capped at 16 mi (standard program); should be 25–30% of weekly mileage and 2:00–3:00 hr max. Author note: differs from common "20-miler" convention. [Ch. 3]
- **Speed workout:** interval/repeat sessions at 5K or 10K goal pace, run just under 100% VO2max. [Ch. 3]
- **Strength workout:** SOS run emphasizing intensity over volume at goal marathon pace minus 10 sec/mi (MP−10); higher-intensity, moderate lactate accumulation. [Ch. 3]
- **Tempo run (HMM):** a marathon-pace run. NOTE: differs from many coaches who define tempo as shorter/faster (near strength pace); here tempo pace = goal marathon pace. [Ch. 3]
- **Anaerobic threshold (AT):** point where lactate is produced faster than it can be cleared; a.k.a. lactate threshold, onset of blood lactate. [Ch. 2]
- **Aerobic threshold:** point where the body begins burning carbohydrate stores. [Ch. 2]
- **VO2max:** volume of oxygen uptake; max O2 transport/utilization while running (ml/kg/min). [Ch. 2]
- **Running economy:** O2 required to run a given pace. [Ch. 2]
- **Metabolic efficiency (ME):** body's ability to utilize fat vs carb stores at appropriate times. [Ch. 3]
- **Negative split:** running the second half of the race slightly faster than the first. [Ch. 11]
- **Positive split:** running the second half slower than the first (author advises against). [Ch. 3]
- **"Cautious confidence":** being realistic about race difficulty while trusting training. [Ch. 11]
- **Fractional utilization of maximal capacity:** ability to run at a higher pace for a longer period; raises AT. [Ch. 3]
- **Overpronation:** foot/ankle rolls inward excessively (flat-foot tendency). **Supination/underpronation:** foot stays on outside edge through toe-off (high-arch tendency). [Ch. 10]
- **Toe-off:** point at which toes propel runner forward as foot pushes off ground. [Ch. 10]
- **Three Two Rule:** no more than 3 marathons every 2 years (Elite Program rule). [Appendix A]

[SRC: HMM | 2nd ed., 2016 | Ch. 1–3, 10, 11; Appendix A]

### Q-7 — Taper parameters
- Last SOS workout: **10 days** before the marathon (SOS takes ~10 days to show physiological benefit).
- ~**55% reduction in overall volume** in the final 7 days.
- Keep the SAME number of running days (reduce daily mileage, not frequency).
- Taper can elicit improvements up to **3%** (e.g., 4:00 → 3:53 marathon).
- Author critiques 2–4 week tapers (risk losing fitness gains); HMM uses ~10-day taper.

### Q-8 — Recovery from long/hard workout glycogen depletion
- 2:00 hr running bout ↓ muscle glycogen by up to ~50% (Costill); takes up to 72 hr to bounce back.
- Continuous easy-to-moderate run at 70–85% VO2max ≥2:00 hr → greatest glycogen depletion (Noakes).

## 6. Testing & Assessment Protocols

### T-1 — VO2max lab test (author's view)
- Standard test: incremental protocol raising treadmill incline (not pace) → hard to translate to training paces.
- Yields VO2max, aerobic & anaerobic thresholds + corresponding heart rates.
- Author recommendation: get **RER (Respiratory Exchange Ratio)** collected too — reveals % calories from fat vs carb, calories expended at each intensity → dial in fueling & test whether goal pace is too aggressive.
- Verdict: one tool; a starting point/checkpoint. "Take it with a grain of salt."
- Case example (Celeste): VO2max Dec 2014 = 49 ml/kg/min at 7:40/mi max; May 2015 after Advanced plan = 60 ml/kg/min at 6:40/mi; then set marathon PR by ~8 min.
tags: type:test subtopic:vo2max-test level:intermediate sport:running metric:hr metric:power
[SRC: HMM | 2nd ed., 2016 | Ch. 1, 2 "Ask the Coach"]
[EVIDENCE: case-study] (Celeste)

### T-2 — 1-mile field test / time trial (goal-setting)
- Warm up as before any SOS workout.
- Run 1 mile as fast as possible at steady hard pace.
- Cool down; plug time into race-equivalency chart (Table 6.1) for marathon prediction.
- Longer time trial or race = more accurate. Half-marathon > 1-mile as predictor. Use multiple distances for a range.
tags: type:test subtopic:field-test level:general-population sport:running metric:pace
[SRC: HMM | 2nd ed., 2016 | Ch. 6]

### T-3 — Sweat-loss calculator (Appendix C)
Steps (per worked example):
1–2. Weigh before and after training; compute weight loss (1 lb ≈ 15 oz / 0.5 kg ≈ 500 ml fluid).
3. Record fluid consumed during session (e.g., 30 oz / 960 ml).
4. Add fluid lost + fluid consumed (15 + 30 = 45 oz; 500 + 960 = 1,460 ml).
5. Divide total by hours of training → sweat loss per hour (45 oz ÷ 1 hr = 45 oz/hr).

### T-4 — Gait/shoe assessment
- Examine wear pattern on old shoes: entire medial (inside) tread worn → overpronating, need more support; outside edge worn → supinating, need cushioning; even wear → likely correct category.
- Running-store treadmill + camera gait analysis; or sports-performance-lab gait analysis (~$100); or a basic force/pressure pad (shows arch outline + pressure points).
- Shoe fit checks: heel snug (no slippage); toe box has room in length & width; buy/fit at same time of day you run (feet swell).

## 7. Methodology & Progression Logic

### M-1 — SOS structure & weekly rhythm
- Two run categories: **Easy** and **SOS** (speed, strength, tempo, long).
- SOS days scheduled **Tuesday, Thursday, Sunday**; always an easy or rest day between SOS workouts (avoid back-to-back SOS).
- Never run hard workouts back-to-back; use active recovery (easy runs) between.
tags: type:methodology subtopic:weekly-structure level:general-population sport:running phase:build
[SRC: HMM | 2nd ed., 2016 | Ch. 3, 4]

### M-2 — Phase sequencing rationale
- **Speed early** in the program (not near race): marathon race-specific work (tempo/long/strength) belongs close to the race; speed builds VO2max/AT and provides a shorter-race baseline, and allows dress rehearsal for strength sessions.
- After several weeks of speed, focus shifts to **strength + longer tempo + longer long runs** → maintain VO2max, adapt to marathon fatigue. "Everything is solely focused on marathon preparation."
- Every 4 weeks, weekly mileage increases slightly via easy days, tempo runs, and long runs; vary stress and progress upward.
tags: type:methodology subtopic:periodization level:intermediate sport:running phase:base phase:build
[SRC: HMM | 2nd ed., 2016 | Ch. 1, 3, 4]

### M-3 — Speed workout progression (Advanced)
- Week 1: 400s; Wk 2: 600s; Wk 3: 800s; Wk 4: 1 km; Wk 5: 1200s; Wk 6: Ladder; Wk 7: 1600s; Wk 8/9: repeat sessions 800–1600 m.
- Full Advanced ladder sequence: 12×400 → 8×600 → 6×800 → 5×1000 → 4×1200 → 3×1600, then descend: 4×1200 → 5×1000 → 6×800 → finishing last speed week with 12×400.
- Each speed session = **3 miles total at faster intensity** + WU/CD + recovery.
tags: type:methodology subtopic:speed-progression level:advanced sport:running system:vo2max metric:pace
[SRC: HMM | 2nd ed., 2016 | Ch. 3, 4 — Tables 68–74 image-only]

### M-4 — Tempo run progression
- Beginner: jumps 5 mi → 8 mi tempo.
- Advanced: steady 1-mile bumps — 6, 7, 8, 9, 10 mi.
- Max tempo = 10 mi at GMP; total day (with WU/CD) can reach 12–14 mi and ~90 min.
- Rationale: improvements come faster for beginners; advanced need finer, more incremental work (a 6% gain = fewer minutes for faster runners).
tags: type:methodology subtopic:tempo-progression level:intermediate sport:running phase:build metric:pace
[SRC: HMM | 2nd ed., 2016 | Ch. 3, 4 — Tables 85/86 image-only]

### M-5 — Long-run scheduling logic
- Long runs on Sundays; increase by ≤2 mi week-to-week; ~25% of weekly mileage.
- Alternate a higher-mileage long run every 2 weeks (10 mi one week, 15–16 mi next) so as not to do three long runs every 8 days (tempo day totals ~15 mi with WU/CD count as long effort).
tags: type:methodology subtopic:long-run-scheduling level:general-population sport:running phase:build
[SRC: HMM | 2nd ed., 2016 | Ch. 4]

### M-6 — Adding mileage (progression, don't add intensity)
Once workouts peak in mileage, add volume via **easy days**, not harder running. Order to add mileage (from FAQ):
- Monday: +1–2 mi (1.5–3 km)
- Tuesday: maximize WU/CD to 2–3 mi (3–5 km) each
- Wednesday: add 30–60 min recovery run
- Thursday: same as Tuesday
- Friday: same as Monday
- Saturday: as scheduled
- Sunday: do all above first, then increase long run (stay within 25–30% & ~3 hr).
- Double runs only once averaging ~12 mi/day.
tags: type:methodology subtopic:mileage-progression level:intermediate sport:running metric:load
[SRC: HMM | 2nd ed., 2016 | Ch. 4, 5]

## 8. Structured Plans / Programs

### PLAN-1 — Just Finish Program
- **For:** goal = finish; new runner; never run a marathon; no SOS experience; want simple effective plan.
- Structure: several run days/wk, mileage significantly less than Beginner; less intense, slow careful buildup; controlled mileage & intensity (mostly easy runs).
- Key rules: run easy/conversational for most runs; build time on feet (intensity secondary); if you feel like running hard, do it then keep next day easy; NOT a walk/run program (walk breaks permitted as a teaching tool); should handle 10 mi/wk of running at start.

### PLAN-2 — Beginner Program
- **For:** performance-minded; time goal; first marathon but experienced at other distances; moderate–higher mileage; marathon experience but new to SOS.
- Starts 15 mi/wk → peak upper 50s.
- Phase layout: first 5 weeks all easy (base building, no SOS); week 6 introduces SOS (speed + tempo); then marathon-specific block (strength replaces speed, tempo & long runs lengthen); final ~10-day taper.
- Mileage jump wk 5 (~25 mi) → wk 6 (~41 mi) due to SOS start + loss of Monday rest/crosstrain. Optional smoothing: substitute 4–5 mi (6–8 km) easy Monday; do 8×400 instead of 12×400 Tuesday → wk5 ~29 mi, wk6 ~38 mi.
- Long-run buildup: gradual to 10 mi, then 15–16 mi alternating with 10 mi.
- Speed at 5K/10K pace; tempo at GMP; strength at MP−10.
- Total ~700 miles over 18 weeks (basis for needing 2 pairs of shoes).

### PLAN-3 — Advanced Program
- **For:** performance-oriented; marathon experience; experience running higher mileage. Best for ≥1 prior marathon (or first-timer accustomed to 50+ mi/wk).

## 9. Discrete Protocol Library

### P-1 — Easy Run
- Goal / target outcome: aerobic development (mitochondria, capillaries, fat-burning), active recovery, injury prevention via gentle overload.
- Domain / context: endurance-sport
- Total duration: 20 min – 2.5 hr
- Structure: continuous easy running.
- Intensity/dose anchors: 55–75% VO2max; GMP + 1:00–2:00 min/mi. "Fast easy" = GMP+1:00; "slow easy" = GMP+2:00.
- When used: daily backbone; slow end for WU/CD & day after SOS; faster end day after tempo / 2nd easy day before long run; Saturday can be faster if feeling good.
- Execution notes: truly easy; run too slow only breaks down tendon/bone without aerobic benefit; don't habitually run faster (injury risk).
- tags: type:protocol sport:running system:aerobic metric:pace subtopic:easy-run level:general-population
- [SRC: HMM | 2nd ed., 2016 | Ch. 3]

### P-2 — Long Run
- Goal: endurance, glycogen storage, fat utilization, capillary growth, mental coping; simulate LAST 16 mi of marathon.
- Domain / context: endurance-sport
- Total duration: 2:00–3:00 hr optimal window
- Structure: continuous easy-to-moderate run; max 16 mi (standard programs).
- Intensity/dose anchors: easy-to-moderate; 25–30% of weekly mileage.
- When used: Sundays; increase ≤2 mi/wk; alternate high/low every 2 weeks.
- Progressions/variations (Elite): Steady-State Effort; Last 3 GO!; Depletion (see P-9, P-10, P-11).
- Execution notes: beginners err easy; advanced hold moderate. Exceeding ~2 hr → muscle breakdown, diminishing returns.
- tags: type:protocol sport:running system:aerobic subtopic:long-run level:general-population metric:pace phase:build
- [SRC: HMM | 2nd ed., 2016 | Ch. 3]
- [EVIDENCE: benchmark] (Daniels 25–30%/2:30–3:00; Martin 90 min–2 hr/18–25 mi elites; Vigil 2:00–3:00 hr; Noakes 70–85% VO2max ≥2 hr; Costill 50% glycogen at 2 hr)

### P-3 — Speed Workout (intervals)
- Goal: VO2max, AT, running economy, myoglobin, mental toughness, pacing baseline.
- Domain / context: endurance-sport
- Structure: WU 1.5–3 mi → repeats at 5K/10K pace with jog recoveries → CD 1.5–3 mi. Total fast running = 3 mi.
- Intensity/dose anchors: 5K or 10K goal pace; 80–95% VO2max; just under 100% VO2max.
- Interval duration: optimal 2–8 min per rep (400 m ~2 min for beginner; too short wastes time, too long causes lactate buildup).
- Recovery: 50–100% of repeat duration (e.g., 2-min rep → 1–2 min recovery); beginners get longer recovery initially; shortened with fitness. "If too tired to jog recovery, running too hard."
- Example decoded: 2-mi WU / 6×800 m @ 5K pace w/ 400-m jog recovery / 2-mi CD.
- When used: early in program.
- Sequence: 12×400 → 8×600 → 6×800 → 5×1000 → 4×1200 → 3×1600 (Advanced ladder up then down).
- Execution notes: never exceed 100% VO2max pace (structural muscle breakdown, anaerobic reliance, injury). If can't reach 3 mi total, running too hard.
- tags: type:protocol sport:running system:vo2max system:threshold subtopic:speed-intervals level:intermediate metric:pace phase:build
- [SRC: HMM | 2nd ed., 2016 | Ch. 3, 4]

### P-4 — Strength Workout
- Goal: raise AT/fractional utilization, lactate tolerance & clearance, running economy; adapt to marathon fatigue; maintain VO2max.
- Domain / context: endurance-sport
- Structure: WU 1.5–3 mi → longer repeats (e.g., 6×1 mi) with SHORT jog recovery → CD 1.5–3 mi. Total = ~6 mi higher-intensity running (double speed volume).
- Intensity/dose anchors: **GMP − 10 sec/mi (MP−10)**; 60–80% VO2max; near half-marathon pace for faster runners.
- Recovery: fraction of repeat duration (e.g., 6×1-mi → ¼-mi jog ≈ 2:30–3:00 at 8:00 pace, <50% of interval).
- When used: marathon-specific phase (after speed block).
- Example impact: GMP 8:00 → 3:30 finish; 7:50 (MP−10) → 3:25 finish.
- Execution notes: don't exceed prescribed pace (adaptations occur at that speed). Use marked bike path/loop; avoid monotonous track.
- tags: type:protocol sport:running system:threshold subtopic:strength-run level:intermediate metric:pace phase:build
- [SRC: HMM | 2nd ed., 2016 | Ch. 3 — Table 3.4 image-only]

### P-5 — Tempo Workout
- Goal: internalize/practice race pace; running economy at GMP; endurance; nutrition/gear dress rehearsal; goal-validation.
- Domain / context: endurance-sport
- Structure: WU 1–2 mi → continuous run at GMP → CD 1–2 mi. Tempo distance progresses: Beginner 5→8 mi; Advanced 6,7,8,9,10 mi (10 mi at GMP; total 12–14 mi w/ WU/CD).
- Intensity/dose anchors: exactly goal marathon pace (below AT).
- When used: added ~same time as speed (Beginner wk6; Advanced 2nd week); continues through program before long run (cumulative fatigue).
- Execution notes: never hammer a tempo because it feels easy (compromises gains + pace-internalization). If struggling to hold GMP for long tempos → goal may be too aggressive.

## 10. Professional Heuristics & Decision Rules

- **Pace enforcement:** Elite team does push-ups for every second run too fast in a workout. Rule of thumb: "If too tired to jog the recovery interval, you're running too hard." [Ch. 1, 3]
- **Switching days:** SOS on Tue/Thu/Sun; if a day conflicts regularly, shift the WHOLE schedule consistently (e.g., all speed to Monday, long to Saturday). Keep an easy/off day between SOS. [Ch. 4]
- **Alt weekly combos** (more recovery): Combo A: Mon Easy / Tue Speed-or-Strength / Wed Off / Thu Fri's-easy / Fri Tempo / Sat Easy / Sun Long. Combo B: Mon Speed/Strength / Tue Mon's-easy / Wed Off / Thu Tempo / Fri Easy / Sat Long / Sun Sat's-easy (good for masters). [Ch. 4]
- **Added-recovery swap:** swap Thursday & Friday → 2 full days between the week's two hardest sessions. [Ch. 5]
- **Adding mileage priority:** increase easy days / run on rest day BEFORE touching the long run. Add Wednesday easy 4–8 mi = ~10% weekly increase. Easy days 6–8 → 8–10 mi → ~70 mi/wk. [Ch. 4, 5]
- **Doubles:** add only when averaging ~12 mi/day; consider two-a-days near 100 mi/wk (14–15+ mi/day). [Ch. 4, 5]
- **If not hitting workouts → back everything down to original volumes.** [Ch. 5]
- **Tired vs overtrained:** if hitting paces → cumulative fatigue (OK). If worn down, near illness, paces suffering → overtraining onset → scale paces, take a few easy days; check sleep/stress first. [Ch. 4]
- **Pain rules (run or not):** on 0–10 scale pain ≤3; no limp during/after; if you need medication to run/numb pain → don't run. [Ch. 5]
- **Illness rule:** fever → do NOT run (focus recovery); simple cold/runny nose → OK to push/modify. [Ch. 5]
- **Workout modification ladder (8 tiers, best→least):** 1 full as prescribed; 2 full translated to effort/time (e.g., 10×2 min on/3 min off); 3 full volume, modified (slower/less hard running); 4 full volume, easy only; 5 easy, less volume; 6 crosstrain + core/resistance; 7 core/resistance; 8 rest. [Ch. 5]
- **"Something is always better than nothing"** — even a 25–30 min run beats skipping. [Ch. 4, 5]
- **Goal-raising caution:** "You'll run a better marathon slightly undertrained than overtrained." Don't ramp goals late. [Ch. 6]
- **Race adjustment for heat:** ~+5 sec/mi at 60°F, +10 sec/mi at 70°F, +15 sec/mi at 80°F (also stated as 5–8 sec/mi in the 60s+). Individual; less if heat-acclimated, smaller runners, highly trained. [Ch. 11]
- **Race pacing:** negative split; "You can't mess anything up going out too slow, but you certainly can going out too fast." Don't try to make up early lost time (crowded start); ease back to GMP and pick up gradually. [Ch. 11]
- **Mid-schedule race adjustment:** replace midweek tempo with an easy day; race replaces a tempo (both stimulate AT). Best insertion: Beginner end of wk 8 (3 weeks into speed); Advanced end of wk 4 or wk 6. [Ch. 4, 5]
- **Shoe rotation:** rotate 2 pairs so cushioning recovers on off days → shoes last longer. [Ch. 10]
- **Racing-flat cutoff:** ~3:10 marathon (male or female). Slower than 3:10 → wear regular shoes; faster may choose flats. Consider lightweight trainers as transitional. [Ch. 10]

## 11. Applied Guidance

### Nutrition — calories
- Caloric need formulas (choose by day's run length; author expects both used across training):
  1. Light–moderately active (45–60 min moderate most days): **body weight (lb) × 16–20 cal/lb**.
  2. Very active (60–120 min moderate most days): **body weight (lb) × 21–25 cal/lb**.
  - Example: 150 lb × 16 = 2,400; × 20 = 3,000 → 2,400–3,000 cal/day.
- Men use middle–higher end; women middle–lower end.
- Aging adjustment: men cut ~10 cal per year of age past 20; women ~7 cal/yr (less if highly active life-long).
- Avoid large deficits / calorie-cutting diets during training (reducing ≥1,000 cal is harmful). Hard training alone tends to yield slight weight loss.
- Macro split: carbs **≥50%** (recreational 50–60%, competitive ~70%); fat **~20%** (nuts, fish, seeds, avocado; limit fatty meats); protein **~10–15%**.
tags: type:nutrition subtopic:calories level:general-population sport:running
[SRC: HMM | 2nd ed., 2016 | Ch. 8 — Table 8.1 image-only]
[EVIDENCE: author-opinion] (author notes "not registered dietitians")

### Nutrition — carbohydrate stores (facts)
- Total body carb storage ≈ **2,000 cal** (liver + muscles + blood). Muscles ≈ 1,500 cal but only working muscles' glycogen usable (no inter-muscle borrowing). Liver glycogen reserved for brain/CNS.
- Every gram of stored carbohydrate is stored with **3 grams of water.**
tags: type:nutrition subtopic:glycogen-stores level:intermediate sport:running system:fatoxidation
[SRC: HMM | 2nd ed., 2016 | Ch. 8]

### Hydration — thresholds & rules
- 2% hydration loss (~3 lb for 150-lb runner) negatively affects performance. 3% sweat loss → 4–8% ↓ aerobic capacity.
- Sweat rates ~1–2 lb/hr cool/dry; commonly 2–4 lb/hr.
- Every 1% body weight lost → HR ↑ up to 7 bpm and pace slows ~2%. At 8:00 pace, 1% loss ≈ 5 sec/mi slower; 2–4% loss → ~8:20 pace (3:29 vs 3:38 finish).
- Thirst-based drinking recovers only 68–82% of losses; thirst appears at ~2.3% loss (Butler et al., Clinical Journal of Sports Medicine, 2006).
- Rules: start early (drink within first 10–20 min / first stop); **2–8 oz every 15–20 min**; 1 gulp ≈ 1 oz, aim 4–6 gulps/stop; cooler fluids empty faster during exercise; don't overdo.
tags: type:nutrition subtopic:hydration level:general-population sport:running
[SRC: HMM | 2nd ed., 2016 | Ch. 8]
[EVIDENCE: study] (Butler et al., 2006)

### Hyponatremia
Three types: euvolemic (water ↑, sodium same); hypervolemic (both ↑, water far more); hypovolemic (both ↓, sodium faster). All dilute blood sodium. Serious (coma/death). Guidelines: use sports drink if session >1 hr; know sweat rate and drink to match; recovery drinks with electrolytes.
tags: type:caution subtopic:hyponatremia level:general-population sport:running
[SRC: HMM | 2nd ed., 2016 | Ch. 8]

### Fueling timing
- **Pre-workout:** SOS days prioritize carbs + fluids without over-filling; easy days less critical; if it costs sleep to fuel 1 hr early, prioritize sleep + bedtime snack. (Table 8.2 image-only.)
- **During workouts (>1 hr):** consume **30–60 g carb/hr** (60 g/hr if >4 hr). 8-oz sports drink = 50–80 cal; gel = 25 g carb. Total **200–300 cal/hr** (8 oz sports drink every 20 min ≈ 195 cal/hr). One gel every 30–45 min. Chase gels with water, NOT sports drink.
- **Post-workout:** first 15–30 min most important. Replace **20 oz (2.5 cups) water per lb lost**; take **50–100 g carb** (high-glycemic: banana, OJ, sports drink, cornflakes, oatmeal, baked potato, cooked carrots, bread, beans, ice cream); eat a carb+protein meal within following 2 hr (chocolate milk a good option). Plan ahead; refuel before returning home.

[SRC: HMM | 2nd ed., 2016 | Ch. 8 — Tables 8.2 image-only]
[EVIDENCE: benchmark]

### Race-week & race-morning fueling
- Final week: keep normal diet; **70% carbs** (mostly complex); expect +1–5 lb weight gain (normal, don't cut calories); hydrate all week (research: endurance ↑ up to 20% via full glycogen restoration).
- Day before: healthy beverage w/ every snack/meal; avoid gas/GI-disruptive foods, high-fiber foods, sugar substitutes; limit alcohol; healthy bedtime snack (unbuttered popcorn / bagel+PB / sports bar).
- Race morning carb guide: 1 hr prior 50 g; 2 hr 100 g; 3 hr 150 g; 4 hr 200 g. Measure liquid by sips near start.
- Late-night pre-race snack: body burns ~half of liver glycogen overnight; 100–200 g snack can reduce morning need.

## 12. Recovery, Monitoring & Lifestyle Factors

### Overtraining continuum (4 stages)
1. **Fatigue** — right after workout; recovery 24–48 hr (via easy running, not necessarily time off).
2. **Functional overreaching** (= cumulative fatigue) — target zone; recovery up to 2 weeks.
3. **Nonfunctional overreaching** — over the line; performance suffers; recovery several weeks.
4. **Overtraining syndrome** — training virtually nonexistent; recovery several months.
- **Key discriminator:** you have crossed into overtraining ONLY if performances are actually suffering.
tags: type:recovery subtopic:overtraining-continuum level:general-population sport:running
[SRC: HMM | 2nd ed., 2016 | Ch. 9 — Fig 9.1, adapted from Asker Jeukendrup, "Overtraining: Is It Real?"]

### Overtraining symptoms (self-monitorable)
Early fatigue onset; chronic muscle soreness; incomplete workouts; insufficient recovery; frequent infections/colds; irritability; loss of motivation/enthusiasm/competitive spirit.
tags: type:recovery subtopic:overtraining-symptoms level:general-population sport:running
[SRC: HMM | 2nd ed., 2016 | Ch. 9]

### Recovery aids (ranked by "return on investment")
- **Sleep (highest ROI):** baseline 8 hr; +1 hr for ~10 hr training/wk (→9 hr); 9.5 hr for 15 hr training; 10 hr for 20 hr training. REM releases growth hormone; protein synthesis high overnight. Tips: cool/dark/quiet room; consistent routine; no TV in bed; no caffeine 4–5 hr before bed; careful fluids before sleep. Naps: 20 min OR >90 min; avoid ~45-min naps (grogginess). (Source: Sage Rountree, The Athlete's Guide to Recovery.)
- **Nutrition:** post-workout **1.2 g carb/kg** ASAP (~82 g for 150-lb person) + repeat over next 2 hr. Rehydrate **150% of fluid lost within 5 hr** (2 lb lost → 3 lb fluid), spaced over 2–3 rounds, include sodium.
- **Stretching:** not immediately post-workout (prioritize fuel/fluid); do hours before/after; keep light if post-run.
- **Ice baths:** save as a "special treat" (cold blocks inflammation needed for adaptation). If used: soak 10–15 min after 2+ hr long run or intense 90-min SOS; 50–55°F water; within 60 min of run (best within 15 min).
- **Compression gear:** jury out; wear compression socks ~2× workout duration (2-hr run → ~4 hr).
- **Massage:** deep-tissue every few weeks (or monthly) breaks down scar tissue/adhesions; improves resilience.
tags: type:recovery subtopic:recovery-aids level:general-population sport:running metric:hrv
[SRC: HMM | 2nd ed., 2016 | Ch. 9]
[EVIDENCE: author-opinion] (compression "jury still out"); [EVIDENCE: study] (Rountree recommendations)

### Heart-rate & tool monitoring
- HMM does NOT prescribe by HR — pace is primary (system built on goal/race pace). HR/GPS = tools, not the boss.
- Use HR mainly to gauge intensity & monitor overtraining (trends > daily numbers). Don't wear monitor/GPS every day; learn to listen to the body.
- If using HR: get max HR + threshold HR ranges tested (VO2max test); 220−age formula OK for populations but individually questionable.
tags: type:recovery subtopic:hr-monitoring level:intermediate sport:running metric:hr metric:hrv
[SRC: HMM | 2nd ed., 2016 | Ch. 3]

### Post-race recovery timeline
- 2 weeks NO running (Elite rule; was 10 days, found too short). No new races scheduled.
- First 30 min: eat anything (calories priority). First 2 hr: carbs + fluids. Rest of day: hydrate, light walk. Days 3–5: no running. 2 weeks: optional light crosstrain. After 2 weeks: resume easy (beginners ~30 min every other day; veterans 45–60 min easy 6 days/wk building). RT 2–3×/wk on non-future-SOS days.
- 4–6 weeks post: build to average mileage (Advanced avg 35–45 mi; Beginner avg 30–35 mi), then structured training.

## 13. Adaptations by Population / Context

- **Beginner vs advanced pacing:** beginners run easy days closer to GMP+2:00 to transition to higher mileage safely; advanced handle faster end even after SOS. Beginners often find GMP slower than their easy pace early (low prior volume). [Ch. 3, 4]
- **Improvement expectations:** newer/less experienced = bigger % gains. 5:00→4:00 marathon = 20% improvement possible; 2:15→2:14 ≈ 1% (requires far more detail: nutrition, stretching, strength). 5% improvement: 4:00 marathoner ≈ 12 min; 2:30 marathoner ≈ 7:30. [Ch. 6]
- **Masters runners:** age less a factor than experience; eventually performance levels off/declines; training regimen may need to change to maintain. Alt weekly combos (extra recovery between sessions) suit masters. Recommended nap/sleep. [Ch. 4, 6]
- **Elite runners:** nine-day cycle, 120–140 mi/wk, longer SOS (proportionate %), naps + 8–10 hr sleep, medical support; peak ~29–30 yrs old (recruited at 23–25). [Appendix A]
- **New/charity runners (Just Finish):** intensity, not mileage, is the main injury driver (aerobic system develops faster than bones/tendons). Crosstraining as a bridge to more running days. [Ch. 4, 7]
- **Course/weather adaptation:** flat = slightly faster prediction; hilly = slower; hot/humid = adjust goal; large race corrals = add minutes. [Ch. 6, 11]

## 14. Common Mistakes, Contraindications & Cautions

- **Running SOS/easy runs faster than prescribed** = the biggest error → overtraining, burnout, injury; nullifies workout-specific adaptations; that IS junk mileage. [Ch. 1, 2, 3]
- **Speed above 100% VO2max** → structural muscle breakdown, anaerobic reliance, missed aerobic gains, likely injury. [Ch. 3]
- **20+ mile long runs (recreational):** cause mitochondrial/capillary damage lingering ≥8 weeks (1985 study of 40 males); "training to survive, not adapt." Avoid unless 90–100 mi/wk. [Ch. 3]
- **Tapering too much / too long (2–4 weeks cutting everything)** → sluggishness, lost fitness; keep frequency, cut only volume/intensity over ~10 days. [Ch. 3]
- **Starting anything new during training** (crosstraining, minimalist shoes, new race activity): "Don't start anything new until after 26.2." [Ch. 7, 10]
- **Racing flats if slower than ~3:10:** biomechanics/injury risk from insufficient cushioning at fatigue. [Ch. 10]
- **New shoes on race day:** never; race in shoes with 50–100 mi (2–3 weeks). Shoes last 300–500 mi (some 350–500). [Ch. 10]
- **Minimalist shoes without transition:** heel-strike in minimal shoes = landing force ~7× vs shod heel strike; injuries typically ~2 weeks (Lieberman et al., Nature 2010). Transition gradually over months; not for daily high-mileage training. [Ch. 10]
- **Overstriding / trying to lengthen stride** → heel-strike braking, more shock, slower pace. [Ch. 10]
- **Static stretching before workouts/races** → reduces force production/elasticity, tear risk; wrong time. [Ch. 7]
- **Ballistic stretching** (fast, bouncy, beyond ROM) → injury risk; avoid. [Ch. 7]
- **Ice baths / anti-inflammatories habitually** → block inflammation needed for adaptation. [Ch. 9]
- **Fever → do not run.** [Ch. 5]
- **Pain >3/10, limping, or needing meds to run → do not run.** [Ch. 5]
- **Skipping easy runs / inconsistency** → fitness lost fast (a week or two off retreats gains); 6–8 wk to recoup 3 wk gain. [Ch. 1]
- **Carbo-loading myth:** not multiple plates of pasta; eat a normal-sized healthy high-carb meal. [Ch. 11]
- **Standing at the expo** on hard floors — a common mistake; go early and leave. [Ch. 11]
- **Gel-only at mile 18** race setups: fuel BEFORE that or it's too late. [Ch. 11]
- **Overtraining from too many marathons/yr** → neglect of speed, stale performances; Three Two Rule. [Appendix A]
- **Medical/legal caveat:** authors are "not registered dietitians"; recommend consulting sports nutritionist/physician for specific guidance. [Ch. 8]

## 15. Formulas & Calculations

- **Easy pace:** Easy = GMP + 1:00 to 2:00 min/mi. [Ch. 3]
- **Strength pace:** Strength = GMP − 0:10 min/mi (MP−10). [Ch. 3]
- **Speed pace:** = 5K or 10K goal pace. [Ch. 3]
- **Long-run cap check:** long run = 25–30% of weekly mileage AND ≤ ~3:00 hr (optimal 2:00–3:00 hr). [Ch. 3]
- **Caloric needs:** BW(lb) × 16–20 (light-moderate) OR × 21–25 (very active) = daily calories. [Ch. 8]
- **Age caloric adjustment:** men −10 cal/yr past age 20; women −7 cal/yr. [Ch. 8]
- **kg conversion:** weight(kg) = weight(lb) ÷ 2.2. [Ch. 8]
- **Race calorie burn:** cal = 1 cal/kg/km × body weight(kg) × 42.195 km. Example 68.18 kg → 2,877 cal. [Ch. 8]
- **Carb fraction by intensity:** total cal × (0.55 @60% / 0.65 @70% / 0.825 @80% VO2max) = carb calories. [Ch. 8; Table 8.3]
- **Glycogen storage:** BW(kg) × leg-mass fraction (0.21 male / 0.20 female) × 80 cal/kg = available carb (local). [Ch. 8]
- **In-race carb intake:** 30–60 g/hr (60 g if >4 hr); total 200–300 cal/hr. [Ch. 8]
- **Post-run rehydration:** 20 oz (2.5 cups) water per lb lost; recovery: 150% of fluid lost within 5 hr. [Ch. 8, 9]
- **Post-run refuel:** 1.2 g carb/kg ASAP + 50–100 g carb (immediate). [Ch. 8, 9]
- **HR by every 1% dehydration:** HR ↑ up to 7 bpm; pace slows ~2%. [Ch. 8]
- **Heat pace adjustment:** +5 sec/mi @60°F, +10 @70°F, +15 @80°F. [Ch. 11]
- **Detraining goal adjustment:** 2 wk off → +3–5%; ~3 wk → +7–10%. Example 3:30 (210 min) × 0.03–0.05 = 6.3–10.5 min → 3:36–3:41. [Ch. 5]
- **HRmax (referenced, not endorsed for individuals):** 220 − age. [Ch. 3]
- **Sweat rate:** (fluid lost + fluid consumed) ÷ training hours = oz/hr. [Appendix C]
- **Taper volume cut:** ~55% reduction in final 7 days; last SOS 10 days out. [Ch. 3]
