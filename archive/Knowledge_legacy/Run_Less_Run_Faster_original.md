## 2. Philosophy & Core Principles

- **P2-01 — Training with Purpose (central philosophy):** Every run must have a specific, defined goal targeting a determinant of running performance. Belief that most runners do not train with purpose and cannot explain the objective of each run.
  - tags: type:philosophy sport:running subtopic:training-with-purpose level:general-population
  - [SRC: RLRF | 3rd Ed., 2021 | Ch.1, Ch.5, Ch.6]
  - [EVIDENCE: author-opinion + study] (supported by 3 FIRST studies, see Section 4/Evidence)

- **P2-02 — Quality over quantity / intensity over frequency & volume:** FIRST favors quality over quantity, intensity over frequency, fast running over accumulation of miles. "If you want to race faster, you need to train faster." Prescribes a *smaller* percentage of total training as running than other programs.
  - tags: type:philosophy sport:running system:threshold subtopic:intensity-vs-volume level:general-population
  - [SRC: RLRF | 3rd Ed., 2021 | Ch.1, Ch.2, Ch.6]

- **P2-03 — Three quality runs + two cross-training workouts (3Plus2):** The foundation of the program is three quality runs/week (track repeats, tempo, long run) plus a minimum of two cross-training workouts. Running less but NOT training less — total training volume comparable to other programs.
  - tags: type:philosophy sport:running subtopic:3plus2 level:general-population
  - [SRC: RLRF | 3rd Ed., 2021 | Ch.1, Ch.5, Ch.7]

- **P2-04 — Specific paces/distances individualized to current fitness:** Prescribe specific pace and distance for every workout based on the runner's current 5K fitness, giving structure, accountability, and a clear performance measure per run.
  - tags: type:philosophy sport:running metric:pace subtopic:individualization level:general-population
  - [SRC: RLRF | 3rd Ed., 2021 | Ch.1, Ch.6]

- **P2-05 — Recovery is where adaptation occurs:** Build in more recovery between hard runs than typical programs; Key Runs must not be run on consecutive days. Fitness adaptation happens during recovery, not the workout itself.
  - tags: type:philosophy recovery sport:running subtopic:recovery level:general-population
  - [SRC: RLRF | 3rd Ed., 2021 | Ch.1, Ch.8]

- **P2-06 — Faster long runs than other programs:** A distinctive FIRST feature — long runs at a designated demanding pace (faster than most programs recommend) to prepare runners physiologically and mentally for racing, especially the marathon.
  - tags: type:philosophy sport:running phase:build metric:pace subtopic:long-run level:general-population
  - [SRC: RLRF | 3rd Ed., 2021 | Ch.1, Ch.6]
  - [EVIDENCE: study] (FIRST studies) / [EVIDENCE: author-opinion]

- **P2-07 — Total fitness, not just faster running:** Goal is a fitter, healthier, injury-free, lifelong runner — not merely a faster one. Cross-training, strength training, and flexibility are essential components. Scott Murr: "a fit person who runs, rather than a runner who is fit."
  - tags: type:philosophy sport:running subtopic:total-fitness level:general-population
  - [SRC: RLRF | 3rd Ed., 2021 | Ch.1, Ch.5, Ch.7, Ch.16]

- **P2-08 — Not claimed to be optimal or universal:** No single training program suits everyone; individuals respond differently. FIRST never claims to be the optimal way to train — only an effective program that works for many.
  - tags: type:philosophy sport:running subtopic:individual-differences level:general-population
  - [SRC: RLRF | 3rd Ed., 2021 | Ch.1]

- **P2-09 — Realistic goal setting is as valuable as the training plan:** Setting unrealistic (usually arbitrary/round-number, or Boston-qualifying) goals leads to too-fast early pacing and disappointing finishes even after excellent efforts.
  - tags: type:philosophy sport:running subtopic:goal-setting level:general-population
  - [SRC: RLRF | 3rd Ed., 2021 | Ch.3, Ch.17]

## 3. Underlying Mechanisms (physiological model)

Three primary determinants of running performance targeted by the three Key Runs:

- **M3-01 — Maximal Oxygen Consumption (VO2 max):** Ability to produce energy aerobically ("size of the engine"). Higher VO2 max → potential to run faster. Typical values 40–80 ml/kg/min. Can increase up to ~20% via combined endurance + interval training. Limited more by cardiovascular factors (e.g., maximal cardiac output).
  - tags: type:physiology system:vo2max metric:power subtopic:vo2max level:general-population
  - [SRC: RLRF | 3rd Ed., 2021 | Ch.6]
  - [EVIDENCE: study] "Research has shown VO2 max to increase as much as 20%"

- **M3-02 — Lactate Threshold (LT):** Measure of metabolic fitness; intensity sustainable for extended periods (usually ≥30 min). Untrained reach LT ~50–60% of max HR; well-trained ~80–95% of max HR. Most people (except highly trained) are limited by metabolic fitness rather than cardiovascular fitness.
  - tags: type:physiology system:lactate system:threshold subtopic:lactate-threshold level:general-population
  - [SRC: RLRF | 3rd Ed., 2021 | Ch.6]

- **M3-03 — Running Economy:** Oxygen consumed relative to body weight and speed. Unnecessary body motion increases O2 cost / decreases economy. Improves slowest of the three determinants — takes longest training to show measurable gains.
  - tags: type:physiology subtopic:running-economy metric:pace level:general-population
  - [SRC: RLRF | 3rd Ed., 2021 | Ch.6]

- **M3-04 — Lactate metabolism adaptation:** Faster running increases the muscles' ability to metabolize/use lactate as an energy source rather than accumulating it (which inhibits aerobic energy for muscular action).
  - tags: type:physiology system:lactate subtopic:lactate-clearance level:general-population
  - [SRC: RLRF | 3rd Ed., 2021 | Ch.1]

- **M3-05 — Determinants of running speed:** Speed = stride length × stride frequency. Stride length increased by more push-off power (stronger hips/lower extremities), NOT by overstriding (landing foot ahead of knee). Stride frequency improved by limiting ground contact time.
  - tags: type:physiology subtopic:stride-mechanics metric:cadence level:general-population
  - [SRC: RLRF | 3rd Ed., 2021 | Ch.6, Ch.14]

- **M3-06 — Crossover concept (fuel utilization by intensity):** ~30% aerobic capacity → fuel mainly stored fat; 40–60% → fats and carbs used evenly; 75% → mainly carbohydrate; 85% → muscles rely exclusively on carbohydrate. FIRST training is almost always ≥70% intensity, requiring readily available muscle glycogen.
  - tags: type:physiology system:fatoxidation subtopic:crossover-concept nutrition level:general-population
  - [SRC: RLRF | 3rd Ed., 2021 | Ch.12]

- **M3-07 — Glycogen depletion / "hitting the wall":** Body has limited glycogen storage (skeletal muscle + liver). Depletion during long/intense exercise forces a shift back to less-efficient fat burning → pace decreases ("the wall"). Training raises the pace at which fat burning predominates, delaying depletion.
  - tags: type:physiology system:fatoxidation subtopic:glycogen-depletion nutrition level:general-population
  - [SRC: RLRF | 3rd Ed., 2021 | Ch.12]

- **M3-08 — Cardiovascular drift:** During prolonged steady-pace exercise, plasma volume decreases (sweating) → reduced venous return → reduced stroke volume → HR rises to compensate. Holding constant HR on a long run therefore causes progressive pace slowing; reason FIRST prefers pace over HR.
  - tags: type:physiology metric:hr subtopic:cardiovascular-drift level:general-population
  - [SRC: RLRF | 3rd Ed., 2021 | Ch.6]

- **M3-09 — Overload → fatigue → recovery → adaptation cycle:** Adaptations occur at the cellular level and only during recovery; there is a finite capacity to endure and adapt. Exceeding the tolerance threshold → staleness/exhaustion/overtraining and impaired performance.
  - tags: type:physiology subtopic:overload-adaptation recovery level:general-population
  - [SRC: RLRF | 3rd Ed., 2021 | Ch.2, Ch.8]

- **M3-10 — Muscle-fiber-specific fatigue vs. cross-training recovery:** Running the same muscle fibers day after day → accumulated fatigue, not adaptation. Using different fibers/activity permits recovery and glycogen recharge while still gaining cardiorespiratory benefit.
  - tags: type:physiology recovery subtopic:cross-training-rationale level:general-population
  - [SRC: RLRF | 3rd Ed., 2021 | Ch.1]

- **M3-11 — Fat oxidation via cross-training:** Cross-training at prescribed intensity increases blood flow around muscles → increases muscle's ability to use oxygen and fat as fuel, sparing limited glycogen.
  - tags: type:physiology system:fatoxidation subtopic:cross-training-rationale level:general-population
  - [SRC: RLRF | 3rd Ed., 2021 | Ch.1]

- **M3-12 — Altitude physiology:** O2 is a constant 20.9% (20.95%) of air at all elevations; lower atmospheric pressure at altitude reduces the *partial pressure* of O2, reducing O2 availability. Endurance is hampered beginning ~3,000 ft; effect greater over longer distances. Living/sleeping >7,000 ft stimulates red blood cell volume / hemoglobin increases and VO2 max, but does not automatically improve performance because training pace at altitude is slower.
  - tags: type:physiology subtopic:altitude system:aerobic level:general-population
  - [SRC: RLRF | 3rd Ed., 2021 | Ch.10]
  - [EVIDENCE: study] Levine & Stray-Gundersen 1997 (see Section 6)

- **M3-13 — Sarcopenia / muscle loss with aging:** Adults lose ~0.5% muscle mass per year after age 25 [SRC Ch.14], loss accelerating after age 60; loss begins by age 30 [SRC Ch.13]. Type II (fast-twitch) fibers reduce in number/size sooner than slow-twitch. Muscle mass drives metabolism, injury absorption, and VO2 max (fat-free mass is a major VO2 max determinant).
  - tags: type:physiology subtopic:sarcopenia level:masters
  - [SRC: RLRF | 3rd Ed., 2021 | Ch.13, Ch.14]
  - [EVIDENCE: study] Harvard Health 2016; Brunner et al. 2007 (Type II fibers)
  - [Note: "loss begins by age 30" (Ch.13) vs. "lose ~0.5%/yr after age 25" (Ch.14) — both recorded as printed.]

- **M3-14 — Heat/humidity physiology:** Evaporation of sweat cools the runner; high humidity slows evaporation → little/no cooling → more circulatory stress moving heat to skin → HR rises → sweat rate rises → blood volume falls → more circulatory stress. Wet skin does not sweat/cool as well as dry skin ("Pour it in, don't pour it on").
  - tags: type:physiology subtopic:thermoregulation caution level:general-population
  - [SRC: RLRF | 3rd Ed., 2021 | Ch.9]

## 4. Glossary — Author's Definitions

- **G-01 — 3Plus2 Training Program:** Three quality runs per week (Key Runs) PLUS at least two cross-training workouts per week; functional strength training and stretching are also essential. [SRC: Ch.1]
- **G-02 — Key Run #1 (Track Repeats / Interval Training):** Repeated intervals of 400–2000 m of fast running (≈5K race pace or slightly faster) with brief recoveries; targets VO2 max, running economy, and speed. Most sessions total ~5000 m of fast running. [SRC: Ch.6]
- **G-03 — Key Run #2 (Tempo Run):** Comfortably-hard sustained run to raise lactate threshold; tempo portion typically 3–5 mi (5–8 km) at ~10K pace or slightly slower (marathon training: 8–10 mi / 13–16 km at planned marathon pace). [SRC: Ch.6]
- **G-04 — Key Run #3 (Long Run):** Steady run 6–20 mi (10–32 km) at a demanding, prescribed pace to raise aerobic metabolism / endurance. [SRC: Ch.6]
- **G-05 — ST (Short Tempo):** ~15 sec slower than per-mile 5K race pace (9 sec slower per km). [SRC: Ch.6]
- **G-06 — MT (Mid Tempo):** Intermediate tempo pace (see Table 6.7). [SRC: Ch.6]
- **G-07 — LT (Long Tempo):** Longest-duration tempo pace (see Table 6.7). Note: "LT" as a pace label is distinct from LT = Lactate Threshold physiologically. [SRC: Ch.6] [Flag: label collision — "LT" means "Long Tempo" in pace tables but "Lactate Threshold" in the physiology text.]
- **G-08 — MP (Marathon Pace):** Planned/goal marathon pace. [SRC: Ch.6]
- **G-09 — HMP (Half-Marathon Pace):** Planned/goal half-marathon pace. [SRC: Ch.6]
- **G-10 — RI (Recovery/Rest Interval):** A timed recovery interval or a distance you walk/jog between repeats. [SRC: Ch.6]
- **G-11 — "+" notation (e.g., MP + 30):** Seconds per mile (or per km) *slower* than the reference pace. Example: "15 miles at MP + 30" = run 15 mi 30 sec/mile slower than planned marathon pace. [SRC: Ch.6]
- **G-12 — Strides:** Gradual accelerations over 80 m to ~90% full speed with a 20 m deceleration (100 m total). Used in warm-up. [SRC: Ch.6]
- **G-13 — Cardiovascular drift:** Decreased stroke volume + compensatory HR increase during prolonged constant-intensity exercise, due to decreased plasma volume from sweating. [SRC: Ch.6]
- **G-14 — Crossover concept:** The intensity-dependent shift in the relative contribution of fat vs. carbohydrate to energy. [SRC: Ch.12]
- **G-15 — "Prehab" (vs. rehab):** Performing stretches and strength training proactively to prevent injury, rather than waiting until rehabilitation after injury. [SRC: Ch.11, Ch.15]
- **G-16 — Functional strength training:** Resistance exercises specific to the movements of your sport (running). [SRC: Ch.14]
- **G-17 — Self-limiting condition:** An injury (e.g., Runner's Knee) where training may continue as tolerated but must be reduced to allow tissue recovery. [SRC: Ch.11]
- **G-18 — Streakers:** Runners who never miss a day (or run a marathon every week); typically prioritize the streak over performance potential and are vulnerable to injury/illness. [SRC: Ch.8]
- **G-19 — Tendinopathy:** Preferred clinical term over "tendinitis" because pain often develops in a tendon without actual inflammation. [SRC: Ch.11]
- **G-20 — Hyponatremia:** Dangerous dilution of the body's sodium stores from drinking too much fluid during prolonged exercise; can be life-threatening. Symptoms may resemble dehydration. [SRC: Ch.12]
- **G-21 — Cardiac drift / "hitting the wall":** ("Hitting the wall") glycogen depletion effect forcing a slower pace. [SRC: Ch.12]
- **G-22 — BQ:** Boston Qualifying (time). [SRC: Ch.17]
- **G-23 — Dew point:** The temperature at which there is 100% water vapor in the air; used (summed with temperature) by FIRST to assess heat/humidity training hazard. [SRC: Ch.9]

## 5. Quantitative Reference: Zones, Doses & Thresholds

### 5.1 Three Quality Runs — Essentials (TIER B)

| Element | Key Run #1 Track Repeats | Key Run #2 Tempo Run | Key Run #3 Long Run |
|---|---|---|---|
| Purpose | Improve VO2 max, running speed, running economy | Improve endurance by raising lactate threshold | Improve endurance by raising aerobic metabolism |
| Intensity | 5K race pace or slightly faster | Comfortably hard; 15–45 sec slower than 5K race pace | ≈30 sec slower than goal marathon pace |
| Duration of run | 10 min or less (per repeat) | 20–45 min at tempo pace | 60–180 min |
| Frequency | Repeat shorter segments until quality work totals ~5K per session | One tempo run per week | One long run per week |
- [SRC: RLRF | 3rd Ed., 2021 | Ch.6, "Three Quality Runs: The Essentials"]

### 5.2 Pace anchor definitions (TIER B)
- Short Tempo (ST) = 5K per-mile pace + 15 sec/mi (+9 sec/km). [SRC: Ch.6]
- Long run (5K/10K) pace = 5K pace + 45 sec. [SRC: Ch.6]
- Long run (half/marathon) pace = 5K pace + 75 to 90 sec = 15 to 30 sec/mi (9 to 19 sec/km) slower than planned marathon pace. [SRC: Ch.6]
- Key Run #3 general prescription = ≈30 sec/mile slower than goal marathon pace (Essentials table). [SRC: Ch.6]

### 5.3 Base-training prerequisites before starting FIRST (TIER B)
- General programs: ~15 miles (24 km)/week for 3 months prior.
- Marathon program: closer to 25 miles (40 km)/week base.
- Required long-run capability at start:
  - 5K program: 5 mi (8 km)
  - 10K program: 6 mi (10 km)
  - Half-marathon & novice marathon: 8 mi (13 km)
  - Marathon program: 15 mi (24 km)
- [SRC: RLRF | 3rd Ed., 2021 | Ch.6 Q&A]

- Ideal racing temps: 40°F–60°F (5°C–16°C).

### 5.10 Aging performance-decline rates (TIER B)
- Serious training reduces inevitable decrements by 50% or more.
- Late 30s → mid-40s: typically <1%/yr racing decrement for those who sustain training.
- Mid-40s → mid-50s: 0.5%–1%/yr.
- After age 55: accelerates to 1%–2.5%/yr.
- Shorter races (5K–10K) slow sooner than longer races; marathoners may run best times late 30s/early 40s.
- [SRC: RLRF | 3rd Ed., 2021 | Ch.3]

## 6. Testing & Assessment Protocols (TIER B)

### T-01 — Determining current fitness (5K reference time) hierarchy
1. Use a recent 5K race time representative of current fitness → select targets/paces (Tables 6.6–6.8).
2. If no current 5K but a current 10K: use Table 3.1 to find the 5K equivalent of the 10K.
3. If neither: perform the 3 × 1600 m field test (T-02).
- Half/marathon-only racers who do NO speed work should NOT use Table 3.1 to predict 5K (unreliable) — use the field test instead.
- [SRC: RLRF | 3rd Ed., 2021 | Ch.5]

### T-02 — 3 × 1600 m field test for estimating 5K race pace
- Warm-up: ~1–1.5 mi jog or 10–15 min easy jogging.
- Run 3 × 1600 m (4 laps) with 1-minute recovery between (walk, don't jog).
- Run the fastest pace maintainable for all three; goal = minimal variation across the three.
- Compute average 1600 m time; **add 15 sec** → predicted per-mile 5K race pace.
  - Example: avg 7:20 → 7:35/mile predicted 5K pace (≈23:34 5K, per Appendix B).
- Metric conversion: avg 1600 m time × 0.62, + 9 sec → predicted per-km 5K pace.
  - Example: avg 8:00 → 8:00 × 0.62 = 4.96 min (4:57.6), + 9 sec = 5:06.6 ≈ 5:07/km (≈25:35 5K).
- Validity check: if the three repeats vary too much (Example 1: 7:05/7:15/7:40 = 11-sec range) the estimate is invalid → redo. (Example 2: 7:15/7:20/7:25 acceptable.)
- tags: type:test sport:running metric:pace subtopic:5k-field-test level:general-population
- [SRC: RLRF | 3rd Ed., 2021 | Ch.5]

### T-03 — Pre-field-test speed familiarization (for pure distance runners)
- Week 1: 4 × 800 m with 400 m jog recovery, even pace.
- Week 2: 1 × 800 m (400 m jog), 1 × 1600 m (400 m jog), 1 × 800 m — even pace, record times.
- Week 3: perform the 3 × 1600 m test (T-02).
- [SRC: RLRF | 3rd Ed., 2021 | Ch.5]

### T-04 — Track repeat consistency standard
- Goal: keep the full set within a couple seconds' range.
- Bad example set (800s): 3:00, 2:58, 3:04, 3:08, 3:09, 3:02 (11-sec range).
- Good example set: 3:02, 3:01, 3:02, 3:02, 3:03, 3:02 (2-sec range).
- [SRC: RLRF | 3rd Ed., 2021 | Ch.6]

### T-05 — FIRST Running Retreat / Workshop lab assessments (4-day)
- VO2 max test, Lactate Threshold (LT) test, body composition (BodPod or DXA), running gait analysis (video/digital).
- [SRC: RLRF | 3rd Ed., 2021 | Ch.3, Ch.11, Ch.13]

### T-06 — Hydration self-tests
- Pre-run: urine plentiful and clear/pale yellow.
- Sweat-loss test: weigh before and after; losing >1.5% body weight during a run = losing too much fluid.
- Post-exercise replacement: consume 16 oz fluid per pound lost.
- [SRC: RLRF | 3rd Ed., 2021 | Ch.9, Ch.12]

### T-07 — Boston-readiness test (per target time)
- Meet the stated 5K/10K/half-marathon criteria for the target time (half-marathon = best predictor), AND
- Be able to complete all three specified Key Runs for that Boston time within the same week.
- [SRC: RLRF | 3rd Ed., 2021 | Ch.17]

## 7. Methodology & Progression Logic

### 7.1 Five Principles of Training (TIER A)
- **PR-1 Progressive Overload:** Gradually increase training stress (time and/or intensity); adaptations occur at cellular level; allow adequate recovery before next overload. Overload only ONE variable (frequency, duration, or intensity) at a time. [SRC: Ch.2, Ch.8, Ch.11]
- **PR-2 Specificity:** Improvement is specific to mode, intensity, and duration. FIRST prescribes a smaller % of training as running but strong specificity for *intensity* (to run faster, train faster). [SRC: Ch.2]
- **PR-3 Individual Differences:** Runners progress at different rates and need different recovery; focus on your own progress. [SRC: Ch.2]
- **PR-4 Law of Diminishing Returns:** Novices gain rapidly; near optimal performance, small gains require lots of hard training. [SRC: Ch.2]
- **PR-5 Reversibility:** "Use it or lose it." Consistency is key; easier to stay in shape than get in shape. FIRST supports year-round training (low-to-moderate intensity maintains fitness). [SRC: Ch.2]

### 7.2 The 3Plus2 training week structure (TIER B template)
| Day | Session |
|---|---|
| Day 1 | Cross-Train #1 |
| Day 2 | Key Run #1 (Track Repeats) |
| Day 3 | Cross-Train #2 |
| Day 4 | Key Run #2 (Tempo Run) |
| Day 5 | Off (Rest) |
| Day 6 | Key Run #3 (Long Run) |
| Day 7 | Rest or Optional Cross-Training |
- Modifiable order as long as Key Runs are never on consecutive days. [SRC: Ch.5]

### 7.3 Program selection by race distance
- 5K: Key Runs Table 6.1 + cross-training Tables 7.1/7.2.
- 10K: Table 6.2 + Tables 7.1/7.2.
- Half-marathon: Table 6.3 + Tables 7.3/7.4.
- Marathon: Table 6.4 (Novice) or 6.5 + Tables 7.3/7.4.
- Programs are 12-week (5K, 10K) or 16-week (half, marathon).
- [SRC: Ch.5, Ch.6]

### 7.4 Pace adjustment / progression rules (TIER A + B)
- Determine paces from *current fitness* (recent race), NOT goal times — using goal times risks falling apart on Key Run #3 and increases injury risk.
- Adjust to faster paces after: (a) a race producing a new standard, OR (b) completing all three weekly workouts at target with perceived exertion judged easy-to-moderate (less than challenging).
- Stay as close as possible to prescribed paces — neither slower (insufficient stimulus) nor faster (jeopardizes next Key Run; risks overtraining/injury).
- [SRC: Ch.6 Q&A]

### 7.5 Long-run distance cap logic (marathon)
- Longest recommended marathon training run = 20 miles. Runs >20 mi require extra recovery interfering with next week's training and force slower pace; FIRST prioritizes pace/intensity over overdistance. Five 20-mile runs over 15 weeks recommended (but "nothing magical" about five; some have qualified for Boston with a single 20-miler using Novice plan).
- [SRC: Ch.6 Q&A]

### 7.6 Periodization / year-round plan
- Three basic workouts (repeats, tempo, long) usable year-round.
- Recommended annual cycle example: winter 5K, spring 10K, late-summer/early-fall half, fall marathon.
- FIRST does NOT recommend a four-race year of all marathons or marathons + half-marathons.
- Marathon block = 16 weeks + 3–4 weeks recovery ≈ 5 months.
- Between formal 12/16-week programs, still do one interval (KR#1), one tempo (KR#2 = 30–45 min run with 15–35 min comfortably-hard), one long run (10–15 mi for half/marathoners; 6–8 mi for 5K/10K racers), relying more on perceived exertion (leave the watch at home).
- [SRC: Ch.6 Q&A]

### 7.7 Supplemental running / frequency
- Additional easy runs are permitted but must NOT interfere with hitting Key Run targets; most runners in studies chose not to add runs and did equally well; FIRST prefers cross-training over extra running.
- Optional third cross-training workout permitted.
- [SRC: Ch.6 Q&A]

### 7.8 Cross-training volume equivalence formula
- Run-equivalent miles = total cross-training minutes ÷ your normal per-mile (or per-km) run pace.
- Example: 40 min stationary biking at comparable perceived exertion ≈ 5 equivalent miles (for an 8:00/mi runner). [SRC: Ch.6 Q&A]

### 7.9 Taper logic
- Taper begins with reduced training load: ~2 weeks before a marathon; ~1 week before a 5K or 10K.
- Non-running day before a Key Run acts as a "mini-taper."
- Reduce/skip cross-training the week before a race.
- [SRC: Ch.8]

### 7.10 Back-to-back marathon guidance
- Not recommended; increases injury risk and provides inadequate recovery.
- Acceptable ONLY if the first marathon was run at training pace / jogged (not an all-out effort). Then:
  - If 2nd marathon within ~4 weeks: add one more 15-mile long run, then follow the last few weeks of Table 6.5 (two tapers).
  - If ~2 months away: resume marathon plan at week 7 or 6 to the end.
- Does NOT apply after a hard, disappointing effort — take a month to recover, target a race several months out.
- [SRC: Ch.6 Q&A]

## 9. Discrete Protocol Library (TIER B)

### P-01 — Key Run #1: Track Repeats / Interval Training
- Goal: improve VO2 max, running economy, speed.
- Domain: endurance-sport
- Total duration: with warm-up + cool-down typically 5–6 mi / 8–10 km.
- Structure:
  - Warm-up: 10–15 min easy jog → dynamic stretches (5 min) → 2 × 100 m strides (accelerate 80 m to ~90%, decelerate 20 m; 30 sec recovery) → 2 × 100 m strides with drills ("butt kicks" 20 m then accelerate 60 m/decelerate 20 m; then "high knee lifts" 20 m + accelerate 60 m/decelerate 20 m).
  - Main: repeats 400–2000 m at target time (Table 6.6); most sessions ~5000 m of fast running.
  - Cool-down: jog 10–15 min.
- Intensity anchors: 5K race pace or slightly faster; target times from Table 6.6.
- Execution cues: run entire set at target time with minimal deviation (~2 sec range); do NOT race the repeats — preserve recovery for KR#2/#3.
- Example 1: 6 × 800 m (90 sec RI). Example 2: 5 × 1 km (400 m walk/jog RI, ~2–4 min).
- tags: type:protocol sport:running system:vo2max system:neuromuscular metric:pace subtopic:track-repeats level:intermediate
- [SRC: RLRF | 3rd Ed., 2021 | Ch.6] [EVIDENCE: study]

### P-02 — Key Run #2: Tempo Run
- Goal: raise lactate threshold / endurance.
- Domain: endurance-sport
- Structure:
  - Warm-up: dynamic stretches (5 min) + 1–2 mi (1.5–3 km) easy, gradually increasing to near tempo pace.
  - Main: tempo portion 3–5 mi (5–8 km) at ~10K pace or slightly slower; marathon training 8–10 mi (13–16 km) at planned MP.
  - Cool-down: 1 mi / 10 min easy.
- Intensity anchors: ST/MT/LT paces (Table 6.7); comfortably hard, 15–45 sec slower than 5K pace.
- Example: 1 mi WU, 2 mi at ST, 1 mi CD = continuous 4 mi (6.5 km).
- tags: type:protocol sport:running system:threshold system:lactate metric:pace subtopic:tempo-run level:intermediate
- [SRC: RLRF | 3rd Ed., 2021 | Ch.6]

### P-03 — Key Run #3: Long Run
- Goal: raise aerobic metabolism / endurance; prepare for race-day sustained pace.
- Domain: endurance-sport
- Structure:
  - Warm-up: early miles serve as warm-up; prescribed pace not required for first couple miles/km.
  - Main: 6–20 mi (10–32 km); 5K/10K pace + 45 sec (5K/10K long runs); for half/marathon long runs = 5K pace + 75–90 sec, i.e. 15–30 sec/mi (9–19 sec/km) slower than planned MP; general anchor ~30 sec/mile slower than goal MP.
  - Cool-down: ~10 min easy walking + sports/recovery drink + static stretches.
- Execution cues: start slightly slower than prescribed, pick up mid-run, finish strong.
- Example: "15 miles at MP+30" — for a 3:10 target (7:15/mile MP): open 7:55, 7:50, settle 7:45; may do miles ~7:35–40 mid-run. Metric: "24km at MP+19" for 3:10 (4:30/km): open 4:55, 4:45, settle 4:49.
- tags: type:protocol sport:running system:aerobic phase:build metric:pace subtopic:long-run level:intermediate
- [SRC: RLRF | 3rd Ed., 2021 | Ch.6]

### P-07 — Post-Race Recovery Protocol (by Scott Murr)
- Domain: endurance-sport
- **Marathon:** Wk1 no running (light spin/swim); Wk2 light easy running, stop when body says stop; Wk3 some running, no distance/pace focus; Wk4 regular routine, no pace/effort focus.
- **Half / 20K / 10-Miler / 15K:** Wk1 minimal running + relaxed spin/swim; Wk2 some running, no pace/distance focus; Wk3 regular routine, intensity by perceived effort.
- **10K / 8K:** Wk1 relaxed running by perceived effort; Wk2 regular routine by perceived effort; Wk3 regular routine.
- **5K:** Wk1 relaxed running by perceived effort; Wk2 regular routine.
- Also: post-race rest from running 2–3 days (10K) to a week+ (marathon); cross-train to stay active.
- tags: type:protocol recovery subtopic:post-race-recovery level:general-population
- [SRC: RLRF | 3rd Ed., 2021 | Ch.8]

### P-08 — Pre-race fueling protocol (see also Applied Guidance)
- Formula: (hours before race) × (body weight lb) = calories to consume; metric (hours) × (kg × 2.2).
- Typical: 300–500 cal 3 hr before half/marathon + 100–150 cal sports drink 1 hr before.
- <1 hr races (5K/10K): few if any pre-race calories.

## 10. Professional Heuristics & Decision Rules (TIER A)

- **H-01 — Never run Key Runs on consecutive days.** [SRC: Ch.5]
- **H-02 — Overload one variable at a time** (frequency, duration, or intensity); never increase distance and intensity together. [SRC: Ch.2, Ch.11]
- **H-03 — Train at current fitness, not goal pace.** Using goal paces → falls apart on KR#3 + injury risk. [SRC: Ch.6]
- **H-04 — Advance paces only after** a race sets a new standard OR all three weekly Key Runs feel easy-to-moderate. [SRC: Ch.6]
- **H-05 — Bad single workout is normal; don't panic.** Causes: work, sleep, nutrition, cumulative fatigue, weather, unknowns. One bad run won't derail a 12/16-week plan. [SRC: Ch.6]
- **H-06 — Missed sessions:** Missed 1 workout → skip it, stay on schedule (don't cram). Missed 1 week → not a problem, continue. Missed 2+ weeks → reassess target race; if missed in first 4 weeks and fit at start, likely continue; if missed mid/late and inactive/weight-gained, choose a different race. Injury-related misses → Ch.11. [SRC: Ch.6]
- **H-07 — Do NOT use HR to gauge Key Run intensity** — HR varies with body position, core temp, hydration, emotions, time of day, sleep, recovery, nutrition, medications, and cardiovascular drift; use pace. [SRC: Ch.6]
- **H-08 — Injury: stop and get evaluated early** (PT or orthopedist familiar with running injuries). At first hint of irritation, reduce distance & pace; if unresolved, reduce frequency; add conservative treatment. Early intervention shortens recovery. "Taking time off is not an option" usually precedes a worse injury. [SRC: Ch.11]
- **H-09 — "Prehab" not rehab:** do stretching + strength training proactively. [SRC: Ch.11, Ch.15]
- **H-10 — Heat racing modification:** relax pace to compensate (author example: planned 7:13 → ran 7:33/mile, a ~4% slower marathon, at 70°F). Do not believe you can defy environmental conditions. [SRC: Ch.9]
- **H-11 — Heat Key Run modification:** substitute short repeats (400s/800s) for long ones (1200s/1600s) in KR#1; take longer recoveries; hydrate throughout; run KR#2/#3 by perceived effort (moderate-to-hard) if unable to hit pace. [SRC: Ch.9]
- **H-12 — Cold-start rule:** be uncomfortably cold for the first ~10 min of a cold-weather run; if comfortable at the start you'll be too hot once producing heat. [SRC: Ch.9]
- **H-13 — Hill running:** run constant *effort* (not constant pace) up/down — slower than target up, faster down; stay focused on downhills (where runners relax). More time lost uphill than gained downhill; roughly evens out on out-and-back/loop. For Boston, train downhills (long gradual downhills fatigue quads before the uphills). [SRC: Ch.6]
- **H-14 — Treadmill equivalence:** O2/energy cost at a given pace is the same on treadmill vs. road; no elevation adjustment needed IF treadmill is calibrated (belts loosen; verify). Use Appendix A mph↔min/mile table. [SRC: Ch.6]
- **H-15 — Race-as-training-run caution:** in theory fine (run at training pace, add/drop miles), but in practice runners get pulled along and run too fast / don't drop out, undermining the goal race. [SRC: Ch.6]
- **H-16 — Altitude high-responder variability:** individuals are high/low/non-responders; must experiment. Live-high/train-low (HL) is the only scenario shown to improve low-elevation 5K times; benefits last only 2–3 weeks after returning to lower elevation. [SRC: Ch.10]
- **H-17 — Recovery aids:** most commercial recovery products lack strong evidence; sleep and rest from training are the universally supported exceptions. Listen to your body; overcome the urge to do more when the body needs rest. [SRC: Ch.8]
- **H-18 — Weight loss for injury reduction:** excess body weight strains joints/ligaments/muscles; non-weight-bearing cross-training helps lose weight without added impact. [SRC: Ch.4, Ch.11, Ch.13]
- **H-19 — RK gait fix:** increase cadence by up to 5% to reduce kneecap load; land with foot under hips; keep kneecap pointing forward (not collapsing inward). [SRC: Ch.11]
- **H-20 — Goal-setting method (Q&A):** set three goal tiers (acceptable / very good / outstanding) rather than one arbitrary round number. Example for a runner predicted 41:00 10K: (1) 41:15 acceptable, (2) sub-41:00 very good, (3) sub-40:45 outstanding. [SRC: Ch.3]

## 11. Applied Guidance (mixed; numbers TIER B)

### Nutrition & Fueling
- **A-01 — Diet composition:** runner's diet ≈ general healthy diet based on fruits, vegetables, whole grains (complex carbs), plus more fluid and energy. Carbs 60–70% of total calories (high-carb diet supports full glycogen); protein 15–20% (tissue growth/repair, not major energy); minimize simple sugars (avoid glucose roller-coaster). [SRC: Ch.12]
- **A-02 — Pre-workout hydration:** 16 oz sports drink or water 2 hours before (allows clearing/kidney processing). Avoid hyperhydration (no performance benefit; urination need). Thirst is a generally reliable guide. [SRC: Ch.12]
- **A-03 — During exercise fluids:** 8 oz every 30–40 min (thirst-dependent) under normal conditions; use 6–8% carbohydrate beverage for events >60–90 min (sodium/potassium replace sweat losses; sodium stimulates thirst/retention). [SRC: Ch.12]
- **A-04 — During exercise carbs (>90 min):** ACSM recommendation 30–60 g (120–240 cal) carbohydrate per hour. 8 oz of 6% sports drink = 14 g carb; every 20 min = 42 g/hr (168 cal); every 30 min = 28 g/hr (112 cal). Gels + water hourly an option. [SRC: Ch.12]
- **A-05 — Pre-race meal:** low fat/fiber, high carb, moderate protein, familiar foods; author eats 3–4 hr before a marathon (ensure elimination). Example liquid option: 8 oz meal supplement (e.g., Ensure/Boost) = 220 cal / 32 g carb; 16 oz = 440 cal without heavy stomach feeling. Common foods: oatmeal, bananas, bagels, toast, peanut butter, sport bars. "Nothing new on race day." [SRC: Ch.12]
- **A-06 — Post-workout/race refuel:** glycogen resynthesis fastest in first 30 min; within 2 hr eat high-glycemic solid carbs (bagels, bananas, pudding). Recommended: 200–400 cal carbohydrate + 40–50 cal protein with sufficient fluid. [SRC: Ch.12]
- **A-07 — Post-exercise fluid replacement:** 16 oz per pound lost (weigh before/after). [SRC: Ch.9, Ch.12]
- **A-08 — Carbohydrate loading:** unnecessary if habitually high-carb; the natural taper (reduced activity + normal 60–70% carb diet) produces carb-loading. Day before: high carb, favor refined (lower fiber). Slight reduction in total calories during race week. [SRC: Ch.12]
- **A-09 — Even pacing spares glycogen:** most efficient glycogen yield with even pace; fast early start speeds depletion → hitting the wall. [SRC: Ch.12]
- **A-10 — Real-runner fueling note (reader Andy Mellon):** last 20-mile run 2 weeks before, tapered 2 weeks; ate 10 g/kg carbs day before → no wall, even splits (recorded as reader report, not FIRST prescription). [SRC: Ch.12 Real Runner Report] [EVIDENCE: case-study]

### Environment
- **A-11 — Heat/humidity training adjustment (Tables 9.1/9.2):** Add temperature + dew point; apply the % adjustment; convert 5K time via Table 9.2; use adjusted 5K to reselect Key Run targets and to set realistic race goals (via Table 3.1). [SRC: Ch.9]
- **A-12 — Summer training for fall races:** run early morning (low 70s, low radiant heat) even if humid, to preserve neuromuscular/biomechanical training; consider treadmill for faster running; mix outdoor (acclimatization) + indoor (speed). Choose late-fall races if in the deep South. [SRC: Ch.9]
- **A-13 — Cold-weather dress:** layer; base layer wicks moisture (silk/synthetics); optional insulating layer (wool/down/fleece); wind/water-resistant breathable shell; cover head (major heat loss), mittens > gloves, moisture-wicking socks, breathable shoes; check windchill; use sunscreen. Remain dry to stay warm. [SRC: Ch.9]
- **A-14 — Heat acclimatization:** requires *exercising* in heat (sitting in heat insufficient); full acclimatization 10–14 days, initial adaptations within first 5 days. [SRC: Ch.9]
- **A-15 — Altitude race-goal adjustment (Table 10.1):** Low→High: find 5K time, read across to race elevation column → adjusted 5K; convert to race distance via Table 3.1. High→Low: reverse. Example: sea-level 24:00 5K → at 5,280 ft = 24:44; adjusted half = 1:54:40 (vs 1:51:14 at sea level). At 5,000 ft ~3.5% 10K reduction; at 7,500 ft ~6.3% 10K reduction. [SRC: Ch.3, Ch.10]

### Getting started (new runners)
- **A-16 — Medical clearance:** if any existing medical problem or over age 40, get physician clearance before starting. [SRC: Ch.4]
- **A-17 — Shoes:** get properly fitted at a running specialty store; visit several (2–3) to see recommendation trends; consider reflective gear. [SRC: Ch.4]
- **A-18 — Start with a 5K, not a marathon:** build a base gradually; physiological development for running peaks after ~8–10 years of training. [SRC: Ch.4]
- **A-19 — Overweight beginners:** >30 lb overweight → walk (not run) until weight lost; use non-weight-bearing cross-training machines; combine sensible diet + exercise. [SRC: Ch.4]
- **A-20 — Training partner/group:** research shows better adherence with a partner or group. [SRC: Ch.4]

## 12. Recovery, Monitoring & Lifestyle Factors

- **R-01 — Overtraining symptoms to monitor:** mood disturbances, irritability, sleep disturbances, increased susceptibility to colds, appetite changes, struggle to maintain standard training performances. Intervene with increased rest early. [SRC: Ch.8]
- **R-02 — Nontraining stressors add to overload:** elevation, colds/allergies, poor diet, environmental extremes, travel, stressful work/relationships. [SRC: Ch.8]
- **R-03 — Recovery rate factors:** age, fitness level, exercise background/experience, life stressors, health, diet, sleep. [SRC: Ch.8]
- **R-04 — Rest day after each Key Run** for the weight-bearing running muscles; cross-train on other days; non-running day before a Key Run = mini-taper. [SRC: Ch.8]
- **R-05 — Recovery aids the author personally uses/endorses:** cool-down after Key Runs; post-workout static stretching; yoga/Pilates (reported by many); massage; foam roller (relief from muscular tightness). Most commercial recovery products lack strong evidence; sleep and rest are the exceptions. [SRC: Ch.8]
- **R-06 — Aging & recovery:** older runners need more recovery, often a rest day after each run day; reducing frequency/intensity impairs maintenance of performance-determining factors → FIRST + cross-training helps maintain intensity while running only 3×/week. [SRC: Ch.3, Ch.6]
- **R-07 — Flexibility for recovery/economy:** poor flexibility → poor posture/form → reduced running economy (more O2/glycogen at a given pace); restricted ankle ROM transfers stress to calves/knees/hamstrings/hips; tight hamstrings/lower back → shortened stride. [SRC: Ch.15]
- **R-08 — Body composition monitoring:** BMI is a poor athlete measure; FIRST uses low-error methods (BodPod/DXA). Abdominal fat = significant health risk. See Table 13.1 ratings. [SRC: Ch.13]

## 13. Adaptations by Population / Context

- **AD-01 — New/novice runners:** use 12-week beginner programs (Table 4.1 Novice, 4.2 Intermediate) based on *time not speed*; progress slowly; follow even if steps feel easy (anatomical structures need to adapt even when cardiorespiratory system isn't stressed). [SRC: Ch.4]
- **AD-02 — Masters/aging runners:** FIRST especially popular; intensity > frequency ("choose hard over often"); serious training reduces decline by ≥50%; use WMA age-graded factors/calculators for realistic goals; running into old age is a realistic goal with smart, consistent training. [SRC: Ch.3]
- **AD-03 — Age & prediction tables:** aging runners have more endurance than speed; a 55-yr-old with same 5K as a 20-yr-old likely runs a faster half/marathon; older women (55+) tend to slow at an accelerated rate vs men. [SRC: Ch.3]
- **AD-04 — Sex & prediction tables:** women tend to run relatively faster at longer distances, men at shorter (same 5K → woman likely faster marathon; same marathon → man likely faster 5K). [SRC: Ch.3]
- **AD-05 — Elite/national/world-class:** FIRST NOT tested on them; designed for age-group/recreational runners aspiring to improve. Elites willing to bear injury risk of higher frequency/volume won't find the approach appealing. [SRC: Ch.1, Ch.11]
- **AD-06 — Distance-specific emphasis:** more intensity emphasis for 5K/10K (faster repeats/tempo); more endurance emphasis (faster long runs) for half/marathon. [SRC: Ch.6]
- **AD-07 — High-mileage tolerance varies:** smaller/lighter/younger runners tolerate more miles; aging/limited-time/injury-prone runners benefit most from fewer running days. [SRC: Ch.6]
- **AD-08 — Boston aspirants:** 16 age/gender-specific BQ plans; include downhill training; set realistic goals to avoid the common trap of over-ambitious pacing. [SRC: Ch.17]
- **AD-09 — Altitude residents/travelers:** live-high/train-low residents can race faster at sea level; low-elevation residents must adjust goals upward for high-altitude races (Table 10.1). [SRC: Ch.10]

## 14. Common Mistakes, Contraindications & Cautions

- **C-01 — Track repeats not a race:** running repeats too fast sabotages KR#2/#3 recovery. [SRC: Ch.6]
- **C-02 — Training at goal (not current) pace** → likely to fall apart and risk injury. [SRC: Ch.6, Ch.11]
- **C-03 — Unrealistic goals → too-fast early race pace → disappointing finish** even after superb effort/preparation. [SRC: Ch.3, Ch.17]
- **C-04 — Overtraining / "streaking":** running same muscles daily → fatigue not adaptation; streakers compromise immunity and performance potential. [SRC: Ch.1, Ch.8]
- **C-05 — Increasing multiple training variables at once** significantly raises injury risk. [SRC: Ch.11]
- **C-06 — Track repeats when unaccustomed:** be careful — fast running changes form and shifts stress to different muscles. [SRC: Ch.11]
- **C-07 — NSAIDs:** useful for reducing inflammation during recovery, but must NOT be used to mask inflammation to permit training (risk greater injury/complications); treat as medicine, consult physician. [SRC: Ch.11]
- **C-08 — Hyponatremia (drinking too much):** dilutes sodium, potentially life-threatening; slower marathoners (4+ hr) who over-drink at low workload are at risk; symptoms mimic dehydration (giving more fluid worsens it). Use weight-loss guide for replacement. [SRC: Ch.12]
- **C-09 — Heat disorders (categories & response):**
  - Heat cramps — mildest; painful involuntary muscle spasms.
  - Heat exhaustion — cramps + excessive sweating, weak pulse, dizziness, headache.
  - Heat stroke — most severe; high fever, sweating cessation, possible unconsciousness → medical emergency.
  - Prevention: proper fluid intake, attention to symptoms; don't risk health to finish a scheduled run. [SRC: Ch.9]
- **C-10 — Cold: frostbite** below freezing (esp. with wind); hypothermia risk after prolonged exercise/glycogen depletion. [SRC: Ch.9]
- **C-11 — Altitude simulation products** (overnight tents, altitude masks): do NOT truly simulate living/training at altitude; no strong evidence they produce desired physiological changes or faster times. [SRC: Ch.10]
- **C-12 — Eating disorders / weight taboo:** weight is significant but discussed cautiously due to prevalence of eating disorders among runners; both too heavy and too thin undermine health/performance; stress fractures more common among thin runners (inadequate muscle to absorb impact). [SRC: Ch.13, Ch.14]
- **C-13 — Excessive diet soda (esp. dark) / low calcium / osteopenia/osteoporosis / amenorrhea** → increased stress fracture risk (women who miss menses higher risk). [SRC: Ch.11]
- **C-14 — Ellipticals & stair climbers NOT used as FIRST cross-training** (weight-bearing, simulate running); FIRST prefers non-weight-bearing modes to let running muscles recover. Ellipticals viable during injury recovery. [SRC: Ch.7]
- **C-15 — CrossFit / short intense workouts NOT FIRST cross-training:** technique-dependent (injury risk), too short/anaerobic, may not help distance speed; may count as strength if not so intense they harm the next run. [SRC: Ch.7]
- **C-16 — Yoga is NOT aerobic cross-training** (no steady rhythmic cardiorespiratory stimulus); good for flexibility/strength/core but not a substitute. [SRC: Ch.7]
- **C-17 — Weight/resistance training is NOT counted as cross-training** in FIRST (cross-training = aerobic without leg pounding). [SRC: Ch.7]
- **C-18 — Runs >20 mi** typically require extra recovery that interferes with next week and force slower pace. [SRC: Ch.6]
- **C-19 — Back-to-back marathons** generally not recommended (see H/Methodology). [SRC: Ch.6]
- **C-20 — Ballooning Boston field / stricter standards:** meeting the qualifying time does NOT guarantee entry (field cap 30,000; in 2018, 7,248 qualifiers denied; for 2020, needed 1:39 faster than new standard; 3,161 still denied). [SRC: Ch.17]

## 15. Formulas & Calculations (TIER B)

- **F-01 — 5K prediction from 3×1600m (imperial):** predicted 5K per-mile pace = (average 1600 m time) + 15 sec. [SRC: Ch.5]
- **F-02 — 5K prediction from 3×1600m (metric):** predicted 5K per-km pace = (average 1600 m time × 0.62) + 9 sec. [SRC: Ch.5]
- **F-03 — Cross-training run-equivalent volume:** run-equivalent miles/km = (total cross-training minutes) ÷ (your normal per-mile/km run pace). [SRC: Ch.6]
- **F-04 — Pre-race caloric needs (imperial):** calories = (hours before race) × (body weight in pounds). [SRC: Ch.12]
- **F-05 — Pre-race caloric needs (metric):** calories = (hours before race) × (body weight in kg × 2.2). [SRC: Ch.12]
- **F-06 — Heat pace adjustment (marathon):** slow ~1 sec/mile for every degree above 60°F [Ch.3]; ~1–2% increase in metabolic cost per 1.5°F above 60°F [Ch.9]. [Note: two different formulations across chapters — recorded as printed.]
- **F-07 — Heat/humidity index:** temperature + dew point → % adjustment (Table 9.1). [SRC: Ch.9]
- **F-08 — Water-loss injury threshold:** losing >1.5% body weight during a run = losing too much fluid (drink more). [SRC: Ch.9, Ch.12]
- **F-09 — Post-exercise fluid replacement:** 16 oz fluid per pound lost. [SRC: Ch.12]
- **F-10 — Race time → pace per mile:** total seconds = hours×3600 + minutes×60 + seconds; seconds/mile = total sec ÷ distance (miles); convert to min:sec by ÷60. Example: 3:47:23 marathon = 13,643 sec ÷ 26.22 mi = 520.3 sec/mi = 8:40.3/mi. [SRC: Appendix A]
- **F-11 — Race time → pace per km:** seconds/km = total sec ÷ distance (km). Example: 3:47:23 = 13,643 ÷ 42.2 = 323.3 sec/km = 5:23.3/km. [SRC: Appendix A]
- **F-12 — Distance equivalents:** 1 mi = 1.609 km; half-marathon = 13.109 mi = 21.095 km; marathon = 26.219 mi = 42.190 km; 1 km = 0.6214 mi. [SRC: Appendix A]
